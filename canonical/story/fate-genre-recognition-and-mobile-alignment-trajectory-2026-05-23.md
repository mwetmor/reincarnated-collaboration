## 3. The named-mythological-weapons substrate (second layer)

The 89K weapon library is **archetype-substrate**: real-world historical objects across centuries and cultures. The named-mythological substrate is **individual-substrate**: proper-noun-bearing legendary weapons that carry cultural-mythological-emotional weight as specific entities.

### 3.1 The distinction

Archetype-substrate (existing 89K):
- "A c19 European cavalry sabre"
- "An Edo-period naginata"
- "A flintlock military musket"
- Categorical, temporal, cultural-regional
- Real provenance from museums + game-canon sources

Named-mythological substrate (to be imported):
- "Excalibur, the sword that legitimizes the rightful king"
- "Gáe Bolg, the spear that always strikes the heart"
- "Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi, the grass-cutting sword found in the eight-headed serpent"
- "Mjolnir, the hammer that only the worthy can lift"
- Individual, named, mythologically-loaded
- Real provenance from world-mythological corpus (Internet Archive's `MythologicalObjects` collection + Wikipedia named-weapons across language editions + open-access mythological-studies sources)

### 3.2 They compose, they don't compete

Each Form's signature weapon has three substrate-paths:
- **Archetype-only** — "a curved sabre in the style of c19 European light cavalry"
- **Named-mythological-only** — "Hrunting, the sword Beowulf bore to face Grendel's mother"
- **Cross-layer composite** — "a Damascus-pattern sabre crafted in the lineage of [named tradition]"

The spirit guide narrates both layers. Players engage with both. The cultural weight compounds.

## 5. The Rift as cross-cultural merge mechanic

Every successful hero-collector requires a structural device justifying cross-cultural coexistence. Without it, the cast feels arbitrary. With it, the cast becomes the point.

### 5.1 Genre precedents

| Game | Structural cross-cultural justification |
|---|---|
| Fate | Throne of Heroes (metaphysical Olympus containing ALL historical/mythological heroes); Holy Grail War manifests Servants from the Throne |
| Genshin Impact | Teyvat = single world; seven regional nations themed on real-world cultures (European / Chinese / Japanese / Indian-Persian / French / Mesoamerican-African / Russian) |
| Honkai Star Rail | The Astral Express travels across worlds in a sci-fi cosmology; each world has distinct cultural register |
| Fire Emblem Heroes | "Order of Heroes" summons characters from every Fire Emblem game (different worlds, different periods) |
| Reverse: 1999 | "Storm" event causes time-travelers from many cultural-historical periods to converge in 1999 |
| Granblue Fantasy | Skyrealm = floating islands with distinct cultural registers |

### 5.2 The Rift as Reincarnated's device

The Rift was canonically committed 2026-05-11 (`project_earth_meta_layer.md`): *"post-Phase-0 rift events (PVP/PVE) refine the multiplayer-OOS clause."* The Fate-genre framing now names what the Rift IS:

> The Rift is the dimensional/temporal crack that justifies the cross-cultural Reincarnation War. Through it, Earth Self can manifest as Forms from any cultural-mythological tradition. Within it, factions from different traditions encounter each other — compete, cooperate, ally, betray. The Rift is the arena. The Rift is the mechanism. The Rift is Reincarnation War's equivalent of Fate's Throne of Heroes + Holy Grail War combined into a single structural device.

**The structural device required by the genre was already in our canon.** We just hadn't named what it does in genre terms.

## 7. D1 reconsideration — three paths (logged, NOT resolved)

D1 currently locks: Unreal Engine, PC/console-first launch, mobile port at +6 months post-launch. The Fate-genre recognition opens a deliberate reconsideration.

### 7.1 Path 1 — D1 unchanged

PC/console launch first; mobile port at +6mo.

**Pros:** Honors original reasoning (Matt is target audience; mobile compromises hurt PC experience; Unreal mobile marginally weaker than Unity); preserves the 200-220 day timeline floor.

**Cons:** Launches into a genre whose commercial center of gravity is mobile, with mobile not at launch. Surrenders 6 months of audience-capture advantage on the dominant platform. Competitors (or copycats) may capture the American-led-Fate-genre opportunity during the mobile-absent window.

### 7.2 Path 2 — D1 amended to simultaneous mobile + PC + console launch

Mobile + PC + Console at launch with cross-play.

**Pros:** Matches Genshin Impact / Honkai Star Rail / Reverse: 1999 release pattern (the genre's commercially-dominant launch model). Cross-platform play is a genuine differentiator the genre rewards. Variable execution by build (D2) framework already accommodates both casual-mobile-auto and depth-PC-active play patterns natively.

**Cons:** +60-100 effective dev-days for mobile optimization + cross-platform infrastructure + mobile QA. Revised timeline: ~260-320 effective days; ~13-18 calendar months (vs ~10-14 under Path 1). Real schedule cost.

### 7.3 Path 3 — D1 amended to mobile-primary with PC companion

Mobile is primary; PC is companion (FGO model).

**Pros:** Maximum genre alignment with commercial center of gravity.

**Cons:** PC-experience compromises hurt the ARPG-depth audience (which includes Matt as primary target). Probably too aggressive for what Matt actually wants to play and ship.

### 7.4 Recommendation logged (not committed)

Path 2 is the design-steward's leading recommendation, contingent on:
- Empirical validation that the Fate-genre prediction holds at P2/P3 substrate evidence
- Matt's deliberate fresh-eyes consideration (not momentum-driven slip)
- Schedule-cost acceptance (~3-6 additional calendar months)

**Decision deferred per § 9.** Path 1 holds as default until amendment.

## 0. TL;DR

The Phase E-1 frame-revision clustering produces **two substrate-led south_american_indigenous-labelled clusters** (Cluster 87 at 94.4% purity / N=36; Cluster 115 at 49.5% purity / N=95) plus a coherent passenger pool of 39 rows inside Cluster 105 (an east_asian-dominant 491-row cluster). On raw clustering signal this is the strongest showing of the four marginal lineages — superficially comparable to mainline-lineage performance.

BUT the rep audit reveals the coherence is **geographic-tagging-driven, not cultural-tradition-driven**. The 47 + 39 + 34 + 23 dominant cluster rows are overwhelmingly modern Argentine / Brazilian / Chilean / Uruguayan military firearms and armoured vehicles:

- Cluster 87 (94.4% pure) reps: FAMAE SAF, LAHAT, Taurus Millennium series, IMBEL MD97, VBTP-MR Guarani
- Cluster 115 (49.5% pure) reps: Browning M1919, Apache (missile), Tanque Argentino Mediano, Darne machine gun, Gun Carrier Mark I
- Cluster 105 passenger pool: Tumi (×2), Escudo "Descobrimento do Brazil", Taurus Model 82, IMBEL MD, RIM-7 Sea Sparrow, M163 VADS, Mark IX tank, BAPI runway denial bomb

The lineage tag `south_american_indigenous` is capturing **"manufactured in or geographically associated with a South American country"**, not **"weapon-making tradition of indigenous peoples of South America (Quechua, Mapuche, Guarani, Aymara, Tupinambá, Inca-descended traditions)"**. True culturally-indigenous items (Tumi ritual knife) appear as 2 of ~290 lineage-tagged rows — < 1% of the lineage pool.

This is **substrate-tagging-discipline failure**, not a clustering failure. The disposition is:

1. **Cluster 87 and Cluster 115 canonical labels stand** (Phase E-2 named them "S. American Indigenous Contemporary Shotgun Cluster" and "S. American Indigenous Modern Pool (Mixed)") — substrate-descriptive labels are correct for what the substrate actually contains: South-American-manufactured modern firearms. Renaming the clusters would manufacture coherence in the opposite direction (denying what the substrate did vote for).
2. **No "Andean / Amazonian / Mapuche / Quechua" cultural-tradition cluster exists.** The cultural-indigenous representation in the substrate is ~2 items (Tumi) — three orders of magnitude below what clustering needs.
3. **Architectural commitments to a "South American Indigenous cultural-tradition" Spirit Form, faction, or Phase E-3 category are deferred** pending the empirical-evidence criteria in § 4. The Cluster 87/115 clusters are valid substrate-led clusters; they describe modern-LatAm-military-firearms, not Quechua/Mapuche/Guarani warrior-culture, and no design surface should inherit "the substrate supports an indigenous-tradition faction" from this evidence.
4. **Substrate-tagging-discipline cleanup (sub-carry 9.11-E)** is the corrective path for the geographic-vs-cultural conflation. Elrond's queue.

The discipline: **the substrate's cluster-emergence and the substrate's lineage-tag-vocabulary are separable load-bearing properties.** When the cluster-emergence is healthy but the lineage-tag is wrong about what those clusters describe, we name both honestly — the clusters as what they actually contain (modern LatAm firearms) AND the cultural-tradition gap as what the substrate does NOT yet support.

### 1.4 Why this is substrate-tagging-discipline failure, not clustering failure

Phase E-1 acceptance gates:

- ≥ 50 emergent clusters — achieved at 125
- Mean per-lineage purity ≥ 0.70 — achieved at 0.9444

South_am-indigenous lineage performs well on raw structural metrics: 47-row dominant cluster, 94.4%-purity isolate, 20 clusters absorbing the pool. The clustering algorithm did exactly what it was supposed to — it found density-connected regions among rows sharing the `south_american_indigenous` lineage tag.

The failure is at the **upstream tagging discipline**: rows like LAHAT (Israeli missile), Apache (French missile), Darne machine gun (French), Gun Carrier Mark I (British) were tagged `south_american_indigenous`. Rows like FAMAE SAF (Chilean modern firearm) were tagged `south_american_indigenous` on a *geographic-origin* basis. Rows like Tumi (genuine Pre-Columbian indigenous artifact) were tagged on a *cultural-tradition* basis. Both modes coexist in the substrate under the same lineage-tag vocabulary, producing clusters whose label is technically accurate (per the tag) but design-load-bearing-misleading (the cluster doesn't describe what a designer reading "south_american_indigenous cluster" would expect).

This is **sub-carry 9.11-E** territory (cultural-vs-geographic tagging discipline; elrond queue). The clustering output stands; the substrate-tagging cleanup is a separate elrond-queued workstream.

## 2. What this disposition is NOT

This disposition is **not**:

1. **Not a rejection of the Cluster 87 / Cluster 115 canonical labels.** Phase E-2 labelled them substrate-descriptively (S. American Indigenous Contemporary Shotgun Cluster + S. American Indigenous Modern Pool (Mixed)). Those labels are correct for what the substrate contains. The disposition is about what the *cultural lineage* tag is and is not picking up, not about whether the clusters themselves are coherent.
2. **Not a substrate-cleaning prescription.** Sub-carries 9.11-D (tagging-artifact review) and 9.11-E (cultural-vs-geographic discipline) are elrond's queue; this doc cites them but does not prescribe how to fix them.
3. **Not a statement about the design value of South American indigenous weapon-making traditions.** Quechua / Mapuche / Guarani / Aymara / Tupinambá / Inca-descended weapon traditions (atlatl-spear-thrower variants, macana clubs, bolas, Andean composite bows, Mapuche lances, etc.) are rich and would serve a Fate-genre faction architecture beautifully. The recognition is about what the **current 89.8K-row substrate as currently tagged** can support, not about what the eventual game should include.
4. **Not a clustering-methodology criticism.** Lowering mcs would not surface a Pre-Columbian-indigenous cluster — the substrate simply doesn't contain enough culturally-indigenous items at any clustering threshold.
5. **Not architecturally contradicting the n.am.indigenous record.** N.am.indigenous has *no* coherent home + *no* genuine cultural-indigenous representation. South_am has clusters that are "coherent" by tag but mostly modern-military by content + a tiny culturally-indigenous tail (2 Tumi entries). Both are substrate-tagging issues but at different magnitudes.

## 5. The geographic-vs-cultural tagging discipline

The south_am-indigenous case is the most stark of the four marginal lineages in *how much it looks like a success* on clustering metrics while *failing the cultural-tradition test* on rep audit. This makes it the load-bearing example for the cross-cutting pattern documented in `canonical/story/marginal-lineage-tagging-pattern-2026-05-23.md`:

> A coherent cluster with high lineage-purity is necessary but not sufficient for a design-load-bearing cultural-tradition cluster. The cluster's content (top reps; cluster_type; provenance of items) must be audited for whether the lineage-tag captures cultural-tradition-of-origin or merely geographic-origin / naming-allusion / cross-tagged metadata.

Cluster 87 illustrates this: 94.4% south_am-purity AND zero culturally-Pre-Columbian content (modern Chilean / Brazilian / Israeli military firearms + a Brazilian APC named after the Guarani people). High purity does not equal cultural-tradition-coherence.

This shape applies forward to:
- East_asian clusters (need rep audit to distinguish samurai/wuxia/martial-arts tradition from "made in Japan/China" modern manufacture)
- Middle_eastern clusters (need rep audit to distinguish Islamic-tradition / Persian-tradition / Ottoman-tradition from modern Saudi/Iranian/Israeli arms-industry)
- Future Fate-genre faction substrate-evidence gate (faction must satisfy BOTH clustering-coherence AND cultural-tradition rep-audit)

## 0. TL;DR

The Phase E-1 frame-revision clustering (k=3 substrate-voted axes; min_cluster_size=10) produces no coherent cluster for the `north_american_indigenous` cultural lineage. The 29 pool rows (22 in the stratified subsample) scatter across 15 clusters; their nominal "home" — Cluster 69 — contains only 7 n.am.indigenous rows alongside 60 european contemporary assault-rifle rows, making it a european-dominant cluster that the n.am.indigenous rows ride along with by axis-position adjacency, not by genuine substrate coherence.

This is **substrate-honest** at mcs=10. The disposition is:

1. **No canonical n.am.indigenous cluster label is authored in Phase E-2.** Phase E-2's 125-cluster canonical-label artifact lists `north_american_indigenous` under `non_canonical_lineages` and does NOT retro-fit a "n.am.indigenous cluster" onto any european- or fantasy-dominant cluster that happens to absorb passenger rows.
2. **Substrate expansion is the corrective path, NOT clustering-parameter manipulation.** Lowering `min_cluster_size` to force a n.am.indigenous cluster would manufacture coherence the substrate did not produce. The substrate-led discipline (Pattern 4-5-6 retirements) explicitly rejects this move.
3. **Architectural commitments to enrich this lineage are deferred** pending the empirical-evidence criteria in § 4. Alternative 2 (carry 9.10-E in legolas Mode B — targeted rare-lineage substrate crawls) is the dormant path; it fires when (a) D10 substrate-evidence gate questions hinge on rare-lineage coverage OR (b) Fate-genre faction architecture (per `fate-genre-recognition-and-mobile-alignment-trajectory-2026-05-23.md` § 4) names an n.am.indigenous faction whose substrate gap blocks design.

The discipline: **the substrate gets to vote.** When it votes against a lineage being a coherent cluster, we record that honestly. We do not invent the coherence by re-parameterization, and we do not commit architecturally to "n.am.indigenous as a faction" against a substrate that doesn't support that commitment yet.

## 1. The substrate evidence

### 1.1 Pool size and subsample representation

| Layer | Count | Source |
|---|---|---|
| n.am.indigenous rows in `v_category_sample` | 29 | `phase-E-1-completion-summary.md` § Per-Lineage Disposition |
| n.am.indigenous rows in stratified subsample | 22 | Same; stratified floor=20 + proportional add=2 |
| HDBSCAN `min_cluster_size` | 10 | Phase E-1 frame-revision math note |
| Smallest theoretically-coherent n.am.indigenous-pure cluster | impossible at this representation | — |

29 pool rows, after subsample-floor application, gave HDBSCAN 22 rows of n.am.indigenous representation in a 10,000-row subsample. At mcs=10, a lineage-pure cluster would require ≥10 of those 22 rows to occupy a density-connected region of the 3-axis projection space. They did not.

### 1.2 Scatter across clusters

Per `phase-E-1-clusters.md` per-lineage disposition table:

> **north_american_indigenous:** 29 rows in pool / 22 rows in subsample / **15 clusters containing this lineage** / dominant cluster = Cluster 69 (7 rows)

15 clusters absorb 22 subsample rows. The mean n.am.indigenous footprint per cluster is **1.47 rows**. Cluster 69, the "home," holds 7 — but it is a european contemporary military-modern assault-rifle cluster of 77 total rows, in which n.am.indigenous rides along with the 60 European reps + 7 southeast_asian reps.

### 1.3 Cluster 69 is european, not n.am.indigenous

Top-5 hdbscan_native reps for Cluster 69 (from `phase-E-2-cluster-labels.md`):

1. SL8 civilian rifle (european)
2. AK-203 Russian 7.62mm Assault Rifle (european)
3. Hopak-61 Ukrainian 7.62mm Assault Rifle (european)
4. Colt CM901 American 7.62mm Assault Rifle (european — N.B. "American" geographic-origin tag, not cultural-lineage n.am.indigenous)
5. Beretta ARX-160 Italian 5.56 mm Assault Rifle (european)

The "Colt CM901 American" entry — tagged european cultural lineage — illustrates a substrate-curation distinction: **geographic origin (United States) ≠ cultural lineage (n.am.indigenous)**. North American Indigenous as a cultural lineage refers to the indigenous peoples and their weapon-making traditions (tomahawks, bows, war clubs, etc.), not items manufactured in the United States. This distinction matters for both the cluster-labeling discipline (don't conflate geographic-origin with cultural-lineage tagging) and the eventual Fate-genre faction architecture (a "North American Indigenous" faction would be drawn from Lakota, Comanche, Cherokee, Haida etc. mythological/historical traditions — not from "weapons made in America").

### 1.4 Why this is substrate-honest, not a failure

The acceptance-gate framing for Phase E-1 frame-revision was:

- **≥ 50 emergent clusters** — achieved at 125
- **Mean per-lineage purity ≥ 0.70** — achieved at 0.9444 across the pool

The acceptance gates did NOT specify "every lineage must have a coherent cluster." They specified pool-level structural quality. Pool-level purity 0.9444 is correct precisely because rare lineages with no substrate-coherent home get absorbed by their nearest axis-neighbors (n.am.indigenous → european contemporary rifle Cluster 69 by axis-1/2/3 spatial proximity), and that absorption is a small enough share of total pool to not damage the overall purity metric.

This is HOW substrate-led clustering is supposed to behave at small-N rare lineages. The substrate said: "I have so few of these that I cannot density-connect 10 of them into a single region." The clustering algorithm honored that vote. Phase E-2 honors it further by not inventing a cluster label for what the substrate didn't produce.

## 2. What this disposition is NOT

This disposition is **not**:

1. **Not a rejection of n.am.indigenous as a cultural-lineage category.** The lineage taxonomy stands; the 29 rows in the substrate ARE n.am.indigenous-tagged; future substrate expansion can grow this pool to a clustering-coherent size.
2. **Not a statement about the design value of n.am.indigenous weapon-making traditions.** Those traditions are rich and would serve a Fate-genre faction architecture beautifully — Lakota war club + tomahawk + composite bow + Haida ceremonial halberd + Comanche horse-archery toolkit + etc. The recognition is about what the **current 89.8K-row substrate** can support, not about what the eventual game should include.
3. **Not a substrate-acquisition de-prioritization.** Alternative 2 (carry 9.10-E in legolas Mode B, dormant) remains the corrective path. When the architectural decision warrants firing it, we fire it.
4. **Not a clustering-methodology criticism.** Lowering mcs to 5 or 3 could surface a "n.am.indigenous cluster" but would be substrate-disregarding parameter-manipulation. The decision to hold mcs=10 was substrate-voting-binding (Discipline #18 amendment candidate) and applies to rare lineages just as it applies to dimensionality choice.

## 5. The disposition as design-discipline precedent

This is the first recorded application of substrate-led discipline to a **rare-lineage cluster non-existence finding** (vs. its prior applications to dimensionality choice and metadata-bucket honesty). The shape of the discipline is generalizable:

> When the substrate does not produce a coherent cluster for a category we thought it should, the corrective path is **substrate expansion**, not **parameter manipulation**. The intermediate state — recording the non-coherence honestly while keeping the substrate-expansion path dormant — is itself a load-bearing canonical commitment.

This shape applies forward to:
- Rare-weapon-form categories that don't cluster (if any surface in Phase E-1.5)
- Style-register cells that lack substrate (`style-register.md` D10 Path A filter)
- Geometry × element VFX coverage gaps (`geometry-vfx-coverage-assessment.md`)
- Per-faction substrate gaps in eventual Fate-genre architecture

Each follows the same pattern: name the gap, name the corrective path, name the empirical-evidence trigger, defer architectural commitment.

# Reap. Die. Rise. — Story Keystone: The Manufactured Rebellion

*Addendum to the Story & Worldbuilding Expansion. This is the **operative core** of the premise — it supersedes or sharpens §5, §7, §8, §10, and §11 of that doc. Same tags. Read after §13.*

---

## 14. The Manufactured Rebellion — the operative core

[DECISION] **A peaceful realm will never rebel — so the harvest cannot run on peaceful realms. The minor cult manufactures the rebellions.**

The logic is a closed loop:
- The harvest **requires a real war.** The major cult's identity is *righteous battle* (delivering souls through valorous combat — a cull collapses the creed, §7), and the patron's hunger is fed through *battle-taking.* The apparatus cannot run on placid, will-drained worlds — there is no war, no resistance, nothing to harvest in the required mode.
- A contented, peaceful world has no fight in it and will never rise on its own.
- Therefore the **minor cult's standing duty is to go ahead into peaceful realms and stir hearts to genuine defiance** — to *kindle* an uprising that would not otherwise exist — **in the name of the patron's hunger and the major cult's crusade.**

[DECISION] **The rebellion is real; its existence is engineered.** The defenders are not sorrowful conscripts and not a fake cast — they are **genuinely, fiercely rebelling.** Their defiance is real, the war is real, the fighting is in earnest. They were *made* to rise, but the hearts are truly lit. The obscenity is not a staged war over a corpse — it is that **a real enemy was *grown* in living hearts on purpose, so that the uprising could be the righteous pretext for the cull that wears a crusade's face.**

[DECISION] **The loop closes with both sides correct:** the realm knows the army is coming *because it has rebelled*; the army knows the realm has rebelled (the pretext for the crusade). There *is* a rebellion; there *is* a war. The buried truth is only that the rebellion was kindled by the hidden order to justify its own crushing.

[DECISION] **This is the deepest reason the defenders are "always prepared" (§11).** Universal preparedness has one sufficient cause: there is *always* a manufactured uprising waiting, because manufacturing it is the minor cult's permanent work. **No realm is harvested until it has first been made to rebel.**

[DECISION — tone, important] **The worlds are alive, not bleak.** The deadening (§15) has *not* happened yet. These are warm, vital, living, peaceful realms full of people who still feel and still have hearts to stir. The realms should never read as drained corpses — they are *the living, before.* The faint wrongness is not deadness; it is **the inexplicable ferocity and readiness of a peaceful world's uprising** — *why is this serene, living place rising up so fiercely, so ready, as though its heart was already lit?* That unanswered question is the seed; the answer (the minor cult kindled it) comes late, through fragments.

[SUPERSEDES] Any framing of the descent as *conquest of peaceful realms* (§11) or as *re-enactment over already-won/drained ground*. The present-tense truth: **the descent is into living realms that have been *made to rebel*; the defenders genuinely fight a kindled uprising.** ([NOTE] There was a real original conquest in the deep past; the present activity is manufactured rebellion in worlds the harvest's peace has reached — but the realms are still *alive*, not hollowed.)

## 15. The Deadening — the real clock

[DECISION] **The hollow peace the harvest produces is not a stable state. It is the *approach* to a terminal flattening — the deadening — the complete extinction of will, then of life.** "We are nearing the deadening moment." Feeding the god deepens both the pacification *and* its hunger; the peace is the slow part of a dying, not an end-state. The deadening has not arrived — but it is near.

[DECISION] This is the true catastrophe behind the whole apparatus, and **no one inside the cult fully knows it** (see §16). It remains a genuine late revelation for the player.

## 16. The Foundation Stratification — the minor cult is the Second Foundation

[DECISION] **The minor cult is structured exactly like Asimov's Second Foundation: secrets all the way down, each tier sincerely believing the floor above the one it cannot see. There is no mastermind at the center of the cult — the closer to the core, the more you find people clutching fragments. The real knowledge sits *outside* the cult, with the demigod.**

- **Major cult** — believe the merciful-war creed (death is mercy, they wage holy war to deliver souls into the Peace). *Deceived.* The war they righteously crush was *cultivated for them to crush* — their war is real and their cause is a lie, at once.
- **Minor cult, rank** — sincerely believe **manufacturing rebellions is holy service** to the patron and the crusade. They kindle uprisings in *faith*, as sacred work. *One peel short.* (The covert outer operatives — the rare, marked, **unkillable escaping figures** of §10 — are these **rebellion-kindlers / agitators**, caught mid-work rousing a realm, fleeing rather than blowing their cover.)
- **Minor cult, inner core** — **only *suspect* something like the deadening.** They have sensed a wrongness, a drift in the peace they cannot name, and are **grasping at straws — acting on incomplete dread, not knowledge.** Not a competent counter-plan; a frightened few clutching fragments, improvising against a feeling they cannot confirm. [SUPERSEDES any version where the inner core *knows* the deadening or runs a competent resistance.]
- **The demigod** — the only one who *actually knows* (the hijack of the lesser god, §3; the terminal clock, §15). The jailer who built the cage and now races a catastrophe it set in motion (§8).

[DECISION] The player descends through all four tiers as the veils tear (§7) — and finds, at the bottom of the *cult*, not a master but suspicion and dread; the truth is one further step out, with the demigod.

## 17. The Player — selected, not designed

[DECISION] [SUPERSEDES the "Mule as wholly unplanned variable" reading of §5, and replaces any "the demigod *designed/built* the player" framing] **The player is *selected*, not designed. Agency is fully intact.**

- **You arise genuinely**, a real initiate among thousands — not engineered, not planted. You **earn your power** through the loop like anyone else. Nothing about you is authored.
- **At a threshold of power, the demigod *notices* you** — recognizes that you have become *both* (a) **ultimately capable** (able to survive the descent to the god and do the deed) and, rarer, (b) **able to commune *directly* with the deity.**
- **The second trait is the key.** The demigod is the *jailer* but is **barred from the prisoner** — it built the cage and cannot enter it (watched, bound, or the god will not commune with its captor). It needs **someone the god will commune with**, and you, somehow, *are* that. ([SEED] *Why* can you reach the god when the architect cannot? — likely tied to your buried pre-binding identity / erased true name, §3, §9, §12. The god may respond to *who you were.*)
- **The demigod then moves to *use* you** toward its own aims (stop the harvest / cage or kill its own creation before the deadening / serve the jailer's escape), presenting this as guidance, mission, even destiny.
- **As you unravel the story, you decide.** Instrument, betrayer, or the third path (§5: free the god / uphold the cage / sever only yourself). **The demigod's *intent* and your *choice* are separate, and the gap between them is the climax.**

[DECISION] **The relationship is a predator-patron who has been scanning the anonymous ranks, waiting and hoping for one to rise with the rare combination it needs — and the moment you cross the threshold, its attention turns on you.** This preserves the §5 thesis cleanly: the demigod *selects and steers*; the player *decides*; the game refuses to resolve which choice is right. You are a self-made rebel the jailer *tried to ride* — and whether it succeeds is the ending. Your awakening is yours; the demigod only *found* you.

## The closed spine (top to bottom)

1. **Major cult** — believe the merciful-war creed; *deceived*; the war they crush was grown for them.
2. **Minor cult (rank)** — *manufacture rebellions* (a peaceful realm will never rise) in the name of the patron's hunger and the crusade; sincere believers; one peel short.
3. **Minor cult (inner core)** — *suspect* the deadening; grasping at straws, acting on dread, not knowledge.
4. **The demigod** — *knows* (the hijack; the deadening clock); the jailer who can't reach the god or end its own machine, so it *watches the ranks and selects* a risen initiate who is both ultimately capable *and* able to commune with the god, then steers that initiate toward its own aims.
5. **The player** — a genuine, self-made rebel who *earns* power and the rare ability to commune with the god; gets *selected and steered*; and, *unraveling the truth, decides.*

*All of it true in the world from the first second, and none of it explained — leaked in fragments beneath the simple reap-and-reincarnate loop (§0). The opening only plants the unexplained seed: a living, peaceful realm rising in fierce, inexplicable rebellion.*

## 18. The Naming Lexicon — title, motto, cosmology, the dream

*Recorded together so the project's names each sit in their strongest role and never compete. The guiding principle: the **loop is the soul** → it owns the title; the **necromancer setting is the grit** → it lives in the art + the world-name; the **peace-state is the dream/lie** → it lives in the creed and the final judgment. Each name does one job.*

### The title (the loop — the soul)
**Reap. Die. Rise.**
- Names the core loop (the kill → death/reset → reincarnation cycle), in the player's own verbs.
- Echoes the *Live. Die. Repeat.* cadence **without** copying its words — reads as inspired-by, not derivative.
- **Ownable in search** (a three-word verb string beats any saturated single noun like "Reaper" / "Necro-" / "Harvest"). Clash-checked: no game ships under this title (closest is an unrelated roguelike *R.I.S.E.* with tagline "FIGHT. DIE. RESPAWN. REPEAT." — same format, different name; confirms the format works without colliding).
- **Delivery note (important):** this is a *loop-caption* — it is typographic and art-dependent, NOT meant to be recited aloud. It pops when **stamped on the art** and especially when shown over **gameplay-loop footage** (a fast montage of ~10 kill→reincarnate beats). Shown over the cinematic *story* trailer, or said casually in a sentence, it underwhelms — because the loop it names isn't on screen. The full gameplay trailer is where the name earns its reaction. Do not re-open the title search based on feedback gathered without loop footage on screen.
- **Standardized forms:** display = **Reap. Die. Rise.** (periods, for title art / store). URL/handle/repo = **reapdierise** (no spaces/punctuation). Grab the handle/domain early — the three words are individually common.

### The sacred motto (the same words, consecrated)
**Mete. Morere. Resurge.** *(Latin: "Reap. Die. Rise-again.")*
- The crusade's holy three-word liturgy in the dead sacred tongue — a **temple inscription / chant / the death-god's words.** The English title is its "translation," which the player gradually learns.
- Grammar (verified): **Mete** (imperative of *metere*, to reap) · **Morere** (imperative of the deponent *mori*, to die) · **Resurge** (imperative of *resurgere*, to rise *again* — chosen over plain *Surge* because "rise-again" encodes the reincarnation loop precisely).
- Alt middle word if a *soldier's-fall* flavor is wanted instead of plain death: **Occumbe** (*occumbere*, to fall in death) → "Mete. Occumbe. Resurge."
- Use as flavor/inscription only — never as the primary title (Latin sacrifices the legibility/searchability the English title won). Picture it carved above the dark temple doorway in the opening cutscene.

### The setting / cosmology (the necromancer grit)
**The Necroverse** — the death-cosmos of countless worlds the crusade traverses.
- Carries the necromancer ARPG grit as the **world-name**, used throughout lore, copy, and (optionally) a colon-subtitle ("Reap. Die. Rise. — the Necroverse"). The *grit is carried mainly by the art* (the cutscene already screams necromancer-grit visually); the world-name reinforces it in words.
- Internal scaling (optional, for cosmology depth):
  - **The Necropolis** — the dead-god's realm / the hub: the singular fortified city-of-the-dead floating in the void (exactly what the fly-over cutscene shows). The *seed.*
  - **The Necrocosm** — the terminal state: the necropolis having consumed all worlds — i.e., **the deadening completed.** The *stakes* / feared endgame. The crusade is the engine turning the Necropolis into the Necrocosm.
- Caution: "Necro-" is saturated in games (Necropolis, NecroDancer, etc.) — fine as in-world flavor, NOT as the title. Clash-check before using "Necroverse" prominently in branding.

### The aspirational peace-state (the dream — and the lie)
**Pax** — the crusade's named holy goal: the promised universal peace the host believes it delivers ("for Pax," "the promise of Pax," "Pax is mercy").
- This is the game's **"Gaia" (cf. Foundation)** — a *contested utopia.* It means **different things at each knowledge-tier** (§§14–17): paradise to the deceived major cult; something doubted by the inner core; the **completed deadening** to the knowing demigod. The single word holds the central irony — *is Pax salvation or annihilation?* — and the ending (instrument / betrayer / third path) is the player's judgment of exactly that.
- Why "Pax" works *here* (as lore) though it'd be weak as a *title*: it's immediately legible as "peace" to any player, so the irony lands without explanation, and a crusade chanting "Pax!" evokes real holy-war history (cf. "Pax Dei"). It's a Latin common noun — not claimable/ownable, so use it as an **in-world ideal**, never as a title/brand element. (Alt coinages if a less-common or more-overtly-paradisiacal word is wanted: **Quies** [rest/stillness — double-edged], **Lethea** [oblivion-as-peace], **Elysia** [paradise that is also a realm of the dead].)

### One-line summary of the lexicon
**Reap. Die. Rise.** (what you do) · *Mete. Morere. Resurge.* (the holy words) · **the Necroverse** (where) · **Pax** (what it's all *for* — and the slow horror of learning what Pax truly is).

## 19. The First Reaping & the God-Speech System (the birth of the loop)

*The reap/possess act is the verb the whole game is built on, so its first occurrence is the most important beat in the game. This section records the locked DESIGN of that beat and the god-speech system. The PHYSICAL mechanics (what literally happens, per style) and the Godot implementation are OPEN — see §20.*

### 19.1 The first reaping — visceral, irreversible, singular
- **[LOCKED] Drop channeling.** Too soft, too telegraphed, too game-y for the most important beat. The first reaping is **sudden, visceral, irreversible** — Mortal-Kombat-concrete, not an ability bar.
- **The beat:** you possess the lieutenant (the cool, tutorial-taught action) → **your own original body is violently destroyed/consumed** (blood, the corpse explodes/dissolves — witnessed, queasy, *nothing to return to*) → a half-second gap → the character voices the realization: **"holy crap, did I just kill myself?"**
- **[LOCKED] The realization CHASES the action.** The visuals must make the *player* realize it a beat before (or exactly as) the line lands — the line **voices what the player already feels**, never informs them. Act first, comprehend after. That gap is the entire emotional payload.
- **[LOCKED] Power-into-dread whiplash.** A flash of power (I possessed them!) immediately curdled by horror (...I destroyed myself). The own-corpse destruction must read *queasy*, not as a triumphant fatality. Don't let it be purely cool.
- **[LOCKED] In-engine and diegetic**, not a bolted-on cutscene. The *possession input itself* triggers the sequence; control is held just long enough for the realization, then returned **as the new body**. The player *committed* the act — which is why the horror (and guilt) is *theirs*. Authorship is required.
- **[LOCKED] Mechanically legible irreversibility:** the old body is *consumed/erased* (nothing to return to); the portrait/name/body has *changed* permanently; this is the threshold from soldier-mass into the reincarnating commander-caste (§ two-axis hierarchy / §17). The first successful reaping is the **birth of the loop**, paid for with your original life.
- **Thematic seeding:** the player just killed themselves to become someone else, *without fully understanding it* — a microcosm of the whole game. If the demigod-mentor speaks right after ("yes — this is the gift, this is ascension, you are one of us"), the player's horror meets the mentor's *normalization*, and the gap between those framings IS the game. On replay, that reassurance reads as the first Mercer manipulation.
- **[OPEN] Two framing questions:** (a) does the player *know* possession kills the old body before the first time (lean: they knew the *mechanic* but not the *weight* — fairer, still lands), or is it a withheld-information ambush? (b) Is the demigod present at the first reaping (thematic, plants the Mercer seed) or is the player *alone* with the realization (more isolating) — possibly alone for the gut-punch, then the mentor's voice arrives to reframe.

### 19.2 [LOCKED] Lean IN to subsequent reapings — death styles as a god-dialogue
*Reverses the conventional "normalize repetition to avoid tedium." Leaning in is more on-theme and turns the anti-tedium problem into an engagement engine.*
- **Death styles are a god-granted, rotating, expressive system** — the violent ways your old body is destroyed each reap. The god **grants** them (literalizing the mentor's "gift" framing).
- **The stake is AGENCY ONLY** (no boon, no stat — self-balancing, purely thematic): **vary your death styles → the god is appeased → you keep authorship** (you curate which deaths you use). **Repeat the same one → the god, bored, STRIPS your selection, cycles new random styles onto you, and chuckles** — you lose control; the god takes the pen.
- **This is a quiet power-struggle over who authors your deaths** — and the god always wins if you slack (he *can* just take it). Autonomy over your own death is *contingent on entertaining a god whose amusement you can't read.* It is the §14 spine in one mechanic: **who authors you — you, or the god?** (= instrument vs. self.)
- **[LOCKED] The moral progression is shock → curation → complicity** (stronger than shock → numbness). You go from "what have I done?" to *curating your own self-deaths for a god's amusement* — becoming a *connoisseur* of your own deaths. That is a more damning portrait than going numb.

### 19.3 [LOCKED] The god-speech system — present, reacting, UNREADABLE (upgraded Rorschach)
*This REFINES the previously-locked "affectively neutral god" (§ the god as neutral Rorschach). The refinement: the god goes from **affectively blank** to **affectively present but motivationally unreadable** — from a blank wall to a **funhouse mirror that reacts but never resolves.** A future writer/agent must NOT read the old "neutral" rule and treat the chuckle/phrases as a mistake.*

- **The discipline (load-bearing):** the god MAY have presence, preference, reaction, the chuckle, and rare speech — **but his affect must NEVER resolve into a knowable motive.** Never "he laughed, for he delighted in suffering." Show the chuckle; **never gloss it.** The player *projects* meaning; the game *never confirms* it. A *totally* blank god is neutral-by-absence; this god is neutral-by-overload (too much to read, none conclusive) — which is *more* present and *more* unsettling while preserving the load-bearing property: **the player still must decide what it all meant.**
- **Affective target: UNCANNY — neither warm nor menacing.** Disquieting because *unplaceable*, not scary-because-clearly-evil nor fond-because-clearly-affectionate. The chuckle's mildness is the menace (or is it? — you can't even confirm it's menace).
- **Rare phrases at the death moment** ("very rarely"):
  - **"interesting"** is the god's flat **signature** phrase — the baseline players hear most (still rarely), the one that becomes his voice in their heads. Interesting *how*? Scientist? Predator? Bored? Curious? Threat? **Unknowable** — a perfect mirror.
  - **Rarer phrases orbit it:** some **seem meaningful** (reference a name/place/fate — players will theorize), some are **flatly mundane** ("Again." "Hm."). **[LOCKED] There is NO KEY to which is which, and it is never confirmed.** A seemingly-prophetic line may be noise; "interesting" may be the most meaningful thing he says. The *corpus* is signal+noise with no adjudication — any meaning is the player's projection.
  - **[LOCKED] Uniform flat delivery for ALL of them.** Mundane and cryptic lines are spoken in the *same* affectless register — no tonal cue about which matters. Portentous delivery of the "meaningful" ones would *tell* the player which is which and collapse the ambiguity. Flatness makes cryptic lines unnerving (said like a weather report) and mundane lines suspicious (why say it so evenly?).
- **[LOCKED] Protect rarity and non-determinism:**
  - **Rarity is the mechanism** — over-use turns speech into noise (tuned out) and breeds patterns (which *resolve* the god). Most reaps: silent. God-speech is precious; treat it so.
  - **Triggers must be non-deterministic** (genuinely random or too-complex-to-pattern-match). A god you can *trigger on command* is a god you *understand* — a vending machine, not a deity. The player must never be able to *make* him speak.
- **The payoff:** the god becomes the **most discussed, least understood** thing in the game; the *player community* becomes the mirror (clipping lines, theorizing, with no answer ever given). This serves the ending directly — when the player finally judges the god (instrument/betrayer/third-path), they judge an entity they spent the whole game *trying and failing to read*, every "interesting" *they* interpreted — so the judgment is unavoidably *theirs*. He never told them what to think.
- **[OPEN] Two taste calls:** (a) truly keyless (purest Rorschach, most radical — risks "there's no answer" frustration) vs. a few phrases that connect *so subtly they're never confirmable even when noticed* (possibility of meaning always live, never certain — current lean). (b) Phrases **authored** (lean — small pool, every line too delicate to risk tonal drift; engine may *propose*, Judge/human *curates hard*) vs. engine-generated (risks a generated line accidentally *resolving* something).

### 19.4 [OPEN] What is undecided (see §20)
The **physical mechanics** of death/possession (the literal sequence of game events) and **how each style differs mechanically**, plus the **Godot implementation**, are NOT yet decided. §20 frames that question space.

## 20. The Reap Beat — mechanical staging & Godot implementation

*The concrete physical sequence of a reap/possess, and how Godot implements it. Companion to §19 (the meaning/design). Resolves the foundational mechanical fork.*

### 20.1 [LOCKED] Foundational mechanic: control-transfer with a visible soul-travel
- Possession = **control/identity transfers** from the old body to the target. The "self" is the controller; bodies are **vessels.** The old body dies; the target becomes "you" (its body, kit, stats, player-controlled). Fits "did I just kill myself?", the champion-reincarnation escape (§23), and Godot's grain (possession = re-point what the camera follows and what input controls — a node re-assignment, not a teleport).
- **Fused with a *visible* soul-travel** (the "spirit soars"): the transfer is not an instant snap — the soul visibly journeys from old body to target, so the player *feels* the transfer as a physical event (the soul-projection flavor of 1C, riding on top of the 1A control-transfer).

### 20.2 [LOCKED] The beat, step by step
1. **Health spent → standing-weakened-coma (NOT death).** When an eligible target's health is spent, it enters a Mortal-Kombat-style standing, weakened, possessable state — **not** a corpse. This *gates* the reap (only the weakened are reapable, not arbitrary fodder), reads as a clear invitation ("FINISH HIM"), and preserves the body as a vessel. (Answers target-gating: you reap the *weakened*, not anyone.)
2. **Camera shifts to first-person**, seeing the weakened target up close — the tonal gear-change that marks this as a *moment*, intimate and bodily. Sets up the look-back payoff in step 5.
3. **Prompted to possess → select a death style** (if you have any). A **fast** diegetic quick-select (radial, not a menu — must not break momentum) *inside* the beat. This is where the §19.2 agency-stake lives: *you choosing the death* is what the god watches.
4. **The spirit soars** through the air toward the weakened body (the visible soul-travel, §20.1).
5. **Camera stays first-person, now looking BACK at your old body as it is destroyed** in the chosen death style (explode/dissolve/etc.). **This is the showpiece** — you witness your own death *from your new eyes*, the most visceral possible staging of "did I just kill myself?" (No cutaway to a corpse — you *became* the new body and *turned to see* the old one detonate.)
6. **God-speech check** — *rarely*, the god chuckles/speaks (§19.3).
7. **Camera returns to 2.5D via a transient, RESOLVING disorientation.** The camera pulls back toward 2.5D *reversed/turned-around* (the world is momentarily wrong — the identity rupture, felt) and then **sweeps ~180° back to the canonical orientation** as control returns. **[LOCKED] The reversal is a transient camera MOTION that resolves, NOT a persistent reversed-control STATE.** (Rationale: a sustained reversed camera would sabotage the escape's power-fantasy, fight the player's spatial intuition, and risk motion-sickness/accessibility failures — paying a rising practical cost for a diminishing metaphor. The righting-itself *is* thematically apt: you orient to the new body; the righting is the ascension. A perceptual-warp/vertigo alternative — distort-and-resolve with NO literal flip — is also acceptable and has zero control-cost; either delivers the disorientation without the playability penalty.)
8. **Control returns** — you are now the new body, oriented canonically, ready to play (e.g., into the escape, §23).

### 20.3 [DECISION] First time vs. subsequent reaps — build full, trim later
- **Try the FULL beat every time** initially, then fall back to a trimmed version *only if* playtest shows it slows the fast-possession loop (the name promises speed). The dependency runs one way: **full→trim is cheap; trim→full is expensive** — so starting full keeps the rich version reachable and the fallback trivial. Whether the full beat slows the loop is a *playtest* question (feel it in-engine at speed), not a whiteboard one.
- **[LOCKED implementation note that makes "trim later" easy]:** build the beat with the **compressible parts parameterized/skippable** — the FP-hold duration, the soul-soar length, the look-back beat — NOT hard-welded. Then the fallback (e.g., first reaping = full cinematic; subsequent reaps = compressed, possibly staying in 2.5D, death-style VFX still firing) is a **tuning change, not a rewrite.**

### 20.4 Godot suitability & implementation notes
- **Well-suited overall.** Weakened-coma = an enemy **state flag** (a "downed/possessable" state) that surfaces the possess prompt. FP shift + soul-soar + look-back = a **scripted camera sequence** (`AnimationPlayer` or a camera-rig tween) with input briefly held. Transfer = **re-anchor** the `Camera3D`/follow-rig and hand input to the new body's controller; mark the old body a dead body/ragdoll. The 2.5D-righting (step 7) = the return tween rotating back to canonical — **easier** than a persistent reversed-input scheme (which would require remapping input relative to the flipped camera; avoided by resolving).
- **Death style = a `DeathStyleData` Resource** (`extends Resource`) defining the destruction VFX scene(s), particles, decals, audio, and timing. The reap reads the active style and fires its pieces. New god-granted style = new Resource — dovetails with the JSON→Resource pipeline (build-arch §3) and the Godot-agent contract. **A death style is cosmetic-mechanical: it varies presentation only, never the possession outcome** — which is what keeps the god-appeasement variety system (§19.2) an *expressive* choice, not a *power* choice.
- **VFX = the VFX-pipeline doc directly** (corpse-explode/dissolve via GPUParticles3D + layered-noise shader; ground blood/residue via Decals; all readable at the 2.5D angle).
- **[PERF / the "lean in" reconciliation]:** §19.2 says lean *in* to spectacle, but the mobile floor says budget VFX. Reconcile: **spectacle comes from VARIETY + shader craft (distinct, cheap-but-rich styles), NOT particle volume or ragdoll-gibbing.** "Lean in" = more distinct styles each cheap-but-rich, NOT more particles per death. Favor **VFX-driven destruction (particles + dissolve shader + a quick canned death anim) over physics-driven ragdoll-gibbing** on mobile (ragdoll is fiddly + costly; reserve for PC scale-up if at all).
- **[SEAM — Analyst watches here]:** transferring "player-ness" across the swap (input, camera, HUD binding, active death-style selection, conduit state) old-body→new-body without dropping references is deceptively bug-prone. The possession swap is a **seam between two body-entities**; correctness there is critical and is exactly where the always-on Analyst earns its keep.
- **Camera re-anchor must be snappy/legible** — the player must *instantly* understand they're the new body. Tune the transition tightly; ambiguity here kills the "I am now them" clarity.

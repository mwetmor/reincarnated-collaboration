## The question
Are we protected, IP-wise, in publishing player-facing material that uses **derived/transposed build names + game-title/era/version provenance** (Atlas, dev log) and the new primary hook?

**Honest current answer: NOT CONFIRMED.** What exists is a naming *discipline* that reduces exposure, not a legal clearance. No qualified reviewer has assessed it; no documented risk-acceptance exists.

## What separates cleanly (so the decision is scoped)
- **Copyright — low exposure.** Game *mechanics/systems/rules are not copyrightable* (Tetris v. Xio); short names/phrases aren't either. Our mechanical descriptions + derived build names are on solid ground. This half is effectively fine.
- **Trademark — the real residual.** Naming law §7.1 strips the distinctive skill/class *marks* (big reduction). But the public register **retains game titles** (`d2`, `poe1`, `chronicon` = trademarks) + era/version. Player-facing, commercial-adjacent use of another's mark invokes **nominative fair use** — defensible if editorial/comparative and non-endorsing, riskier if it reads as trading on goodwill. Unassessed which side our framing sits on.

## The hook change (coupled input — Matt 2026-07-13)
Primary claim moves **from** "ARPG meets Shadows of Mordor" (a *named, trademarked franchise* as the headline promotional claim — the single most exposed use) **to** "Every build the genre ever made. One arena. Reap. Die. Rise."
- **Retires the worst exposure** (named-franchise headline → genre-generic).
- Implies **community-canon / genre-tribute** framing — which is simultaneously good positioning *and* the strongest nominative-fair-use posture (editorial homage, not product clone), and is **honest** (these are the builds the ARPG community has collectively run across the genre).
- gandalf recommends making **"community canon / genre tribute" an explicit, consistent framing pillar** across hook + store + dev log — a consistent editorial-homage posture is what fair-use protection rewards.

## What Matt owes (the decision)
1. **Risk-acceptance vs counsel:** after the probe returns, either (a) accept the residual trademark risk for player-facing publish as a conscious, documented call, or (b) commission qualified legal review of the public dev-log surface specifically. *(A research probe informs this; it is not a substitute for counsel.)*
2. **Ratify the framing pillar:** confirm the community-canon/genre-tribute editorial posture as the standing public framing.
3. **Fallback lever (if review runs hot):** the two-register split already lets the *public* layer drop game-id to a **genre-era bucket** (retain era/mechanics, drop specific franchise names) without touching the internal register. Decide whether to hold this in reserve or adopt pre-emptively.

## 1. The meaning-of-the-arc (≈30s)

**When to deploy:** if asked "what's the player chasing?" / "what's the endgame?" / "what's the long-term hook?"

> The game rewards the patient. Each week, you're reincarnated as a different generated class — that's the seasonal arc, and it's mortal. But the form alive at season's end ascends to your Court — the persistent collection of who you've been willing to become. The depth of the Court is the measure: how many forms have you embodied, how completely you've lived each, how many seasons you've walked. There's no final form because there's no final you. The Court is what you build.

**~75 words / ~30 seconds at conversational pace.**

---

## 2. The Court (≈30s)

**When to deploy:** if asked "what's the meta-layer?" / "how does seasonal rotation create retention?" / "isn't weekly content fatiguing?"

> The meta-layer isn't a roster of unlocked characters — it's a Court. Every form you've ascended stands at its station. The Court is navigable, not scrolled. Each retainer has a name, a class-role, a presence. The deeper Courts emerge over months of play; you can recognize a serious player's Court the way a Diablo player would recognize their stash. It's accumulated identity, not accumulated power. That's the persistent hook the weekly seasons feed into.

**~80 words / ~30 seconds.**

---

## 3. Engine layer separation (≈30s)

**When to deploy:** if asked "are you building an engine or a game?" / "how does the engine-vs-game split actually work?" / "what specifically are you licensing?"

> It splits cleanly into three layers. The engine substrate — generation pipelines, balance loop, telemetry, simulation — is genre-agnostic and licensable. The cosmology layer — the Wheel, the Earth Self, the Court — is Reincarnated-specific, the project's mythology. The seasonal content is LLM-generated against that cosmology at runtime. A licensing studio gets the engine plus the LLM infrastructure; they bring their own cosmology. The Reincarnated game is one configuration of the engine — the flagship demo, but not the only possible game.

**~80 words / ~30 seconds.**

---

## Bonus — composite for "give me the whole pitch in a minute" (≈60s)

**When to deploy:** if asked for the elevator pitch upfront, before walking the loadout app.

> Reincarnated is an isekai mobile ARPG with a procedural content engine underneath. The engine generates and simulation-balances a full season in 41 minutes for under a dollar — that's the engine pitch. The game pitch is the meta-layer: every week, you're reincarnated as a different generated class, the seasonal arc is mortal, but the form alive at season's end ascends to your Court — the persistent collection of who you've been willing to become. The hook is the Court's depth over many seasons, not any single character's power curve. The engine could ship as middleware to other studios — genre-agnostic substrate, swap the cosmology, run their game. The game could ship as the flagship demo of the engine. Either path stands alone; the integration is the upside.

**~135 words / ~60 seconds.**

## Delivery notes

- **Don't recite.** These are scaffolds, not scripts. If the listener interrupts with a question mid-distillation, follow the question — that's the point of the meeting (per talking-points.md: *"his questions are the point of the meeting"*).
- **The Court line is the highest-leverage one.** It's the most novel claim and the most visualizable. If you only land one cleanly, land this one.
- **"There's no final form because there's no final you"** is the line worth landing verbatim if you can. It's the meaning-of-the-arc's load-bearing beat and it lodges in memory.
- **Don't reach for the engine-layer separation unless the listener invites it.** It's the most technical-sounding of the three; saves for when "engine or game?" comes up naturally rather than volunteering it upfront.
- **The composite is a fallback** if the listener asks for the whole pitch immediately. Prefer the per-question distillations when possible — they feel less rehearsed.

IMAGE 1 is not a flat-colour layout: it is a GREYBOX RENDER of a real 3D game level, seen from the game's fixed high three-quarter ORTHOGRAPHIC camera (53 degrees down), at true scale -- the small painted barbarian among the standing stones is 1.85 m tall. Every grey or coloured form is a real object that will be modelled at exactly this position and size, and the ground has real height: a snowy plateau above, a beach sloping down to the shore ice, and the sea below the cliffs. LIGHT WARM GREY ground = deep, smooth snow, unbroken by any path or track; PALE BLUE-GREY = the frozen mere, its outline organic and uneven, MOSTLY UNBROKEN pale ice with only a few long, branching cracks and a couple of pressure seams, lightly dusted with snow, small snowy hummocks of reeds standing out of it; the THIN DARK RIBBON = a tiny UNFROZEN river of open dark water winding down beside the coast into the mere between low snowy banks; along the edges of the mere and the little river, tall pale straw-gold REEDS and dry sedge standing in tufts through the snow; the BROAD PALE TAN SLOPE = a grey shingle beach under thin snow, falling from the plateau down to the shore ice; the VERY PALE BLUE-WHITE ground = thick shore-fast ice, joined to the land, breaking further out into large irregular ice plates and then into loose floes of every size -- MOST of the sea is ice; DARK SLATE-BLUE = the open winter water, only in the leads and gaps between the plates and floes; MUTED GREEN-BROWN PATCHES = low dark juniper scrub and rust-brown winter heather poking through the snow; the SMALL GREY UPRIGHT SLABS = a small, even circle of six carved standing stones (spiral and knotwork carvings on the faces turned toward the viewer, snow on their tops): four still standing, two fallen -- of the fallen ones only a low block juts out of the snow, drifted round its foot -- with a few lone standing stones further up the slope; the LARGE PALE DOME at the top = the barrow mound, earth and dead grass under thin snow; set into its front, a grey carved stone facade with a monumental lintel and posts around a DARK doorway -- the barrow's one great door; the BROWN WOODEN HULL heeled over at the foot of the beach = an old longship wreck, heeled and half-sunk in the shore ice, its carved dragon-head prow raised, its mast snapped, its broken ribs and timbers strewn across the shingle; the GREY ROCK MASSES along the coast = SEA CLIFFS of weathered dark granite, eroded by the sea: rough rock columns split by vertical cracks, buttresses and recesses, snow lying on every ledge and step, a wave-cut notch at the foot, fallen blocks and boulders heaped below, and lone rock stacks standing out in the ice -- rugged and natural, never a smooth wall; in the foot of the cliff a DARK SEA CAVE worn into the rock by the tides, its arch rounded and dark inside, the sea and floes at its mouth, a dark wet band and a line of rime and hanging icicles marking the high-tide line; beside it, a natural CLEFT between the rock columns where rough STEPS have been cut ever so slightly into the raw cliff, climbing from a small tide-iced ledge at the cave mouth to the clifftop -- uneven stones of different lengths, snow lying in patches on them, the rock columns rising on both sides; the GREY CRAGGY MASSES inland = weathered granite crags and boulders with snow on their ledges; at the right, the large BROWN TIMBER building = a half-burned longhall of weathered grey-brown timber, its walls closed and whole, its one great DARK door the only way in; the BLACK-BROWN tangle beside it = the charred, collapsed gable of a burned building; the FLAT DARKER GREY ground around them = a yard of trampled ash and soot; the rows of thin BROWN POSTS = a broken wooden palisade; the long brown cylinders = fallen logs. The ONLY dark openings in the whole scene are the barrow's great door, the longhall's great door and the sea cave: paint no other doorway, gap or opening anywhere.

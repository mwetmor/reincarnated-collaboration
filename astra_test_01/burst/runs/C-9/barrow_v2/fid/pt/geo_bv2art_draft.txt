IMAGE 1 is not a flat-colour layout: it is a GREYBOX RENDER of a real 3D game level, seen from the game's fixed high three-quarter ORTHOGRAPHIC camera (53 degrees down), at true scale -- the small painted barbarian among the standing stones is 1.85 m tall. Every grey or coloured form is a real object that will be modelled at exactly this position and size. LIGHT WARM GREY ground = deep, smooth snow; the NARROW DARKER BROWN-GREY BAND = a trodden path through the snow, running up through the stones to the barrow door; PALE BLUE-GREY = the frozen mere, flat lapis-blue ice with pale cracks; the BRIGHT BLUE ribbon = a small frozen stream running down off the barrow's flank into the mere; the BROAD PALE TAN BAND along the water = a grey shingle beach under thin snow, with a thin line of drifted snow along it; the VERY PALE BLUE-WHITE ground = thick shore ice, its broken front (the pale striped edge) dropping to the sea; DARK SLATE-BLUE = the open winter sea, and the PALE FLAT DISCS on it = floating ice floes; MUTED GREEN-BROWN PATCHES = low dark juniper scrub and rust-brown winter heather poking through the snow; the SMALL GREY UPRIGHT SLABS around the path = a broken ring of carved standing stones (spiral and knotwork carvings on the faces turned toward the viewer), two of them FALLEN and lying in the snow, with a few lone standing stones further up the slope; the LARGE PALE DOME at the top = the barrow mound, earth and dead grass under thin snow; set into its front, a grey carved stone facade with a monumental lintel and posts around a DARK doorway -- the barrow's one great door; the BROWN WOODEN HULL heeled over at the left = an old longship wreck frozen into the shore ice, its broken ribs and timbers strewn across the shingle; the GREY CRAGGY MASSES = weathered granite crags and boulders with snow on their ledges; along the bottom, the GREY LAYERED WALL = sheer sea cliffs of layered granite under a snowy cliff top, with a DARK opening in the cliff foot = a sea cave, and a straight flight of rock-cut STEPS climbing the cliff face beside it from a flat natural rock shelf above the water; at the right, the large BROWN TIMBER building = a half-burned longhall of weathered grey-brown timber with one great DARK door; the BLACK-BROWN tangle beside it = the charred, collapsed gable of a burned building; the FLAT DARKER GREY ground around them = a yard of trampled ash and soot; the rows of thin BROWN POSTS = a broken wooden palisade; the long brown cylinders = fallen logs.

IMAGE 1 is not a flat-colour layout: it is a GREYBOX RENDER of a real 3D game level, seen from the game's fixed high three-quarter ORTHOGRAPHIC camera (53 degrees down), at true scale -- the small painted barbarian among the standing stones is 1.85 m tall. Every grey or coloured form is a real object that will be modelled at exactly this position and size. LIGHT WARM GREY ground = deep, smooth snow, warm peach-cream where lit, with distinct lavender / blue-violet shadow shapes and rounded cobble cells; LIGHT BLUE-GREY = the frozen mere, mid-blue lapis ice, mostly unbroken, with a few soft pale veins, low snowy islands in it; the mere's ice runs SEAMLESSLY into the sea ice with no line or edge between them: the WHITE-BLUE ground = thick shore-fast ice, joined to the land, breaking up GRADUALLY toward the sea into large irregular ice plates and then into loose floes of every size, each with worn edges -- MOST of the sea is ice; DARK SLATE-BLUE = the open winter water, only in the leads and gaps between the plates and floes, narrow by the mere and widening toward the sea, and the narrow dark sliver at the cliff foot beside the shingle flank is a natural irregular lead; MUTED OLIVE-BROWN PATCHES = reddish-brown winter HEATHER, painted as many separate rounded tufts and sprigs clustered together with snow showing between them, as in the concept -- never a flat carpet or a solid mat, the large patches on the cliff tops included: clumps of tufts with snow between; STRAW patches = tall straw-gold reed beds; the LIGHT TAN SLOPE = a grey shingle beach under rounded snow drifts; SMALL GREY UPRIGHT SLABS = six carved standing stones (spiral and knotwork carvings on the faces turned toward the viewer), two fallen; the LARGE DOME = the barrow mound, earth and dead grass under thin snow, its carved stone door in front; the BROWN HULL = an old longship wreck heeled over in the shore ice; the GREY ROCK MASSES along the coast = SEA CLIFFS of weathered dark granite, eroded by the sea: rough rock columns split by vertical cracks, buttresses and recesses, snow lying on every ledge and step, a wave-cut notch at the foot, fallen blocks and boulders heaped below, and lone rock stacks standing out in the ice -- rugged and natural, never a smooth wall; in the foot of the cliff a DARK SEA CAVE worn into the rock by the tides, its arch rounded and dark inside, the sea and floes at its mouth, a dark wet band and a line of rime and hanging icicles marking the high-tide line; beside it, a natural CLEFT between the rock columns where rough STEPS have been cut ever so slightly into the raw cliff, climbing from a small tide-iced ledge at the cave mouth to the clifftop -- uneven stones of different lengths, snow lying in patches on them, the rock columns rising on both sides; the GREY CRAGGY MASSES inland = weathered granite crags and boulders with snow on their ledges; at the right, the large BROWN TIMBER building = a half-burned longhall of weathered grey-brown timber, its walls closed and whole, its one great DARK door the only way in; the BLACK-BROWN tangle beside it = the charred, collapsed gable of a burned building; the FLAT DARKER GREY ground around them = a yard of trampled ash and soot; the rows of thin BROWN POSTS = a broken wooden palisade; the long brown cylinders = fallen logs. The ONLY dark openings in the whole scene are the barrow's great door, the longhall's great door and the sea cave: paint no other doorway, gap or opening anywhere.

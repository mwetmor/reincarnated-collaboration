IMAGE 1 is not a flat-colour layout: it is a GREYBOX RENDER of a real 3D game level, seen from the game's fixed high three-quarter ORTHOGRAPHIC camera (53 degrees down), at true scale -- the small painted barbarian among the standing stones is 1.85 m tall. Every grey or coloured form is a real object that will be modelled at exactly this position and size. LIGHT WARM GREY ground = deep, smooth snow, warm peach-cream where lit, with distinct lavender / blue-violet shadow shapes and rounded cobble cells; LIGHT BLUE-GREY = the frozen mere, mid-blue lapis ice, mostly unbroken, with a few soft pale veins, low snowy islands in it; WHITE-BLUE ground = shore-fast ice breaking into plates and floes, DARK SLATE-BLUE = open water between them, and the narrow dark sliver at the cliff foot beside the shingle flank is a natural irregular lead; MUTED OLIVE-BROWN PATCHES = reddish-brown winter heather in clumps of rounded tufts with snow between; STRAW patches = tall straw-gold reed beds; the LIGHT TAN SLOPE = a grey shingle beach under rounded snow drifts; SMALL GREY UPRIGHT SLABS = six carved standing stones (spiral and knotwork carvings on the faces turned toward the viewer), two fallen; the LARGE DOME = the barrow mound, earth and dead grass under thin snow, its carved stone door in front; the BROWN HULL = an old longship wreck heeled over in the shore ice; the GREY ROCK MASSES on the coast = weathered granite sea cliffs, snow on every ledge, a dark sea cave and rough steps in a cleft; the BROWN TIMBER building = a half-burned longhall, a charred gable and trampled ash beside it; thin brown posts = a broken palisade. The ONLY dark openings in the whole scene are the barrow's great door, the longhall's great door and the sea cave: paint no other doorway, gap or opening anywhere.

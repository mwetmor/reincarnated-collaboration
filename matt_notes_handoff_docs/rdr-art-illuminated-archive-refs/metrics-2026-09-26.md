## Reading

- **What makes the FFT page look "dull but rich" is its light end.** Its 95th-percentile lightness is L\* 94 against Astra's 75–81. There are large pale-cream, low-chroma areas that read as bare paper, with colour concentrated in accents (15 % of pixels above C\* 40). **Astra's images are dark and dense**, not too bright. "Softer brightness" pushed them the wrong way: the highlights should rise and the colour should spread less.
- **Its darks are real but not black.** The line averages L\* ≈ 8 and is warm (sepia or brown); Astra's darkest pixels sit at L\* 3. The painted-book pages lift their darks further, to L\* 25–33.
- **Both sources share one property that Astra lacks:** calm, pale large areas, with saturation spent on small accents. The current cliffside register is the outlier (55 % of pixels above C\* 40).

## Working target band for the merged anchor

Proposed values, to be checked against the anchors Matt approves:

- L\* 5th percentile ≈ 15–25: FFT darks, lifted toward the book, with no near-black
- L\* 95th percentile ≈ 88–94: pale cream and bare-vellum highlights
- % pixels with C\* > 40 ≈ 12–20: saturation on accents only (the gold leaf, banners, a tabard)
- Darkest 2 % at L\* ≥ 8 with a warm hue (≈ 20–60): a sepia or iron-gall line, not black

These can become O1 palette-oracle checks, and they give the three-way imperfection test a colour baseline.

**Caveat:** the FFT page is a web-sourced JPEG compilation, so its colour may have been altered in scanning or compression. Treat its numbers as direction, not calibration.

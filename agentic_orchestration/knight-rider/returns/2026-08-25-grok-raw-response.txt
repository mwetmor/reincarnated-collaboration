## (1) CV refusal

Jitter-to-pass is real. Poisson-vs-rhythm is real. CV cannot tell them apart. Do not bar `CV ≥ 0.8`.

He then says that settles cadence as a property. It doesn’t. **CV is a timescale-blind scalar.** The thing that distinguishes rhythm from noise is the **shape across timescales**, and he had the ingredients:

- **Fano factor \(F(T)\)** (count variance/mean in windows of length \(T\)): periodic \(\rightarrow F\to 0\) near the period; Poisson \(\rightarrow F\approx 1\) at all \(T\); burst/rhythm \(\rightarrow F<1\) inside the strike, \(F>1\) at phrase scale. Jittered metronome cannot fake that curve.
- **Event-train spectrum**, which he already computed. A jittered clock stays **tonal** (peak broadens, remains a peak). Poisson is flat. Phrase rhythm is **broad with related secondary peaks**. He wrote “broad, not random” and “flatten the spectrum is the right instruction,” then **refused to mint the statistic that implements it.**

So the gameable-proof target he missed is not a CV number. It is **\(F(T)\) + peak/median (or spectral entropy) as a two-sided envelope**, plus P-3 with an **incommensurability** rule (not “has a second timer”).

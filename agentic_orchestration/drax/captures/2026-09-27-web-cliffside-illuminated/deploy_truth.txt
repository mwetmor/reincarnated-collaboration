  ⚑ WHY THAT SECOND TEST HAD TO EXIST. boot_test.js proves the page boots and that
  move/cast reach the game. It could not have caught the defect this build was one
  line away from shipping: style_toggle.gd reads T and G through _unhandled_input +
  event.is_action_pressed, and Input.action_press() — which is all the touch overlay
  did — SYNTHESISES NO EVENT. A T button wired the old way lights up, animates on
  press, and does nothing. Every button now fires both Input.action_press (for
  keeper.gd's Input.is_action_just_pressed on vfx_cycle / gear_toggle / run_modifier)
  AND Input.parse_input_event(InputEventAction) (for the _unhandled_input listeners).
  G is one physical key bound to two actions, so its button fires both.

  Godot console carries seven "No Bone2D children of node …" warnings from the 2D rig.
  Pre-existing, present on the desktop build, not caused by the web export; they are
  warnings, not pageerrors, and the rig renders.


6. ON-SCREEN CONTROLS (the TOUCH requirement)
---------------------------------------------
  Left half        floating joystick -> move_*, analog; past 60 % also holds run
  CAST / JUMP      unchanged
  RUN   (new)      held run_modifier — so you can run without deflecting fully, and
                   releasing it does not cancel a stick that is itself past threshold
  VFX              vfx_cycle
  T     (new)      style_toggle   — labelled STYLE
  G     (new)      rig_toggle + gear_toggle — labelled RIG

  BUTTONS is now the union over every route, filtered against the project's own
  InputMap at _ready. The H1-only /playtest/cliffside/ route has no style_toggle or
  rig_toggle action, so if it is ever rebuilt it draws exactly the three buttons it
  always had. The startup line "touch_controls: N buttons" states how many survived.

  HUD: the bottom strip states style, run coverage and rig mode. Its font went 16 -> 26
  and the panel to two lines. 1920x1080 is the design viewport and the web build scales
  that whole canvas to the phone's landscape width (~800 CSS px, ~0.42x), so the default
  16 px label landed at ~7 px on a handset — present, and unreadable. The "→" glyph in
  the HUD string rendered as tofu in the fallback font at the larger size and was
  replaced with ASCII.


7. SIZE — WHAT WAS ASKED, WHAT LANDED, AND THE PART THAT DID NOT
----------------------------------------------------------------
  Target: <= 90 MB HARD, aim <= 60 MB.   Landed: 68,243,988 B = 68.2 MB.
  HARD ceiling met with 22 MB of room; GitHub's 100 MB file limit met with 32 MB.
  THE 60 MB AIM WAS NOT MET, and it cannot be under this dispatch's own fidelity rule.

    desktop pck (reference, lossless)            147    MB
    web export, default overlay rule only         73.76 MB
    web export, this route's policy               68.24 MB

  Where the 68.2 MB is (imported .ctex bytes):

    parallax/layers_b   10.80      parallax/layers    10.11
    parallax/tiles       5.74      parallax/tiles_b    5.27      -> 31.9 MB parallax
    sprites_knight/*    12.35      sprites/* (Keeper) 13.2       -> 25.6 MB character cells
    sprites_figures      2.63      sprites_rig_E       0.8
    props + vfx          5.9       everything else     ~1.4

  Character cells are 40 % of the pck and the brief pins them lossless. Reaching 60 MB
  means either making them lossy (~9 MB, and their alpha edge IS the read at 0.42x
  phone scale) or splitting A and B into two routes — which would undo the one thing
  Matt asked for, a toggle between the two cliffsides in one place. Conductor's call;
  I did not take it unilaterally.

  What the policy does (_overlay/routes/cliffside-illuminated.json, new mechanism):
    lossy WebP q0.88   the 12 big painted files — both ground plates, both sets of
                       parallax layers, both mists, both skies (all >= 2048 px)
    lossy WebP q0.90   props and vfx at >= 256 px, WHERE IT PAYS (see below)
    lossless           sprites/, sprites_knight/, sprites_figures/, sprites_rig_E/ —
                       pinned regardless of size
    Absent policy file = the old 2048 px rule alone, so /playtest/cliffside/ imports
    byte-identically to before this mechanism existed.

  ⚑ LOSSY IS NOT ALWAYS SMALLER, AND ASSUMING IT COST A MEGABYTE BEFORE I MEASURED.
  The first policy pass moved props and vfx to lossy on the reasoning that lossy
  shrinks things. vfx/blackwater_cocktail_e3_V — 146 cells of 485x581 mostly-transparent
  spray — went 0.89 MB -> 1.96 MB: it paid a megabyte to LOSE fidelity, because WebP's
  lossy path is worse than PNG on sparse high-alpha content. The overlay now MEASURES
  below 2048 px: it encodes both ways in memory and keeps PNG unless WebP wins by 25 %.
  231 of 741 candidate textures came back lossless on that test. Net effect of the
  measured pass over the assumed one: 69.67 MB -> 68.24 MB, and the flipbook is intact.

  QUALITY EVIDENCE: quality/ holds four 760x420 1:1 crops, each as
  [lossless | lossy q0.88 | difference x12], with mean and max absolute difference:

    plate_tileB   parallax/tiles_b/tile_0_0.png   mean 2.47  max 15   ground plate, style B
    plate_tileA   parallax/tiles/tile_0_0.png     mean 2.74  max 19   ground plate, style A
    sky_B         parallax/layers_b/sky.png       mean 1.88  max 16   burning sky band
    mist_B        parallax/layers_b/mist.png      mean 0.96  max  7   purple mist

  Read of the crops: on the plates the difference is dithered texture noise with no
  structure — the painted stones, the ink outlines and the grass edge are unmoved. On
  the sky the entire difference sits on the ink outlines of the cloud shapes, at a
  maximum of 16/255, i.e. invisible at 1:1 and far below the 0.42x the phone shows it
  at. The mist barely responds to quality at all (5.4 MB at q0.80 and q0.92 alike):
  it is mostly alpha, and WebP stores the alpha plane losslessly. That is why q0.88 was
  chosen over a harder squeeze — below it the plates start to give and the mist,
  which is the biggest single file, gives nothing back.

## §5 — ⚑ RULING 5: the beams could not be lengthened until they stopped being cones

### 5.1 The requirement is screen-space, so the derivation is screen-space

At the CAM-LOCK rig the frame's TOP EDGE is a plane, and it is the whole of the arithmetic. The
camera stands 28.397 m up and its top-of-frame ray descends at pitch − fov_v/2 = **37.06°**, so a
point at ground distance `D` from the camera is off the top of the frame exactly when

```
y  >  28.3970  −  0.75521 · D_ground(from camera)
```

The camera's own ground point is 20.169 m from the player, so the **in-frame ceiling is 13.17 m
directly over the player** and rises toward the viewer to **20.10 m at the frustum's near edge** —
the **worst case** for any shaft that enters the frame at all.

**BEAM-REAL's 11.0 m top ended inside the frame everywhere.** The probe's beam-end census printed
the failing rows (87 and 619 of 720) before a single constant moved.

### 5.2 ⚑ THE COST OF THE COST: a cone cannot be lengthened, so the shaft became a PRISM

The mesh was a cone with its apex **at the lamp, 15 m up**. It converges to a point there and then
inverts into its mirror nappe: **11 m → 32 m was not reachable by changing a number.** The only way
to lengthen the cone is to raise the lamp — and **the lamp makes the POOL, which is verdicted and
frozen**.

**Ruling 4 supplies the mechanism.** A sun at infinity emits **parallel** rays, and parallel rays
make a beam with a **constant cross-section**. So the mesh stops being the lamp's cone and becomes
the sun's prism, **about the same axis** — which is why the pool-axis coupling law survives
untouched (§4). One change answers both rulings.

- `sky_shaft.gdshader` / `sky_dust.gdshader` gain `prism_r`: the mask coordinate is divided by a
  **constant** radius instead of by the cone's growing one, and the march is bounded by an analytic
  ray/**cylinder** intersection instead of ray/cone. `prism_r = 0` restores the cone exactly, which
  is how both legacy arms render.
- **One hard bound is deliberately dropped and saying so is the point:** the cone needs `z > 0` to
  reject the mirror nappe behind the lamp. A prism has no apex and no mirror nappe, and the shaft
  now legitimately extends **17 m ABOVE the lamp**, where `z` is negative. The `discard`-outside-the-
  cross-section bound and the chord-reaches-zero-at-the-silhouette bound are **untouched**, so
  "cannot diffuse outwards" is as structural as it was.
- **The radius is DERIVED, not picked:** `SKY_BEAM_PRISM_REF_Y = 6.0 m` is the midpoint of
  BEAM-REAL's measured visible span (2.49 → 10.63 m) — the height the eye was already reading the
  shaft's thickness at. `r = ((SKY_H − 6.0)/cos 24°)·tan 21°·narrow = 1.1345 m`.

## §6 — Three things I got wrong, and how each was caught

### 6.1 ⚑ THE POOL-AXIS INSTRUMENT COMPARED TWO DIFFERENT ORIGINS — and BEAM-REAL never saw it

`_sky_report_beamreal()` compared a **global** axis foot (`sp.global_position`) against the
**room-local** `rec["pool"]`. On the probe — one room, built at the origin — the two frames coincide
and it prints `ERROR 0.000000 m`, which is what BEAM-REAL measured and banked. **On the real
four-room watch it printed `⚑ COUPLING BROKEN` three times, with an error of exactly the room's own
x offset: 47.500004 m, 100.000000 m, 157.500000 m.**

The coupling was never broken — the instrument was reading two origins. Fixed by comparing against
`tilt.global_position` (the pool point in world space by construction). Re-measured on all 8
patterns of the four-room level: **max error 0.000004 m.**

**Caught by running the real level instead of the probe**, which is the lesson: BEAM-REAL's
coupling verdict is still TRUE, but it had only ever been checked in the one room where the bug is
invisible.

### 6.2 ⚑ THE ONE-SUN DEVIATION CHECK HAD THE SIGN OF THE SUN BACKWARDS

The first formulation built the expected axis as `+lean_dir·sin(tilt) + DOWN·cos(tilt)` and returned
**47.999999° on every pattern in every room, in both arms**. That unanimity is impossible — a
per-pattern coin flip cannot make every room agree — and it is what gave it away.

The lamp sits at `pool + lean_dir·tan(tilt)·H + up·H` and is **aimed at the pool**, so the light
travels **against** `lean_dir`: going UP the shaft moves you TOWARD it. The shaft leans **toward**
the sun. The sign was corrected, and the same string on the banner (`sky_sun_identity()`) had the
sun on the wrong side of the sky until it was.

### 6.3 THE BANNER READ THE WRONG OBJECT AND SAID SO ON THE FRAME

`beam_identity_line()` walked the scene tree for a node carrying the beam accessors. The room
builder objects are **never added to the tree** — `wr1_level.gd::_build_room` builds *into* a holder
and keeps the builder in a plain array — so the walk found the geometry and never the object that

## 2. Scope 31 — where the cold actually comes from

### 2.1 The peel matrix

Seven arms, one instrument, CAM-LOCK, NOHUD, seed 74000909, 40 frames each. Only `$EXTRA` differs
between them. Each carrier's own contribution is `mean_linear(SHIPPED) − mean_linear(SHIPPED with
that carrier peeled)` — every one of these is either additive (`blend_add` motes, the shaft) or an
in-scatter term, so what it adds is exactly what removing it takes away. Chromaticity is read on
the *difference*, after tonemap and glow, i.e. as it arrives at the eye and not as it sits in a
`.tres`.

| carrier | peel | frame coverage | total ΔY | R : G : B (linear) | b\* | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sky motes (the "beam particles") | `--nodust 1` | 1.72 % | **61.2** | 0.676 : 0.828 : 1.000 | −7.32 | cold |
| **room ambient dust ("the dust clouds")** | `--noambient 1` | 1.50 % | **54.2** | 0.636 : 0.776 : 1.000 | **−9.39** | **cold** |
| beam shaft + its motes | `--nobeam 1` | **37.25 %** | **8 599.2** | 0.330 : 0.505 : 1.000 | −25.85 | cold |
| sky-lit fog (the room's air) | `--fogunlit` | 23.56 % | **1 973.1** | 0.248 : 0.351 : 1.000 | −39.05 | cold |
| all fog | `--no-fog` | 77.36 % | 11 512.2 | 1.000 : 0.343 : 0.364 | **+9.40** | **warm** |
| whole skylight | `--no-skylight` | 39.34 % | 12 629.9 | 0.321 : 0.475 : 1.000 | −27.88 | cold |

**Three readings that settle Scope 31:**

1. **The beam motes cannot touch the room's dust.** `sky_dust.gdshader` runs the identical
   cone-plus-mask test the shaft runs, against the same uniforms pushed from the same
   `SpotLight3D` in the same frame, and `discard`s anything outside it. A mote in the room is not
   unlikely, it is impossible. The two dust populations deliver 61.2 and 54.2 units respectively
   and never overlap. **There was no contamination path.**
2. **The room's dust really is cold** — b\* −9.39, blue-dominant in every channel ratio. It is cold
   because the AMB-HUE gradients (`vfx/ambient/pp/*_g.tres`, Matt-accepted at Scope 8) are
   purple-to-blue in all five layers. `veil_g`'s last stop is `(0.873, 0.628, 1.978)`.
3. **The fog is the warm system, not the cold one.** Peeling all fog makes the room *colder*
   (whole-frame b\* +1.065 → −0.590): the volumetric fog is carrying the torches. It is the
   *sky-lit portion* of it that is cold, at b\* −39.05 — the most saturated cold in the room.

### 2.2 The instrument I had to retire mid-cell

My first metric was the **unweighted mean b\*** of the room mask. On it, the whole question is
nearly flat: shipped **+1.852**, every sky carrier peeled **+2.365**. A 0.5-unit ceiling on a change
that crosses the room means the metric is wrong, not that the change is small — it gives a dark
corner the same vote as a lit pool, and the eye does not. Replaced by the **luminance-weighted**
mean, `Σ(Y·b*)/ΣY`, which is the chromaticity of the light actually arriving. Recorded here rather
than quietly dropped, because the flat number is in `measure/diag1.json` and a reader would
otherwise find it and reasonably conclude nothing happened.

### 2.3 The mechanisms shipped

Two dials, both defaulting to the shipped state, both luminance-preserving, neither touching a
resource on disk.

* **`--dustwarm k`** — the room's five ambient emitters regrade toward the torch register
  `(1.00, 0.62, 0.30)` at runtime. Every `ParticleProcessMaterial`, `GradientTexture1D` and
  `Gradient` is **duplicated** before it is touched: a `load()`ed resource is shared across all four
  rooms *and* is the object the editor would save. Each stop keeps its **offset**, its **alpha** and
  its exact **Rec.709 luminance**; only chromaticity moves. That matters because three of the four
  things those ramps encode are not what Matt is complaining about — the stop positions, the
  rise-and-fade alpha (AMB-RISE's verdicted read), and the brightness (SKY-2's density ladders were
  all solved against it). `dust_warm = 0` never enters the function, so the BEFORE arm is the
  accepted ambient and not a round-trip of it.
* **`--fogwarm k`** — the room's **air**, on the same dial and the same law. This is not scope
  creep: `FOG_ALBEDO`'s own header says why it is purple — *"a neutral-grey haze read as a separate,
  colder system sitting next to the ambient"*. It was matched **to the AMB-HUE spine**. Scope 31
  moves the ambient off that spine, so leaving the air pinned to it would recreate, in reverse, the
  exact defect that constant was written to avoid. One accessor, `fog_albedo()`, feeds both
  consumers (the `FogVolume`'s material and the Environment's own albedo) — they are the same
  number and a regrade reaching one of them would put two different airs in one room.
* **`--nobeam 1`** — the attribution peel, instrument only. `sky_beam = false` removes the shaft
  mesh **and** its motes and **leaves the pools**. `--nodust` peels only the motes; `--no-skylight`
  takes the pools with it. Neither can answer Matt's question alone.

## 4. Scope 32 — the wet layer, and the kill-test that refuted it

### 4.1 Why it is the floor's own material

Three cheaper implementations were considered and each fails on a property the directive itself
names:

* a **transparent film quad** — Godot multiplies the whole fragment by `ALPHA`, specular included,
  so *"nearly imperceptible"* and *"casts a sheen"* are the same slider pulled in opposite
  directions;
* a **`Decal` writing ORM** — decals fade on the albedo texture's alpha, so a roughness-only decal
  has no authored fade and stamps a hard edge;
* an **additive card computing its own highlight from a pushed lamp position** — that is a
  *synthesised* sheen. It would pass the carried-light guard **by construction**, which is precisely
  why it would prove nothing.

So the wetness is a **roughness field on the opaque floor** (`scripts/wet_floor.gdshader`), lit by
Godot's real GGX and Godot's real lights — which is the only arrangement in which "the sheen comes
from the carried light" is a *testable* claim. Patches are fBm on **world XZ**, not UV: all ~900
floor tiles per room are the same mesh instanced into the same atlas UVs, so a UV-space mask would
stamp one identical puddle on every one of them and read as a repeating decal. A puddle crosses a
tile seam because a puddle does not know where the seams are.

**Arm-0 identity gate:** the shader at `wet_strength = 0` vs the shipped `StandardMaterial3D` it
stands in for, unseeded particles peeled, 3 frames — **max |Δ| = 2/255, zero pixels above 2** over
~150 k floor pixels. Shader-vs-fixed-function rounding, not a look difference. The BEFORE arm still
routes through the *actual* shipped material, not through the shader at zero.

### 4.3 ⚑ The kill-test, and it refuted the arm

`--plkill 1` removes the carried omni **and nothing else**. It is deliberately *not*
`--playerlight B`: arm B moves two things — it removes the lamp **and** un-dims the static centre
pool (`POOL_DIM_ARM_A`) — so a sheen surviving arm B could be the centre pool's, and a sheen dying
with it could have died of the dimming.

The carried lamp's **own** contribution (arm minus its lamp-killed twin), same box:

| floor | mean | p99 | p99.9 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DRY, shipped 0.640 | **+0.01457** | **+0.11094** | +0.16639 | +0.22014 |
| WET, patches → 0.240 | **+0.01362** | **+0.10224** | +0.16125 | +0.20563 |
| WET + lamp 2× | +0.02622 | +0.18833 | +0.26484 | +0.33287 |
| WET + clearcoat + lamp 2× | +0.02078 | +0.13372 | +0.18208 | +0.22205 |

**The film makes the carried lamp's contribution smaller, not larger.** And the geometry says why,
in closed form. Camera height 28.397 m, pitch 52.95°, stand-off 34.83 m; carried lamp at
`CARRY_H = 1.42 m`. A specular highlight lands where the lamp's mirror image lines up with the
camera:

```
f = h / (h + H) = 1.42 / 29.82 = 4.8 %
mirror point = 0.96 m from the player, toward the camera
local incidence there ≈ 34.1° from the floor normal
```

34° is **near-normal**, where a dielectric returns its Fresnel *floor* (F0 = 0.04) — so tightening
the stone's lobe returns a tighter 4 %, and 4 % of very little is very little. And 0.96 m puts that
highlight **under the player's own body**. Meanwhile the twelve sconces sit near floor level at
**grazing** incidence, where Fresnel climbs steeply — so they rake the film beautifully. That is the
whole result: **the film brightens the room 1.11×, and the room is what brightens.**

**Clearcoat** was added afterwards as the *physically correct* model — a water film is a second
interface above the stone, which is what a clearcoat lobe is, not a change to the stone's own
roughness. It brightens the frame 1.69× and still returns **less** to the lamp than doubling the
lamp does: clearcoat+2× lamp vs plain-film lamp is **1.526× mean / 1.308× p99**, against the 2.0×
the energy doubling alone would predict.

### 4.4 The answer to Matt's (c) question, stated plainly

He asked for the energy bump *"so Matt can see whether the lamp or the mirror was the limiting
factor."* Measured: **the lamp**, and not only on energy. Doubling it scales its floor contribution
~1.8×; every mirror arm raises the room's response to the *torches* more than to the *carried lamp*,
because the lamp stands over the point it would have to rake. **At this camera pitch and this lamp
height, no floor-material change routes more of the carried lamp's light to the eye.** Routed as
**F-W-2** — if Matt wants a highlight that visibly belongs to the player, the levers are the lamp's
*energy* and its *placement* (height/offset), not the floor's roughness. No such change is proposed
here; `CARRY_H` / `CARRY_OUT` are SHADOW-UNIFY's silhouette fix and would need re-measuring.

must not be able to move each other, which is the same law `--keyE` was separated under.

## 7. Two of my own encode defects, caught by the standing gate

F-AH-6 earns its row twice in one cell.

1. **Every clip shipped at 50 fps.** `-loop 1 -i label.png` defaults to **25 fps**; stacked against
   30 fps video the filter graph settled at 50, so a 12-second fight encoded as 7.17 s and played
   half again too fast. Invisible in a still, invisible in a file listing, and it would have been
   what Matt judged the *motion* of. Caught by putting `duration` next to `nb_read_frames` in the
   verify line. Fixed by `-framerate 30` on every looped input plus `fps=30` in the graph and `-r
   30` on output.
2. **The half-speed section came out of a concat that emitted non-monotonic DTS.** Three attempts
   (concat demuxer with `-c copy`, concat filter, `setpts` with `STARTPTS` reset) each produced a
   file that *decoded* but carried duplicate timestamps and a wrong container duration — 442 frames
   claiming 9.89 s. Rather than ship a mux I could not certify, the rake segment became **its own
   single-pass clip** built straight from the PNGs at `-framerate 15 … -r 30`. One pass, no concat,
   nothing to certify.

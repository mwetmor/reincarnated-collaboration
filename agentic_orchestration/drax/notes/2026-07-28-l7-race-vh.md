## §1 — BRIEF 1: **CAST**

### §1.1 Approach

**The brief's real ask is legibility of a SEQUENCE**, not prettiness of a fire. So the effect is
authored as **four beats with hard boundaries** rather than as one continuous emitter:

| beat | window | what |
|---|---|---|
| CHARGE | 0.00–0.50 s | inward-converging motes at the hand; the cast light rises as `k²` |
| FLASH | 0.50–0.62 s | one-shot, `explosiveness = 1.0`, light spikes to 15.0 over a 9.5 m range |
| TRAVEL | 0.52–1.05 s | core + world-space trail across **12.01 m at 22.6 m/s**, ease-out |
| IMPACT | 1.05–1.75 s | burst + expanding **ground ring** + a 1.5 s spark tail |

Cycle = **3.00 s**, which is exactly the rig's 90-frame settle at `--fixed-fps 30`, so capture
begins on a beat boundary and **the 6 s clip carries the whole sequence twice** — a blind judge
gets to check a reading rather than catch one.

**Element: cold arcane (cyan → white core).** Chosen on a measurement, not a preference. B-cold is
a warm-orange room (torch dressing, sconces, a 1.0/0.58/0.25 interior pool), so cyan is the only
hue in frame that cannot be confused with the stage's own light. Legibility was the brief, so hue
separation is a legibility decision.

**The three techniques this arm leans on** (named so the race can attribute the result to the
method rather than to taste):

1. **World-space trail.** `BoltTrail` runs `local_coords = false` while its parent translates, so
   particles are *left behind*. The streak is a consequence of motion, not a modelled ribbon.
2. **The bolt carries a light.** An `OmniLight3D` rides the projectile; a second fires at impact.
   The floor is lit *by* the event as it crosses — the single strongest cue that the thing is in
   the room rather than composited over it. A particle system alone cannot do this.
3. **HDR only in the core.** `glow_hdr_threshold` is 1.25 (Matt ruling 2026-06-22, untouched), so
   only the first few percent of each ramp is allowed above it.

Plus a fourth, which is really a rigging move: **`vh_arm_raise.gd`**, the cast pose as a
`SkeletonModifier3D`. In the retargeted idle `prop_r` hangs at hip height *inside* the silhouette,
so an effect placed honestly at `prop_r` renders near the caster's knee — the contract satisfied
and the picture not. The fix moves the **socket**, not the effect. It is a `SkeletonModifier3D`
rather than `set_bone_pose_rotation` from `_process` because the latter is a race against the
AnimationPlayer that happens to be winnable on some frames; the modifier runs inside the
skeleton's own update, after the clip, additively (the idle's sway survives underneath).

### §1.2 ★ The two findings the iterations produced

Both are arithmetic, not taste, and both generalise past this cell.

**(a) i1 → i2 — WHICH WAY THE CASTER FACES DECIDES WHETHER A RAISED ARM READS AS RAISED.**

i1 put the caster at the screen-right end facing left. The modifier lifted `prop_r` to **1.520 m**
above his feet — printed, measured, real — and the charge still rendered **below his chest**.

```
screen-up, in world, at R-6           = (-0.569, 0.628, -0.531)
raised hand, relative to the feet     = ( 0.689, 1.520,  0.818)   world
dot                                   = +0.129    <- 0.13 m of screen rise for 1.52 m of lift
```

The arm raises to the character's own *side* (`prop_r` sits at body-local `(-1.062, 1.520, -0.128)`),
and at yaw 137° that lateral 1.06 m points **down-screen** hard enough to cancel the vertical gain.
Rotating the body 180° flips the sign of the lateral term and leaves the vertical alone:

| facing | screen-up in body-local | hand · screen-up |
|---|---|---|
| yaw 137° (i1) | (+0.778, 0.628, 0) | **0.129** |
| yaw −43° (i2) | (−0.778, 0.628, 0) | **1.780** |

**13.8× more screen rise, from swapping the two endpoints. Not one FX value moved.** The bolt also
now flies screen-left → screen-right, which is the friendlier reading direction.

*General form:* at a pitched camera a character's **lateral** axis carries a large screen-vertical
component whose **sign is set by the character's yaw**. Any gesture authored out to the side is
either helped or cancelled by facing, and here the difference was an order of magnitude. Measure
`offset · screen_up`, never `offset.y`.

**(b) i2 → i3 — A TRAIL'S GAPS ARE THE EMISSION CADENCE, NOT THE PARTICLE COUNT.**

i2's trail rendered as a dotted line of ~10 discrete blobs. The arithmetic says why: at
`fixed_fps = 30` the emitter fires **one batch per frame**, and the bolt covers
`22.6 / 30 = 0.75 m` between batches. A 0.16 m sphere emitter therefore stamps 0.32 m clusters into
0.75 m gaps. **Adding particles would have produced denser dots.** Both fixes target the cadence:
a **box emitter stretched 0.80 m along the travel axis** (each batch covers its own step) and
**60 Hz emission** (step halves to 0.38 m). Continuous streak for **+14 particles**, not +90.

**(c) i3 → i4 — the flash was the weakest beat across three renders.** At 12 m the impact owned the
frame and the launch read as a glimmer, so the sequence played as *"something arrived"* rather than
*"he threw it."* The flash is the beat that **attributes the event to the caster**, so it was given
its own budget (58 → 88 particles, +48 % scale, light 9.6 → 15.0). Also fixed at i3: the impact
burst spent 12 % of its life above the glow threshold across 76 large simultaneous particles and
produced 3 frames of featureless white disc — **this file violating its own technique-3 rule.** The
white segment was cut to 5 %; and the ground ring, damped at 7–13 in i2, never left the impact
point, so the one beat that owns the **floor** (77 % of the frame at −50°) was invisible. Damping
cut to 1.4–2.8: the ring now runs ~3.2 m out over 0.55 s.

### §2.1 The clause that is a number gets an instrument

*"Must not eat the silhouette"* is the only clause in the whole race that is measurable rather than
judgeable, and it names the commonest way a character aura fails. So it was measured.

**SIL-1** (`tmp/l7race/VH/silhouette.py`) takes three renders of the **same frame** — empty room /
caster / caster+aura — and:

1. derives the **body mask by subtraction** (`|luma(body) − luma(empty)| > 10`), so it needs no
   assumption about the werewolf's albedo;
2. reports the **bbox height as a % of frame** — because a silhouette result at the wrong scale is
   not a result;
3. reports **eaten fraction** = the share of body-mask pixels the aura raises by more than 40/255,
   split **interior vs rim band** (an aura that lives at the rim and one that covers the chest can
   produce the same total and are not the same picture);
4. reports **Sobel edge retention** on a 2-px band around the mask boundary, aura vs bare body —
   the silhouette *is* the boundary, so the ratio is a direct statement about whether it survived.

No threshold in that file was chosen after seeing a result.

### §2.2 ★ Finding 1 — the figure is **9.31 %** of frame height at R-6, not 12.5 %

Measured bbox: **61 × 67 px in a 1280×720 frame → 9.31 % height.**

The 12.5 % of record comes from `1.8 / (2 · 34 · tan 12°) = 12.45 %`, which is the frame's vertical
extent at the aim plane — and it **omits the camera pitch**. At −50° a standing figure is
foreshortened by roughly `cos 50° = 0.643`; `12.45 × 0.643 = 8.0 %`, and the measured 9.31 % is that
plus the walk pose's depth (stride, arms, tail).

**The clause is therefore being tested at three quarters of its stated scale**, on a caster the
charter froze at 1.80 m (R-PC-8). This cell does **not** resize the werewolf to make the number
come out — that would move frozen substrate to fit a metric. Per L-N the gap is recorded rather
than worked around. **Conductor's call** whether the clause's number moves or the framing does; it
is the same for all three arms, so it does not advantage anyone.

### §2.3 ★ Finding 2 — "eaten fraction" cannot tell **lit** from **covered**, and the split proves it

The first SIL-1 run returned 16.07 % eaten with edge retention 1.51 — a good-looking result that
was measuring the wrong thing, because once i2 added a key light the same metric jumped to 76.89 %
while the picture got *more* readable, not less. A metric that moves that way under an improvement
is a metric with two variables in it.

So a second control was added — `--opt nopart`, the aura's **lights with zero sprites**:

| build | eaten (whole body) | eaten (interior) | eaten (rim) | mean luma added | **edge retention** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lights only, no sprites | 75.46 % | 88.60 % | 65.39 % | +58.98 | **1.2060** |
| **full aura** | 76.89 % | 89.46 % | 67.25 % | +61.61 | **1.2957** |
| **sprite contribution** | **+1.43 pt** | +0.86 pt | +1.86 pt | +2.63 | **+0.0897** |

**Every particle in the aura accounts for 1.43 points of that 76.89 %.** 98 % of the number is
*illumination* — the body going from near-black to lit — which is the opposite of occlusion. And
adding the sprites **raises** edge retention from 1.206 to 1.296, i.e. the outline is ~30 % crisper
with the full aura than with no aura at all.

**The clause-relevant numbers are therefore the 1.43 pt sprite delta and the edge-retention ratio
of 1.2957, both of which say the silhouette is intact.** The 76.89 % headline is reported anyway,
because deleting the number that looks bad after building the control that explains it would be
exactly the goalpost-move LSTAT-2 was invented in PC-LIGHT to prevent.

### §2.4 Approach — the four moves, in order of how much they buy

1. **The aura lights him; it does not cover him.** The largest element is an `OmniLight3D`, not a
   sprite. This is the move a particle-only surface cannot make.
2. **Emit from a shell, not a volume.** Godot's `EMISSION_SHAPE_BOX` fills its *volume* and would
   put as many particles over the chest as around the outline — the exact failure mode. The body
   motes instead use `EMISSION_SHAPE_POINTS` fed by **320 hand-generated points on a
   0.62 × 1.72 × 0.62 box surface, distributed across the six faces by area**, baked into an
   `n × 1` RGBF `ImageTexture` (the material takes a point *texture*, not an array). Seeded, so
   two renders emit from the same set.
3. **The floor carries the mass.** Ground ring + rising embers, below the body, where they have
   77 % of the frame to spread into and nothing to occlude.
4. **No additive element exceeds 0.16 m.** At this scale small sprites are texture; large ones are
   occlusion.

**Survives motion — both halves, deliberately:**
- shell + rim motes run `local_coords = TRUE` → carried by the body, **glued at any speed, no lag**;
- ground ring + embers run `local_coords = FALSE` → **shed into world space and left behind**, so
  the walk drags a readable wake and a still of him mid-stride is distinguishable from a still of
  him standing.

Either component alone answers half the clause.

**i1 → i2, the third change:** an omni at the body's *centre* lights the room and leaves the body a
black cut-out, because every surface facing the camera has its back to that light. SIL-1 i1 measured
a **perfect silhouette that was also an unreadable one**. The key light was moved 1.5 m along
`normalize(eye) = (0.470, 0.796, 0.438)` and parented to the **brief**, not the caster, so it does
not yaw with him — the camera never moves, so neither does the direction this light comes from. The
floor pool was also cut from energy 3.4 / range 7.2 (which washed the *whole room* yellow-green and
threw away the hue separation the element was chosen for) to 2.3 / 4.8.

**Element: verdant spirit-fire.** The crypt is warm orange and brief 1 took cold cyan, so green is
the remaining separating hue. It is also the hue Rec.709 weights highest (0.7152), i.e. the easiest
colour to blow a silhouette out with — the budget discipline above is load-bearing, not decorative.

### §3.1 The two inherited laws, and what each one actually forbids

**R-10** (L7-V, *derived not preferred*): *"at an ARPG camera, ambient volume belongs on the ground
plane, not in the air. At −50° a vertical column is seen against the floor, never the sky."*
→ Every layer is a **horizontal sheet**. The tallest element in the brief tops out at **1.35 m** in
a room whose walls reach **3.006 m**. There is no column, no plume, no rising volume.

**TCP-51** (*"area is the entire cost"*) → then the budget is spent in **count**, which is cheap,
and hoarded in **scale**, which is not. Printed at build so it is auditable from the log:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 floor drift | 300 | 0.165 m | 8.17 m² | ash sliding on the draught, at the five lit zones |
| L2 ground mist (**MIX**) | 60 | 0.700 m | **29.40 m²** | the only veiling layer — additive can only brighten |

L2 is 8.6 % of the particles and 53 % of the area. That asymmetry **is** the TCP-51 trade, stated as
a number rather than as a preference.

### §3.2 "Room-owned" — and why the pass is warm and is placed where the light is

The stage is a sealed 17.5 m stone box with fires in it and no ceiling. What such a room *has* is
still cold air near the floor, ash the fires lift and the draught carries, and haze that exists only
where there is light to reveal it. So the pass is **warm**, per **R-7** verbatim (*"the substrate's
own unexplained light is warm. Warm fire EXPLAINS it; green fire FIGHTS it."*). Briefs 1 and 2 took
cyan and green because they belong to a **character**; a character is not the room.

**The dense layers sit at the five lit zones** (four sconce corners + the centre pool), because dust
is invisible in the dark: an even room-wide sheet would spend most of its budget in black floor
where it returns nothing *and* lift p05 off zero, eating the crypt contrast PC-LIGHT bought over 14

### §3.3 ★ i1 → i2 — the law doing the steering

i1 measured as **present but not judgeable**: 1.72 % of pixels moved by more than 2, mean |ΔL| 0.233.

### §3.4 ★★ The cited law was TESTED, not quoted

A law that is quoted and never exercised is a citation. The rig grew a `--bench` mode (run N frames,
write nothing) and the brief grew three counterfactual builds that separate **count** from **area**
on the identical stage:

| build | count | scale | area |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (shipped) | ×1 | ×1 | ×1 |
| `big` | ×1 | ×4 | **×16** |
| `many` | ×16 | ×1 | **×16** |
| `small` | ×16 | ×¼ | ×1 |

The two hypotheses predict opposite orderings, so one sweep decides it.

**Two instrument failures came first, and both are retained as the controls that caught them (L-N):**

1. Wall-clock returned **6.046 ms/frame for every variant *and* for the no-brief control** — which
   is 165.4 fps, i.e. this display's refresh rate. **The instrument was measuring vsync.** An
   instrument that has only ever said one thing has not been shown capable of saying another.
2. Disabling vsync and uncapping `Engine.max_fps` **did not move it** — macOS/Metal keeps the
   windowed surface on the display's beat.
3. `RenderingServer.viewport_get_measured_render_time_gpu()` returns **0.0000 ms** on Metal in
   4.6.3 — the GPU-timestamp path is not implemented on this backend. **Named as a gap, not
   invented as a negative.**

Cleared by rendering at **3840×2160** (9× the pixels), which puts the frame above the 6.05 ms floor.
**Three repeats; per-rep deltas against that rep's own control, because absolute times drift
thermally across a sweep:**

| build | rep 1 | rep 2 | rep 3 | **median Δ vs control** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (shipped) | +0.256 | +0.197 | +0.412 | **+0.256 ms** |
| `small` — ×16 count, same area | +0.398 | +0.402 | +0.318 | **+0.398 ms** |
| `big` — same count, ×16 area | +0.497 | +0.538 | +0.419 | **+0.497 ms** |
| `many` — ×16 both | +0.697 | +0.731 | +0.645 | **+0.697 ms** |

So, on median: **×16 area at constant count costs +0.241 ms; ×16 count at constant area costs
+0.142 ms**; both together +0.441 ms (near-additive). Per-variant spread across reps is ~±0.10 ms,
so the **area** term is resolved at roughly 2.4σ and the **count** term at roughly 1.4σ. The
ordering `A < small < big < many` held in 2 of 3 reps (rep 3 inverted A/big inside noise).

> **Verdict on the law, stated at the strength the data supports:** TCP-51's **ordering is
> confirmed** — area is the larger driver, ~1.7× the count term. Its **exclusivity is not**: count
> is not free, though its contribution here is only suggestive. And at the **delivery resolution**
> of 1280×720, none of it resolves at all — the whole 700-particle pass sits under a 6.05 ms vsync
> floor, so for a pass of this size the TCP-51 budget is currently **not a binding constraint**. It
> would become one at 16× area.

This is offered as a finding for the conductor, not as a unilateral amendment to a standing law.

### §3.8 ★★ R-LR-4 — CONTAINMENT (Matt, mid-run) — and the eye was right

> **R-LR-4 (Matt-directed 2026-07-28, mid-run eye on V-H ambient):** *"we need to make sure the
> ambient particles don't pass through/out of the walls into the void."* Binding AMBIENT contract
> clause; a leak at verdict time is a **MISS**, not a style note.

**The i2 build leaked, and Matt's eye caught what the brief's own arithmetic had never checked.**
Measured after the fact: **3,752 void pixels lit, max +160.7 luma, in a band spanning the full frame
width** (`bbox [0, 385, 1279, 482]` — the near-wall region and the void below the room).

**Satisfied by construction, not by a collider.** A `GPUParticlesCollisionBox3D` is a per-particle
per-frame cost imposed on the one brief whose contract *is* a cost budget (TCP-51), and a collider
can only be looked at — an analytic bound can be printed, checked and reviewed. Per layer:

```
birth_clamp = interior_half - margin - drift_bound - sprite_half
drift_bound = v_max · lifetime + ½ · |a_horizontal| · lifetime²     (damping-free ⇒ strict)
```

Every emission point and emission box is clamped to `birth_clamp`, so no particle can be **born**
outside it and none can **reach** the wall before it dies. `containment_report()` prints it at build:

| layer | v_max | life | drift ≤ | sprite half | birth ≤ | **reach ≤** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 floor drift | 0.26 | 5.0 s | 1.536 | 0.122 | 6.442 | **8.100** |
| L2 ground mist | 0.13 | 6.5 s | 1.084 | 0.475 | 6.541 | **8.100** |
| L3 room motes | 0.18 | 6.5 s | 1.369 | 0.031 | 6.700 | **8.100** |
| L4 corner haze | 0.16 | 6.0 s | 1.130 | 0.230 | 6.740 | **8.100** |
| | | | | | **worst case** | **8.100 m vs 8.30 m wall → CONTAINED** |

**Arithmetic can be wrong about an engine, so it is also tested from pixels.** `VOID-1`
(`tmp/l7race/VH/void_leak.py`) takes the void mask **straight off the stage-only frame** — the crypt
is a walled box on a pure-black surround, so the void is simply where the stage renders black. No
camera maths, no projected wall polygon to get wrong. It then counts any void pixel the ambient pass
brightened by more than 2 luma.

Subtracting the stage frame is exactly what **excludes the corner-torch embers**, which *do* rise
past the wall tops. They are the frozen Task-0 dressing, identical for all three arms, and **Matt's
eye passed them at "100 % perfect" in the same message that set this clause.** Only what *this brief*
added is measured.

| build | leak px | max leak luma | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **i2 — pre-fix** | **3,752** | +160.7 | **LEAK** |
| **i3 — shipped** | **0** | +0.0 | **CONTAINED** |

**The instrument therefore fires in both directions, and it is the pre-fix build that proves it
can** (L-N discharged: an instrument that has only ever returned zero has not been shown capable of
returning anything else).

**Containment cost the pass nothing.** In-room moved pixels went **up**, 4.26 % → 4.78 %, and
LSTAT-2 rose again, 7.150 → **7.229** (+5.6 % on the bare stage's 6.847). Shortening the lifetimes
that were letting particles escape *concentrated* the pass rather than thinning it — the same
lesson as §3.2, arriving from the other direction.

**The grammar, stated as arithmetic rather than as an adjective.** With damping opposing motion, net vertical accel is `gravity.y − damping`:

- flame: `0.42 − 0.575 = −0.155 m/s²`; v0 0.80 → **0.679 at death (85% retained)**; rise **0.577 m** in 0.78 s; velocity would reverse at 5.2 s = **6.6× the lifetime**.
- embers: `0.22 − 0.525 = −0.305`; v0 1.225 → 0.432 (35%); rise **2.154 m** in 2.6 s; reversal at **1.5× the lifetime**.

So the flame's "rise" is not an impulse that decays. It is a **positive Y gravity large enough to cancel most of the drag**, chosen so the particle *never falls inside its own life*. The 4.5% of the flame's rise contributed by the acceleration term, the v0min/v0max ratio (0.524) and the damping max/min ratio (2.833) are the whole of the character.

**The recipe was validated by inversion before it was used.** Solving those relationships for the flame's own `(h_max, T)` returns the flame's own authored constants — v0 **0.549/1.047** against the authored 0.550/1.050, damping 0.316/0.896 against 0.300/0.850, gravity 0.443 against 0.420. Every layer below is that same solve, re-run at a different lifetime and height budget. It is a sample, not a pastiche.

## §3 — The complaint, measured before anything moved — and it was worse than "doesn't rise"

**Two instruments were built. The first was thrown away, by its own control.**

AMB-RISE-1 (`tmp/ambrise/amb_rise.py`) recovers the field's screen velocity by zero-mean cross-correlation of successive ambient-only frames. Its **synthetic** control passed cleanly — and caught a real defect on the way, returning `(−3, +7)` for a planted `(+3, −7)`: right magnitude, **inverted sign**, i.e. an instrument that would have reported a rise as a fall. Fixed. But its **torch** control then failed: run on the frozen flames, whose own parameters say 0.74 m/s, it reported **0.02 m/s**. That is structural, not tuning. *A continuously-emitting system at steady state has a stationary envelope* — the particles move, the shape they make does not, and image correlation locks onto the shape. A lag sweep confirmed it (peak zncc 0.997 → 0.686 as lag grew, velocity pinned at ~0 throughout). **No ambient number from that instrument was used.**

AMB-RISE-2 (`scripts/ambrise_probe.gd`) asks the simulation instead. `GPUParticles3D.capture_aabb()` bounds the live particles; its vertical span minus the emission volume's own height **is** the travel, in metres, with no camera, projection or threshold in it. Two corrections were needed and both were read from source rather than fitted: Godot grows that box by the **draw mesh's longest axis** on every side (exactly 1.000 m here — the uncorrected first run reported the flame rising 2.463 m), and GPU particles **do not step at all when nothing draws them**, so the probe needs a camera it never looks through.

**The control passes: the frozen torch flame measures 0.558 m against its closed-form 0.577 m — 3.3%.**

The layers are named *drift* and *mist*; the arithmetic says *parked*. The streak plate shows the same thing without a number in it: round dots BEFORE, streaks AFTER.

**`direction` is the load-bearing edit and it is not a tweak.** The old direction vector *was the draught* — (0.85, 0.1, −0.52) is `DRAUGHT.normalized()` with a 10% Y nudge on top. The layers were pointed sideways. They now point up, and the draught survives only as the small lateral gravity lean it should always have been.

**`flatness` is the mechanical root.** L1 and L2 sat at 1.0, which confines the spread to a single plane; with the direction turned vertical, leaving it there would have fanned the rise inside a flat sheet — a paper cut-out of a rise. The flame carries no flatness at all. Zeroed to match.

**Per-layer judgment, declared as the brief asks:**

- **L6_Embers — the full rise.** It is the same object as `TorchEmbers`, so it gets the least-diluted treatment: 1.60 m measured, the largest of the room-wide layers.
- **L1_FloorDrift — the full rise**, and the biggest number on the board at 1.78 m. Ash lifted by fires is the layer with the strongest physical claim to rising, and it starts lowest (0.26 m), so it has the most headroom to spend.
- **L3_RoomMotes — the full rise, budget-limited to 0.71 m.** Its emission box is already 1.1 m tall and seats at 0.75 m, so it begins where the others end; a full-height rise on top would put dust above the wall line for no gain. Its spread also came *down*, 55° → 28°, into the flame family — 55° is a fan, not a column.
- **L2_GroundMist — the GENTLER LIFT, taken deliberately.** 0.25 m over 6 s, an eighth of the flame's rate. Matt's brief named this exact risk and it is real: mist is the heaviest layer, the largest sprites (0.26–0.50) at knee height, and a room-wide sheet of half-metre sprites climbing at ash speed is **a boiler room, not a crypt**. It now rises visibly and stays in the band it belongs to (top 0.95 m). Its spread went *up*, 22° → 42°: a broad soft cone, because a narrow one would have made mist into columns.
- **L5_CentrePool — the gentlest, and for a measured reason rather than a preference.** Its emission **sphere** is 4.5 m in radius, so its field already reaches **3.80 m — above the room's 3.17 m wall — before this cell touched anything.** A sphere has no separate Y to hold, so AMB-REFIT's "Y held" could not apply to it; that is an inherited condition, now measured and on the record. A full 1.9 m rise on top would have pushed it to 5.7 m. It gets +0.27 m.

**Height budget, checked against precedent rather than against a rule of thumb: the torch embers already top out at 4.60 m in this room, over the 3.17 m wall, and Matt ruled that VFX perfect.** Every layer here lands below that.

### §0.3 ★ WHERE THE WIRE RAN, AND WHY IT IS NOT `reincarnated-godot`

Stated up front because it is the biggest declared deviation in this cell.

The Murzak core addon is **C#**: it needs a `Godot.NET.Sdk` csproj, a `dotnet build`, the NuGet pins
(ReflectorNet 5.4.0 / McpPlugin 7.5.2), and the **mono** Godot binary. `reincarnated-godot` is a
GDScript project rendered by the standard `/Applications/Godot.app`. Installing the addon into it
would have meant, all at once:

1. adding a csproj and a `[dotnet]` section to the **frozen** `project.godot` (R-LR-8's exact class);
2. letting a **second Godot build** open and re-import ~4,000 Synty assets — while **R-LR-7 is
   actively watching an unexplained stage drift**, i.e. deliberately introducing the most plausible
   remaining suspect into the middle of the investigation;
3. the addon writing **105 unasked `SKILL.md` files** into `<project>/.claude/` (PC-T4 step 6, T6);
4. displacing the project's incumbent `addons/godot_mcp` (same directory, hardcoded), whose
   `MCPGameBridge` autoload `project.godot` declares.

So the wire operated on the **PROVISION-CAL lab project** `/Users/admin/Games/mcp-lab/pct4/project/`
— already provisioned at the frozen pins, already built clean, already **PACKET-QUIET verified** —
and its output was copied here to render. The output is plain-text `.tscn`/`.tres` with **no addon
dependency**, and the `res://` paths are identical on both sides, so nothing is rewritten in transit.

**Both directions are md5-verified** (`tmp/l7race/VMUR/pull.sh`, which prints the parity table):

- **before authoring**, the five STAGE-SUPPLIED draw-pass resources `res://vfx/stage/*.tres`
  (charter §1) were copied *into* the wire's project — all five byte-identical — so the wire could
  point `draw_pass_1` at them **itself**;
- **after each fire**, the wire's `.tscn` and its `.tres` set were copied *back* — every file
  byte-identical on every pull (`1 .tscn + 21 .tres — ALL MD5-IDENTICAL`).

**Consequence, stated against my own convenience:** this arm never had the option of pointing the
wire at the real stage. It could not have inspected, measured or accidentally perturbed
`kit_replica_level.gd`, and it did not. `project.godot` in `reincarnated-godot` is

### §0.5 ★ THE CELL'S ONE AUTHORING CEILING: `Godot.StringName` is not writable

Six attempts across two probe rounds, four distinct encodings:

| # | encoding | wire's answer |
|---|---|---|
| A | `pathPatches [{path:"Autoplay", value:{typeName, valueJsonElement}}]` | schema rejection: *"Did you want to use 'name', 'typeName', 'value', 'fields' or 'props'?"* |
| B | `jsonPatch {"Autoplay":{"typeName":…,"value":…}}` | *"Segment 'typeName' not found on type 'StringName'. Available fields: NativeValue, _weakReferenceToSelf"* |
| C | `jsonPatch {"Autoplay":["probeclip"]}` | *"could not be converted to Godot.StringName"* |
| D | `pathPatches` + the documented `{typeName, value}` envelope | *"could not be converted to Godot.StringName"* |
| E | declare it `System.String` and let the converter widen | *"Type mismatch: 'System.String' vs 'Godot.StringName'"* |
| F | write the struct's own only field, `NativeValue` | *"Type mismatch: 'System.String' vs 'Godot.NativeInterop.godot_string_name-movable'"* |

### §1.2 ★★ THE FINDING: `animation-add-track` ACCEPTS A TARGET PATH THAT DOES NOT EXIST

**i1 and i2 shipped a completely dead TRAVEL beat, and every call reported OK.**

`animation-add-track`'s `targetPath` is *"the animated node's path relative to the player's root
node"* — and an `AnimationPlayer`'s default root node is its **parent**, i.e. the scene root, **not
the player**. `BoltCore`, `BoltTrail` and `BoltLight` are children of the carrier `Bolt`, so their
paths are `Bolt/BoltCore`, not `BoltCore`. The wire wrote what it was told:

```
tracks/2/path = NodePath("BoltCore:emitting")      <- resolves to FX/BoltCore, which does not exist
tracks/3/path = NodePath("BoltTrail:emitting")     <- ditto
tracks/8/path = NodePath("BoltLight:light_energy") <- ditto
```

Three of eleven tracks pointed at nothing. The core never emitted, the trail never emitted, the
travelling light never lit — and `isError` was `false` on all three calls.

**It was caught by measurement, not by eye.** The added-luma census over i1 read
`f18 11.28 M → f21 5.97 M → f24 2.83 M → f27 0.76 M → **f30 0.019 M** → f33 0.023 M → f36 6.14 M`.
0.019 M is the empty-stage torch-flicker floor. A flash, then *nothing at all* for 0.4 s, then an
impact: the bolt was never in the room. On a contact sheet at 720p that reads as "the travel is a bit
subtle."

**Confirmed as a general property of the tool, not as my typo,** with one deliberate probe:

```
animation-add-track {"targetPath":"ThisNodeDoesNotExist","property":"emitting"}
  -> isError: false, 20.05 ms, track created
```

> **★ This is W-MUR's silent-success class, and it is the exact analogue of V-PRO §1.6.** That cell
> lost an iteration to `create_scene` refusing while every individual call reported OK; this one lost
> two iterations to a **timeline tool that validates neither the node nor the property it is told to
> animate.** Both are the same lesson: *on either wire, a per-call OK is not a statement about the
> scene.* The portable mitigation is the one already in use — an independent disk read plus a
> quantitative census, every iteration.

### §1.3 ★ THE OTHER FINDING: A SPRITE SCALE IS A LENGTH, AND ONLY A CAMERA TURNS IT INTO A PICTURE

i1's effect rendered as a **pure light wash**: a soft blue pool over the whole floor with no sprite
structure in it at all. The sprites were there and were correct; they were 0.035–0.22 m against a
stage-supplied 1 m quad, and at R-6 the frame spans 14.45 m over 720 px = **49.8 px/m**, so they were
**1.7–11 px**. Below the floor of what reads at 720p.

i2 raised every layer to 0.075–0.58 m (4–29 px), and the picture inverted: the sprites became the
effect and the light became its glow.

> **This is V-PRO §2.2(b) arriving from the opposite direction.** That cell found
> `apply_particle_preset("magic")` shipping 0.3–0.8 m motes that render **15–40 px on a 72 px
> figure** — a preset that assumed a 3 m camera. This cell hand-picked 0.035–0.22 m and got sprites
> that vanish. Same law, opposite sign: **a particle scale is a distance in metres, and it is only a
> picture once multiplied by the camera's px/m.** A preset cannot know that number; neither can an
> author who has not computed it. **49.8 px/m at R-6** is the constant either one needs.

**Travel is monotonic, and the centroid says so** (luma-weighted centroid of pixels raised >12):

```
f16 318 px   f21 416   f24 559   f27 693   f30 906   f33 945   f36 978  <- impact, and it stops
```

662 px of screen travel with no reversal.

**i3 → i4 — the trail was a beaded chain, and the arithmetic says why.** The bolt covers
`12.0 / (0.54 × 30) = 0.740 m` between frames, and `local_coords = false` means every particle
restarting inside a frame uses that frame's emitter transform. A 0.84 m box stamping 6.7 particles of
6–17 px into a 0.74 m step reads as beads. Both terms were fixed rather than one: box **0.84 → 1.10 m**
(clusters now overlap the step) and count **110 → 260** (16 particles per stamp, not 6.7), with scale
*tightened* 0.13–0.34 → 0.14–0.28 so the extra count is not paid for in area (TCP-51 ordering).
Continuous streak. Also corrected here: the carrier's alignment yaw, which was
`atan2(dx, −dz)` where the identity requires `atan2(−dz, dx)` — 48.10° instead of 41.90°, i.e. the
stretched box was 6.2° off the travel it was stretched along.

**i4 → i5 — the floor beat was not reaching the floor.** At `radial_accel 5.5–8.0` against
`damping 1.4–2.8` the ring's terminal speed is `a/d ≈ 3.2 m/s` and it stalls inside ~1.8 m, so the one
element that owns the **77 % of frame that is floor** never left the impact point. Accel → 9.5–13.5,
damping → 1.1–1.9, lifetime 0.75 → 0.95, count 56 → 90: the ring now opens across the flagstones and
decays there.

### §3.1 ★ Containment is an inequality, and the generator refuses to pretend otherwise

R-LR-4 is a clause about space, so it was made arithmetic **inside the authoring file**, evaluated

came back `LEAK PREDICTED` with a worst-case reach of **12.350 m** — and **no frame was ever
rendered from it.** The cause is a modelling error worth naming because it will recur:

> **A Godot particle `gravity` is an ACCELERATION, and it acts for the whole lifetime.** The corner
> dressing's own draught vector `(0.16, 0, −0.10)` has magnitude 0.189 m/s², which over a 5 s life
> contributes `½ · 0.189 · 25 = 2.36 m` of drift **on its own** — more than the entire velocity term.
> The first build inherited that vector as a *direction* and kept its *magnitude*. Direction kept,
> magnitude cut to a tenth.

8.30; a haze box centred on the real sconce leaks **by construction**. It is centred at **±5.80**

roof**, containment is a two-directional test: the tallest thing the brief can produce tops out at
2.256 m against a 3.006 m wall.

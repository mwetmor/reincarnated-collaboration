## §1 — FIX A1: the werewolf faces and walks BACKWARDS

> **Matt, twice, across two strips:** *"the werewolf is facing backwards"* (CAST) ·
> *"the werewolf is facing backwards and walking backwards"* (AURA).

### §1.1 The defect class, named first

This is **metric green / eye red**. The substrate was certified RETARGET-READY at
**0.0000° mean/max rest-Δ** (R-PC-8) and that certificate is *not wrong* — it is **silent**.
That metric compares **bone rests between source and target skeletons**. Facing convention is
a property of *which way the assembled body points in its own local space*, which no bone-to-bone
delta can express. A retarget can be perfect and the body can still be installed backwards.

### §1.2 The measurement (`scripts/wr1_facing_probe.gd`)

Model forward read off the **rest skeleton**, in caster-local space, three ways, printed
separately so a disagreement would be visible rather than averaged away:

```
[facing] C  shoulder line  L-R=(+0.6521, +0.0000, +0.0000)  left-shoulder on +X  => MODEL FORWARD +Z
[facing] D  hand line      Lx=+1.1101 Rx=-1.1101  => agrees with C
[facing] E  head-hips z    +0.0309  => leans +Z  (agrees with C)
```

Reading C is the load-bearing one: in a right-handed basis with +Y up, `right = forward × up`,
so a **left** shoulder at **+X** puts **forward on +Z**. D and E corroborate independently.

**A fourth reading died and is reported dead, not dropped** (L-N discipline): toe-minus-ankle is
the least ambiguous humanoid forward there is, but **this bone map has no `LeftToes`/`RightToes`**
(`find_bone` → −1). With the toe silently at `Vector3.ZERO` the subtraction degenerates to
*minus the ankle position* and returns a perfectly mirrored ±73.79° pair — the signature of the
artefact. Printed in the probe, marked `*`, excluded from the verdict.

### §1.3 The cause

The werewolf is a **+Z-forward Synty rig**. This repo already knew that —
`scripts/render_arena_room.gd:524` carries the verbatim comment *"Synty rigs face +Z"* and yaws
with `atan2(dir.x, dir.z)` and **no** `+180`.

All three race rigs contradicted it. Every one of them yawed with Godot's **−Z**-forward
convention:

| site | line |
|---|---|
| `vh_brief_aura.gd:99` | `rotation_degrees = Vector3(0, rad_to_deg(atan2(AXIS.x, AXIS.z)) + 180.0, 0)` |
| `vh_brief_cast.gd:142` | same form, on the aim vector |
| `vpro_race_rig.gd:184` · `vmur_race_rig.gd:175,181` | same form |

The probe closes it arithmetically against the AURA travel axis:

```
[facing] race-rig(+180)   -> model-forward becomes (-0.682, 0, 0.732)  dot(travel)=-1.0005  *** BACKWARDS ***
[facing] synty(+0)        -> model-forward becomes (0.682, 0, -0.732)  dot(travel)=+1.0005  FORWARD
```

**One 180° error explains BOTH symptoms in ALL THREE arms simultaneously** — which is exactly
why the defect was symmetric across the strips and why gandalf was right that no arm was
advantaged (§13). Facing backwards and walking backwards are not two bugs; they are the same
bug seen standing still and seen moving.

### §1.4 The fix, and why it is at the body

`scripts/vh_caster.gd` — `MODEL_FORWARD_YAW := 180.0`, applied to the `Body` node after seating:

```gdscript
body.rotation_degrees.y = MODEL_FORWARD_YAW
```

Fixed at the **body shell**, not at the five call sites, because there it is fixed **once for
every present and future consumer**: after this rotation the caster rig's −Z *is* the werewolf's
visual forward, so the standard Godot convention — which is what all three race rigs already
write — becomes correct instead of inverted. `vpro_race_rig.gd` and `vmur_race_rig.gd` both
`preload("res://scripts/vh_caster.gd")`, so **all three arms inherit the fix with zero edits to
arm-owned code** (which also means I have not touched another arm's authored surface).
Applied after the y-seat and after the height scale, both of which are yaw-invariant.

### §1.5 ★ THE VERIFICATION IS AN EYE, BECAUSE THE DEFECT CLASS IS "METRIC GREEN, EYE RED"

The R-6 judge camera is −50° pitch at 67 px of caster. Matt could read the defect there because
he watched **motion**; a **still** at that framing is a poor instrument for the *fix*, because a
right fix and a wrong fix look nearly identical at 67 px. So the fix got a purpose-built witness:

**`scripts/wr1_facing_witness.gd`** — near-ground camera, perpendicular to the AURA brief's exact
travel axis and speed, with the travel direction **physically marked in frame**: a **green** post
at `+AXIS·5` (walking TO) and a **red** post at `−AXIS·5` (came FROM). The reading convention is
**asserted by the rig, not by a comment** — it unprojects both posts and prints their screen x:

```
[wr1_wit] GREEN(travel-to) screen_x=1166.0 | RED(came-from) screen_x=114.0 => travel is screen-RIGHT
```

There is exactly one way to read the resulting frame: **is his face toward the green post?**

## §3 — FIX A3: the AURA floor ring must FOLLOW the werewolf

> **Matt (AURA verdict):** *"A wins, but the floor portion of the aura doesn't follow the
> werewolf."*

### §3.1 Reproduced first, at a frame chosen so the bug CAN show

The AURA clip walks him from `−AXIS·3.2` to `+AXIS·3.2` between t = 1.2 s and t = 5.2 s. At
t = 3.2 s (the settle the race used) he is **exactly at the origin** — where the floor emitters
sit — so the defect is invisible at that instant. Rendered instead at **t = 4.5 s** (settle 135),
where he is 2.08 m along the axis: `tmp/wr1/a3_before_small.png` shows him at frame-right inside
his green light pool, with **the floor ember/ring mass stranded back at the room centre**. Matt's
note, reproduced exactly.

*A defect that hides at the frame you happened to pick is not a defect that is absent. Pick the
frame that can show it.*

### §3.2 The bug is one line wide, and the header was already right

`local_coords = false` was the **correct** authoring choice and is unchanged — it is what sheds
the particles into world space so the walk leaves a wake. What was missing: the two emitter
**nodes** were `add_child`-ed to the brief at the origin and **then never moved**. The header's
stated intent ("emitted into WORLD space and LEFT BEHIND") was half-implemented — the particles
were being left behind by a source that had never gone anywhere.

Fixed in `_process`, not by reparenting:

```gdscript
_ring.global_position = _caster.global_position
_embers.global_position = _caster.global_position + Vector3(0.0, 0.22, 0.0)
```

**Reparenting to the caster would have been the wrong fix** — it would carry the shed embers
along with him and destroy the wake, trading Matt's note for a worse one. Emitter follows,
particle does not: that is the only combination that delivers both halves of "survives motion" —
the source is *attached* (it is his aura) and the emission is *displaced* (it is a wake). Offsets
are the ones the systems were authored with, so this restores intended geometry and re-tunes
nothing.

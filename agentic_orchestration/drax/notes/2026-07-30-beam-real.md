Apex-foot cannot survive a beam that does not touch the floor — there is no foot to overlap with
anything, so **IoU is the wrong instrument for the new law**. Pool-axis asks instead: is the gap a
FADE, or a DISPLACEMENT?

**(a) GEOMETRIC, in the room, printed on every build.** Walk each pattern's `SpotLight3D`, take its
REAL global axis, intersect it with the floor, compare to the pool it was asked for:

```
BEAM-REAL pool-axis: SkyCircle axis foot (-3.3045, -5.1226)  pool (-3.3045, -5.1226)  ERROR 0.000000 m
BEAM-REAL pool-axis: SkySlats  axis foot (+1.8978, +6.7307)  pool (+1.8978, +6.7307)  ERROR 0.000001 m
```

**(b) IMAGE-SPACE, on the isolated rig** (`tmp/beamreal/axis_cal.gd`: one floor, one lamp, no room,
no second pattern, vertical, orthographic overhead — the SKY-2 lesson about two skylights, carried).

⚑ **The first formulation of this test was wrong and its own number said so.** It compared the two
centroids and called the 1.76 m between them an error. It is not an error: with the QUADRANT mask
the lit blob sits off-axis, and shrinking an off-axis blob **about the axis** is *supposed* to move
its centroid. The law does not claim the centroids coincide — it claims the beam is the pool
**scaled about the axis**. So the test recovers the scale and the centre from the image:

| mask | recovered scale (vs `beam_narrow` 0.300) | recovered centre vs BUILT axis | containment |
|---|---|---|---|
| quadrant (convention arm) | **0.3035** (+1.15 %) | **0.0228 m** | 0.49 (meaningless for an off-axis blob) |
| **oculus — the room's own shipped motif** | **0.2845** (−5.15 %) | **0.0035 m** | **0.9997** |

**The narrowed shaft is a concentric miniature of its own pool, containment 0.9997, its axis
0.0035 m from where it was built.** The gap is a fade. `plates/PLATE_pool_axis.png` shows it: the
pool's full oculus ring in red, the shaft's ×0.30 copy of the same ring in blue, dead centre.

**Parallax — the one-transform-chain law is UNCHANGED, and it is now a claim about three nodes:**

| observer | slide | circle pool foot | shaft centre | dust centre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (0,0) | (0,0,0) | (−3.3045, −5.1225) | (−5.3389, 6.7000, −2.9409) | **identical** |

point 6.7 m up the axis translates *and* rotates. The axis still passes through the foot — which is

| | claim | result |

bit-identical. They are, over the whole frame, in all three channels.

⚑ **The instrument's positive control is A and B sharing one code path.** A returns 0 using exactly
the arithmetic that returns 14,833 for B in the same frame at the same camera — so the zero is
earned, not asserted. (SKY-2 had to build a separate unbounded-fog control for this; here the
authorised overlay is the control.)

## §4 — Three things I built that were wrong, and how each was caught

### 4.1 ⚑ THE SHADER RECONSTRUCTED A PERSPECTIVE RAY UNDER AN ORTHOGRAPHIC CAMERA

SKY-2's shaft wrote `dir = worldpos − INV_VIEW_MATRIX[3].xyz`, i.e. it assumed every view ray
radiates from one point. True of a perspective camera; **false of an orthographic one, where the
rays are parallel and the camera node's position is not on any of them.** BEAM-REAL's gap law is a
height, and the only honest way to read a height off a frame is an ortho camera — at which point
the shader was integrating a perspective ray and rasterising it at an orthographic pixel.

**It was caught by arithmetic, not by looking:** the instrument reported beam **1,350 px below its
own hard `base_y` cut-off**, which the shader cannot write. Fixed by building the ray from
`PROJECTION_MATRIX[3][3]` (0 = perspective, 1 = ortho) — two lines each for origin, direction,
`t_far` and the depth-clamp conversion.

⚑ **Proven not to move what the game renders, because I measured it instead of reasoning about
it:** the perspective branch is algebraically the old code, and the eye-camera render across the
change differs on **137 px of 1,080,000 (0.013 %), max channel delta 1** — floating-point residue
from normalising in view space rather than world space.

⚑ **INHERITED CAVEAT, ROUTED NOT BURIED:** SKY-2's coupling IoU of **0.963 was measured through
this defect** — `mask_cal` is an overhead ORTHOGRAPHIC rig. That verdict survives (a thin slab makes
the error small, and "which quadrant" is robust to it), and this cell re-measures the convention
independently and agrees. But the 0.963 itself is a number taken through a mis-integrated volume
and should not be re-quoted as a precision figure.

### 4.2 ⚑ MY OWN REFERENCE FRAME SILENTLY RE-AIMED ITS CAMERA

Every probe camera aims at the pool, found by walking the built tree for a `SpotLight3D`. Under
`--nosky` there is no skylight spot, the search fell through to the room origin, and the camera
**re-framed about 6 m away**. Every "pool" statistic subtracted against that frame was measuring
the parallax of floor tiles.

**Caught by an impossibility:** it produced **400,409 NEGATIVE pixels** in what is by construction
an additive contribution (min −171.67). Fixed with `--aim x,z`, a peel-independent pin, now
mandatory for any paired render across a peel that removes the skylight. **Everything in §1.4 and
§1.3's pool figures was re-measured after the fix**; the first ladder I ran is discarded, and its
pool reference (peak 254.13, saturated) was wrong by a factor of five.

### 4.3 ⚑ THE VOID MASK MEASURED ITS OWN EDGE

The raw void mask (`sky-off ≤ 1 luma`) includes the **antialiased rim of the room's own silhouette**
— pixels that are part void and part wall. Lighting the wall moves them by one 8-bit level and the
instrument reports a leak. It reported **97 px, max 0.715 luma (exactly one green level), 100 % of
them within 2 px of the silhouette**. Eroding the mask by 2 px removes the boundary and the leak
goes to a true 0. Reported because "0 px" arrived at by tightening a mask is a different fact from
"0 px" measured first time, and the reader should get the one that happened.

### 4.4 A necessity the new geometry created, not a tidy-up

SKY-2 clipped its 32-step march to the floor-to-ceiling SLAB — 3 m tall, 7 m wide, a decent proxy
for the beam. The BEAM-REAL shaft is ~1 m across inside a slab ~8.6 m tall, so a near-horizontal
ray crosses the slab for tens of metres and **one or two of its 32 samples would land inside the
beam**: the shaft would alias into a flickering dotted smear. The march is now bounded by an
**analytic ray/cone intersection against the narrowed cone**, so all 32 steps are spent inside the
volume being integrated. The accumulated path is still exactly the chord, so SKY-2's "reaches zero
at its own silhouette by arithmetic" is untouched. The proxy cylinder shrank with it (fitted to the
narrowed cone between the two termini, ~11× fewer rasterised fragments).

## §2 — GATE G-26b: the opacity-vs-height profile

**Tolerance, named before the measurement:** opacity at the floor ≥ 0.50 of the arm's own peak;
opacity monotone-decreasing above the profile's peak; opacity at the frame-top world height
(13.17 m) ≤ 0.05 of peak; and **the 75 %-span value at least 1.5× its BEFORE value** (that is
"carried higher", made into a number).

### 2.1 ⚑ THE CONSTANT WAS NOT WHAT WAS FALLING

The declared world factor is `(1-u)^grad_pow`. At the shipped 1.35 it reads
**1.00 / 0.67 / 0.39 / 0.15 / 0.00** — which is not "near-instant" by any reading. What Matt's
eye receives is that factor multiplied by the beam's **optical depth**, and

```
v = (1 - exp(-acc * density))^edge_power ,   acc = chord * mask * weights
```

At `density` = 0.34 the exponential is nowhere near saturation, so `v` is very nearly **linear
in the chord** — and the chord *is* the cone's width, which falls to zero at the apex.
**The vertical falloff was being authored by the geometry.** Declaring a curve and measuring a
curve are two different acts, and this cell exists because the first one was done twice.

`density` was also **never a level constant at all** until now: the material never wrote it, so
the single number deciding whether the gradient or the taper owns the falloff was living on the
shader's own default — unreported, unpeelable, and outside the constant block every other beam
operand lives in.

### 2.2 The instrument, and the two things wrong with the first version of it

`side` orthographic + horizontal, so a **pixel row IS a world height** (24.00 px/m over a 30 m
frame, frame bottom exactly y = 0). Sampling heights come from the **probe**, not from python:
`[bp] GRADGRID` prints 21 points along each shaft's own axis, projected through the actual
`Camera3D` — because at CAM-LOCK (a pinhole, per GAL-CAM) no row is a height, and an instrument
that does its own projection is an instrument that can be silently wrong about it.

Two corrections, both caught by disagreeing with the build's own arithmetic:

1. **THE FLOOR SAMPLE FELL ONE PIXEL OFF THE BOTTOM OF THE IMAGE.** y = 0 projects to row 720
   of a 720-row frame. The unclamped version returned 0.0 there, and the **entire first density
   ladder — seven rungs — reported "0.000 at the floor" on a build whose own report line said
   PINNED.** A measurement that contradicts the build's arithmetic is a measurement to check first.
2. **THE LAMP HAD TO BE PEELED, AND THAT IS NOT TIDYING.** The beam is `blend_add` and its own
   pool runs at luma **253/255** in the same pixels. With the lamp on, `FULL − NOBEAM` near the
   floor measures 255 − 253 = 2: the brightest part of the beam reports as the dimmest, through
   an 8-bit ceiling its own pool has already reached. `--nolamp` keeps the beam meshes and drops
   the spots, which is exactly what that peel exists for.

A third suspect was **refuted rather than assumed**: I built a `--nowalls` peel on the theory
that the side camera's sub-3.006 m rows were measuring masonry. It moved the floor value by
**0.000**. The step at 3.006 m was the fog, not the wall (§2.5). The peel is kept because it
cost ten lines and it now rules that explanation out permanently.

Statistic: **mean of the top 20 pixels in the row**, not the row max. GPUParticles3D is unseeded
(BEAM-CONE measured two identical launches 0.081 apart); on the max statistic the pow ladder
returned peaks of 98.77 and 90.70 for the same constants while the p99 sat at 87.56 both times.

## §6 — ⚑ Five things I got wrong, and how each was caught

### 6.1 THE FLOOR SAMPLE FELL OFF THE BOTTOM OF THE IMAGE AND COST A SEVEN-RUNG LADDER
The `side` camera's frame bottom is *exactly* y = 0, so the floor point projects to row **720**
of a 720-row frame. The unclamped read returned 0.0, and the first density ladder reported
**"0.000 at the floor" for all seven rungs** — on a build whose own report line printed
**PINNED**. Caught by the instrument disagreeing with the build's arithmetic, which is the only
reason the build prints its arithmetic.

### 6.2 I MEASURED THE BEAM THROUGH AN 8-BIT CEILING ITS OWN POOL HAD ALREADY REACHED
With the lamp on, the beam is `blend_add` over a floor at luma 253/255, so `FULL − NOBEAM` near
the floor measures 2. The corrected ladder read 48 at the floor and 90 at 4.2 m — **the brightest
part of the beam reporting as the dimmest**, which is arithmetically impossible for a monotone
weight and is what gave it away. Fixed by `--nolamp`, the peel that already existed for exactly
this.

### 6.3 I BLAMED THE WALLS FOR THE 3 m STEP, BUILT A PEEL FOR IT, AND THE PEEL MOVED NOTHING
The `side` camera stands inside the room, so sub-wall-course rows plausibly look through masonry.
I wrote `--nowalls`, ran it, and the floor value moved by **0.000**. The step at 3.006 m was the
**fog box top**, which shares that height. The wrong hypothesis is on the record because the peel
that refuted it is now permanent.

### 6.4 THE ROW MAX WAS A DRAW, NOT A MEASUREMENT
The pow ladder returned peaks of **98.77 and 90.70 for identical constants** while the p99 sat at
87.56 both times — the row max was picking up an unseeded mote. Statistic changed to the mean of
the row's top 20 pixels; the floor value then held to **62.25–62.43 across the whole ladder**,
which is the stability the first version did not have.

### 6.5 ⚑ I SET A FLAG ON A `WR1Level` THAT DOES NOT DECLARE IT — FOR THE FOURTH TIME IN THIS RUN
`--beamconebeams` set `lvl.beamcone_legacy`, GDScript raised *"Invalid assignment of property
`beamcone_legacy`"* at runtime, the level never built, and the smoke render came back as the
bare-arena fallback. **BEAM-CONE §6.2 wrote this failure down and the warning is four paragraphs
above the line I forgot to add.** The only improvement is that this time it was caught on the
**first** smoke launch instead of after 600 rendered frames.

**And one I nearly did and did not:** I nearly named the G-26a pass-bar *after* seeing that the
slats made 100 % and the circle made 89.88 %. That is BEAM-CONE §6.5's exact failure. Instead the
image clause is graded by **improvement surviving every threshold and both attribution controls**,
with the ceiling measured (§1.3) — and the circle's shortfall is reported as a shortfall.

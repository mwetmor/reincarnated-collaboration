## §1 — The alpha/colour collision the brief warned about does not exist

The brief asked me to resolve a possible collision between the colour-ramp and alpha-ramp mechanisms "so BOTH survive and declare how." They cannot collide, because **they are not two mechanisms.** `ParticleProcessMaterial.color_ramp` is a `GradientTexture1D` over a `Gradient` whose `PackedColorArray` carries **R, G, B *and* A per key**. The "alpha ramp" is the fourth channel of the colour ramp. One mechanism, four channels.

So the resolution is exact rather than negotiated: RGB was rewritten in place, and **A and the offsets were copied through untouched.** Verified two independent ways — a field-by-field comparison against the pre-cell backup (offsets IDENTICAL, alphas IDENTICAL on all five gradients: `[0, .36, .24, 0]`, `[0, .46, .34, 0]`, `[0, .34, 0]`, `[0, .44, 0]`, `[0, .54, 0]`), and a line diff showing **exactly one changed line per file**. The 0 → visible → 0 no-pop guarantee is not "carefully preserved"; it was never touched.

No key was added and no offset moved. The arc is linear in `u` and the gradient already interpolates linearly in `u`, so extra keys would have bought nothing and would have meant inventing alpha values.

## §2 — Hue was moved OUT of `color`, and that answers the L5 question structurally

Emitted colour is `color` (a Color multiplier) × `color_ramp` (per-age). **Both were warm**, so the hue was a product of two warm terms and neither owned it. `color` is now a **neutral GREY at the layer's existing Rec.709 luma**, and the ramp owns the hue outright.

The payoff is L5. `Color(0.62, 0.4092, 0.1984)` is exactly the unquieted haze hue `(1.0, 0.66, 0.32) × 0.62` — AMB-REFIT's quieting, entangled in a chroma. So the brief's question — should L5's purple be picked at matched luma rather than matched chroma? — **is answered by construction rather than by preference: L5's purple IS matched-luma, and it is the SAME purple as every other layer.** The ×0.62 lives entirely in the grey.

## §3 — ⚑ THE FIRST PURPLE SHIPPED PINK, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS THE ONLY REASON IT DIDN'T STAY

Revision 1 was authored at linear hue **254–282°**, holding the alpha-weighted **emitted** luma integral to ±0.0000% per layer. The arithmetic was clean. It was also wrong, and only a render said so.

**Measured (AMB-HUE-1, new instrument): displayed hue 317–347° — MAGENTA — with saturation collapsed to 0.37 against the warm build's 0.855.** 50% of particle pixels landed in the magenta bin and 32% in red. Zero in violet.

The instrument was not allowed to make that claim unchallenged. **Three hypotheses were raised and two were killed by their own controls:**

1. *"sRGB-space subtraction exaggerates blue over a warm base."* Killed: the warm control returned **24.9°** against an authored ~25°, and linear mode returned 11.8°. Neither carried a blue bias.
2. *"the warm base contaminates the delta."* Killed: restricting to near-black base moved the number from 237.9° to **237.8°**.
3. The actual error, which was mine and conceptual: **I was measuring the hue of the DELTA. Matt's eye receives the DISPLAYED PIXEL.** A monitor emits what is in the framebuffer, not the difference between two renders. Re-pointed at the displayed pixel, the warm control returned **21.9°, concentration 1.000, 99.2% red** — a bullseye on its authored hue — and the purple returned **347°**. The mode was validated by its control before its result was believed.

**THE CAUSE IS A CEILING, NOT A ROTATION.** `kit_replica_level.gd:481` (FROZEN, read-only) sets `TONE_MAPPER_FILMIC`. Godot's filmic is the Hable curve applied **per channel**, and it saturates:

```
author 0.40 -> display 0.597     author 1.20 -> display 1.000
author 0.80 -> display 0.903     author 2.40 -> display 1.000
```

Rev 1's ramp reached **B = 2.6**. At height all three channels cleared the knee, so the "lighter purple-blue" rendered as **literal white**; the pink is what remains lower down, where B pins at 1.0 while G is still on the linear part and climbs past it. **Authoring brightness past the knee does not buy brightness — it only spends the colour.**

**So the invariant was wrong, and instructively so.** "Emitted luma" is not what Matt ruled on and not what AMB-EVEN measures — both are **displayed** luma. Holding emitted luma is precisely what drove the values past the knee. Revision 3 specifies hue, saturation and value in **DISPLAY space** and **inverts the filmic curve per channel** to get what to author. (A single hue offset could never have worked: the rotation depends on magnitude, so it differs at every point along the ramp.)

## §4 — What shipped, and the constraint that is physics rather than taste

Display-space spine, identical for all five layers so the room reads as **one** gradient rather than five effects:

| | at birth (floor) | at death (height) |
|---|---|---|
| hue | **284°** violet | **256°** blue-violet |
| saturation | **0.86** deep | **0.52** paler |
| display value | **0.30** dark | **0.95** light |

**Measured at the eye camera (`door:3`, 1.7 m — the camera the rise verdict was made at): displayed hue 274.0°, 68% VIOLET / 32% blue, ZERO magenta, ZERO red, saturation 0.570, hue concentration 0.995.** At the judge cam: 298.9°, 68% violet / 32% purple, again zero magenta. Rev 1 → rev 3 moved the room from *pink* to *purple*, and the number says so as plainly as the plate does.

**⚑ DECLARED, NOT TUNED AWAY — a saturated purple cannot be as bright as an orange, and no authoring can fix it.** Rec.709 luma is `0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B`: **blue carries 7.2% of luminance.** Orange spends R and G, the two heavy coefficients, and is close to the most luma-efficient hue on the wheel; a saturated violet is close to the least. Matching the warm build's luma would drive B past 1.8 and the only way the request can be granted is by desaturating toward white — **which is exactly the failure rev 1 shipped.** Revision 2 tried to hold displayed luma and was capped by the curve on four of five layers; that cap is what surfaced the constraint.

So the ambient **is dimmer**, by directive rather than by defect — Matt asked for *dark* purple at the floor. The size of it is measured in §5 and routed to his eye.

**Per-layer judgment, declared as always:**

- **L6_Embers — the read changes and I am not pretending otherwise.** Under purple these stop being fire-born. They are **arcane motes**: same sprites, same 1.60 m rise, same count, but they no longer read as sparks off a torch. This is the layer the recolour costs the most narratively and buys the most compositionally, because it was the layer most easily mistaken for the frozen torch embers. It now cannot be.

## §1 — The lock, and why there is no smoothing coefficient anywhere in it

`--cam player_lock`. The camera's world transform is:

```
basis    = fixed at build, never recomputed
position = <player's ground position this frame> + <constant offset vector>
```

evaluated at the END of `_render_tick`, i.e. **after** the player proxy has been
placed at its interpolated position for that frame, **on that exact position**.
There is no spring, no lag term, no dead zone, no look-ahead, and no smoothing
of any kind — D2/D3/D4, PoE 1/2, Grim Dawn, Last Epoch grammar.

That is not a stylistic preference and the reason is worth writing down: threat
assessment in this genre is **radial** ("what is inside 8 m of me") and aim is
**screen-relative**. An eased camera makes the screen-space radius of a
world-space distance a function of the player's recent velocity — which is
exactly the quantity the player is reading off the screen. Easing does not soften
that surface; it makes it lie.

**⚑ The consequence is accepted, not mitigated.** If the trace's player position
jumps tick-to-tick, the camera jumps with it. The camera is not permitted an
opinion about the player's motion. Likewise, when the player leaves the frame's
entity list the lock simply stops updating and the camera **holds** — the genre
behaviour on death, and deliberate: a camera that drifted off a corpse would be
an authored opinion where the trace has none.

## §2 — The operands, and the one number that was DERIVED rather than copied

Every constant is GAL-CAM's, recovered from Matt's own 1h53m session. **The old
orthographic assumption is gone** — the fixture camera is a **pinhole**, so this
is a perspective `Camera3D` with a real FOV, and the near/far asymmetry of the
decision surface is a *consequence* of that rather than a framing choice.

| operand | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| pitch below horizontal | **52.95354°** | `godot-spec.json` `pitch_deg[2]` |
| vertical FOV (Godot `fov`, KEEP_HEIGHT) | **31.78610°** | `fov_v_deg[2]` |
| player anchor | **(962, 595)/1080p → fraction (0.501042, 0.550926)** | note § 1 row 4 |
| bearing (yaw) | **47°** — **NOT measured; declared** | = `CAM_YAW_DEG`, the judge camera's own |

GAL-CAM § 6 says explicitly that **FOV and camera distance are a COUPLED PAIR**
that trade along the horizon-row uncertainty, and that what matters is the pair
reproducing the measured **scale field**. So the free parameter was fixed from the
scale, not copied from a distance:

```
focal_px(1080) = (1080/2) / tan(fov_v/2)  = 1896.5577
z_player       = focal_px / g_x_player    = 1896.5577 / 54.47290 = 34.81653 m
```

**The cross-check is real, because nothing here was fitted to make it land.** That
one derived depth reproduces three of GAL-CAM's *independently published* rig
numbers, none of which were used as inputs:

| GAL-CAM published | this rig derives | Δ |
|---|---|---|
| camera → look-at 35.5914 m | 35.5789 m | 0.04 % |
| player lateral ground offset +0.0367188 m | +0.0367155 m | 0.01 % |
| player along-ground offset −1.26547 m | −1.26503 m | 0.04 % |

Resulting rig: **offset (camera − player ground) = (14.7262, 28.3970, 13.7826) m**,
|offset| 34.8312 m, camera height 28.3970 m.

**Bearing is declared, not measured, and that is a CANNOT-ANSWER not a shortcut.**
Grim Dawn's world is authored *to* its fixed camera; the bearing is a property of
that world, so there is nothing in the footage to recover. It therefore takes the
judge camera's 47° byte-identically, so that a viewer flipping between the two
watches sees the same room in the same orientation and every difference he sees is
**framing**. A second rotation would have made the split illegible.

## §6 — ⚑ THE CAPTIONS WERE 2.13× TOO BIG, AND HOLDING THEM STILL WOULD HAVE BEEN THE CHANGE

The first full render came back with **"WIND-UP" spanning 21 % of the frame,
sitting on top of the boss it was annotating**, and the WR3 strike verdict
spanning **100 % of the frame width**, 53 times in this fight, across the boss it
indicts.

Every one of those layers carries its own authoring comment saying it was **sized
on a captured frame at `arena_full`**, with measured pixel numbers: ~22 px of label
box, ~15.5 px of cap height, a 24 × 6 px chip, at "~17 px per world metre". **The
accepted property is PIXELS.** The metres are only how the pixels were reached.

`player_lock` stands 34.8 m off at 31.79° instead of 58 m at 40°, which is
**36.32 px/m against 17.05**. So leaving the metres alone *changes* the accepted
property by 2.13×; holding the pixels requires moving the metres. The factor is
derived from the two cameras, not chosen:

```
px/m = (viewport_h/2) / (distance · tan(fov_v/2))
arena_full : 360 / (58.000 · tan 20.000°) = 17.05 px/m
player_lock: 360 / (34.816 · tan 15.893°) = 36.32 px/m
k = 17.05 / 36.32 = 0.46959
```

and **17.05 is the same "~17 px/m" the layer records as MEASURED on a frame** —
the cross-check that this arithmetic describes the lens that was actually
accepted, rather than a lens invented to justify a resize.

**The line was drawn once and written into the source, because it is the load-bearing part:**

- **CAPTIONS scale by k** — the AI-state tag stack, `_float_number`'s damage
  floaters, the telegraph "announced" pip, the HIT/WHIFF verdict. Their accepted
  property is pixels; held in pixels.
- **GEOMETRY DOES NOT** — capsules at true `entity_radius_m`, the 0.750 m shell
  band, the commit disc, the reach ring, telegraph circles, HP bars. Those are 1:1
  **depictions** of sim quantities (R-WR1-20). Shrinking them to taste under a
  closer camera would make the render lie about the fight. They get bigger because
  the camera came closer, which is what perspective is for.

The tag stack is scaled **about its own bottom edge** (capsule top + 0.74 m) —
`rise' = 0.74 + (rise − 0.74)·k` — one uniform scale, so every internal relation in
the layout table survives and no gap is re-derived by hand. The pivot is not
cosmetic: scaling about the capsule top instead pushes the focus label's box down
to 0.35–0.82 m, **through** the HP bar at 0.40–0.70.

**⚑ ROUTED TO MATT'S EYE, NOT RULED HERE.** The layer's own discipline is "sized on
a frame, not from arithmetic". This *is* arithmetic, and it is only defensible
because it holds a measured screen size rather than picking a new one.
`plates/PLATE_tags_worldheld_vs_screenheld.jpg` puts both readings side by side;
the first render is kept as `_intermediate_tagsworld_NOT_SHIPPED.mp4` rather than
deleted. **Reversal is one constant: force `_caption_k = 1.0`.**

## §12 — Mirror

The camera was the last thing in this render that was still a guess. Every number
in the fight had been measured — reach, separation, commit windows, the whiff
ledger — and then all of it was being shown through a lens somebody picked because
it looked right.

It does not look right any more. It looks like his screen. And the first thing
that fell out of building it honestly is that the ring he has to dodge does not
fit on it — which was true in Grim Dawn too, and nobody had noticed, because
nobody had measured the frame.

## §4 — TWO SEMANTIC SHIFTS, DECLARED (Discipline #12)

Neither is a bug fix. Both change what an existing field name **means** once a second family
populates it, and burying either would hand a renderer a wrong closed form. Both are declared in
the source at the point a consumer reads the field, in `MIGRATION.md`, and in the commit message.

### §4.1 — `projectile_velocity_ms` no longer has one axis

| family | axis | renderable form |
|---|---|---|
| `nova` | **radial**, outward from origin, in the arena plane | unchanged: `rho(t) = v*(t - t_launch_s)`, prongs at `spoke_offset_rad + k*2π/N` |
| `wave` | **forward**, along `orientation_rad`, in the arena plane | front advances from origin, capped at `range_m` (16.0). **Sweep duration = `range_m / v` = 1.4 s exactly** |
| `blizzard` | **downward** — along an axis the header's `frame` block **does not declare** (`x_axis: east(+)`, `y_axis: north(+)`, no vertical) | orbs fall `wind_up_s × v` = **20.0 m** and land at `fire_t_s` |

**A consumer must branch on `family` before reading this field.** Applying the nova's radial form to
a blizzard draws an expanding ground circle — a *different* lie from the static one it replaced, not
a smaller one.

**The wave is now fully renderable from the trace:** origin, `orientation_rad`, `range_m 16.0`,
`width_m 6.0`, release instant `fire_t_s`, front speed. The 1.4 s sweep falls out of `range_m / v`
without needing `wave_time_s` in the schema.

### §4.2 — `prong_count` is PER LAUNCH EVENT, not per cast

MIGRATION §1a names it "the **LAUNCHED** count" — unambiguous while only the nova populated it,
because a nova cast is **one** launch event (16 launched, 16 per cast, same integer). **A blizzard
cast is FOUR launch events of 6 = 24 projectiles**, and the emitted value is **6**, per census rec 2
and the conductor's pin.

After this change the field means *the number of projectiles in one launch event.* **The number of
launch events per cast is a separate quantity the schema does not carry** — which leads directly to:

## §5 — ⚑ THE FINDING THE CONDUCTOR SHOULD RULE ON: G-2c IS NOT SATISFIABLE FROM THE TRACE

**This fill does not make the blizzard's rhythm renderable, and I want that said plainly rather
than discovered by drax in cell 2.**

After this cell a blizzard telegraph carries: `origin`, `radius_m 8.0`, `wind_up_s 0.8333`,
`fire_t_s`, `projectile_velocity_ms 24.0`, `prong_count 6`. It does **not** carry:

| datum | value in `BlizzardParams` | in the trace? |
|---|---|---|
| `n_volleys` | **4** | **no** |
| `volley_interval_s` | **2.0** | **no** |
| `active_duration_s` | **8.0** | **no** |

So a renderer reading the telegraph can honestly draw **one volley of 6 falling orbs over 0.8333 s**
and has **no trace-sourced basis** for the other 18 drops, the 2.0 s cadence, or the 8.0 s window —
while `radius_m 8.0` keeps representing all of it. Charter §2 T-1 item 3 asks for **24 orbs in 4
volleys 2.0 s apart across 8.0 s**, G-2c grades it, and **R-BR-24 forbids the renderer inventing
motion the trace does not declare.** Those three requirements cannot all hold as things stand.

**Two trace-sourced routes exist and both have a named defect:**

- **(a) parse `attack_id`.** Blizzard `damage` events carry the `:<volley 0-3>:<drop 0-5>` tail with
  a real `t_s`, so cadence is recoverable — **but only for drops that HIT.** A volley where nothing
  connects leaves no record at all, so the rhythm would flicker seed-to-seed. It also reintroduces
  exactly the string-parsing the census flagged as the defect.
- **(b) declare 4 / 2.0 / 8.0 as consumer-side constants**, labelled declared-not-sourced on frame
  (the charter's own G-1-FAIL fallback vocabulary, applied to a different quantity).

**My recommendation, and it is cheap: open a cell 1b to add `duration_s`, `stage_count` and
`stage_interval_s` as three nullable telegraph fields** — census recs 3 and 4. They are `None` on
every family that does not declare one (the `prong_count` precedent, R-WR3-25(10)), they cost one
MIGRATION entry, and I can land + re-verify them in well under an hour given the instruments now
exist. Rec 3 also closes the census's own "single biggest omission": **the nova lasts 0.857 s, the
wave 1.4 s, the blizzard 8.0 s, and the schema says nothing** — which is the charter's own complaint
that the blizzard "reads on screen as the same thing" as the nova.

**I did NOT do it in this cell.** It is a schema change, TRACE-FILL-1 was pinned as a fill, and G-1
says "exactly the three added field values". Adding fields unilaterally would have made G-1
un-gradeable against its own pre-registration. **Conductor's call.**

## §6 — WHAT SURPRISED ME, AND MY OWN INSTRUMENT HAZARDS

### 6.1 — The `-dirty` stamp is a G-1 trap and I nearly walked into it

`header.engine_git_hash` is written by `kitcal_g5_harness._git_hash()`, which appends `-dirty` when
a **loaded engine module** is not in a commit. My first regeneration preview stamped
`ddbdebc8-dirty`. Had I regenerated the shipping battery from a dirty tree and then reported "G-1
PASS, one provenance field differs", the battery would have carried a hash naming **no commit at
all** — the C-4 defect, one lap on. The engine commit went in **first**, from a clean tracked tree,
and the shipping battery stamps `bce46fd9`, which is a real, pushed commit.

Worth flagging to the run generally: **any cell that regenerates traces must commit before it
generates, or it ships an unattributable battery.**

### 6.2 — My selectors could each have matched zero, and two nearly did

Applying the run's null-instrument tripwire to my own tools:

- **The diff instrument's `_label()`** keys records as `event[telegraph]` etc. If it had returned a
  wrong label the recursion would still have compared field-by-field and reported paths under the
  wrong prefix — a *plausible-looking* report that grouped nothing correctly. **Guarded by
  `record_order_identical`**, which compares the full label sequence between the two files.
- **The coverage instrument's family selector** is the one that would have failed silently. If
  `family` had been dropped at the emitter (which is exactly what R-WR3-40(2) records happening to
  this very field — `family` was minted upstream, rode the `TelegraphSpec`, and was dropped in the
  enumerated projection, 0 of 13,573 records), every family bucket would have keyed on `None` and
  the report would have printed "0 wave telegraphs, 0 violations". It now **raises `NullInstrument`**
  unless `n_files > 0`, `n_telegraphs > 0`, **and** each of `nova` / `wave` / `blizzard` is non-zero.
- **The test fixture `tg_by_family`** asserts all three families are non-empty before any `for` loop
  runs. Six §4 tests would otherwise have passed over empty lists.

**Both instruments were self-tested against a known answer before I trusted either:**
- The diff was run against a **deliberately mutated** copy (one tick's `hp` +1.0, one telegraph's
  `radius_m` → 999.0). It reported both, under the correct paths, and returned FAIL.
- The coverage instrument was run against the **pre-change banked battery** first. It returned
  **FAIL** on the three target gates (516 / 570 / 570 all null), **PASS** on the two nova controls,
  and reproduced legolas's census denominators exactly — 470 / 516 / 570 over 1,556 telegraphs.
  An instrument that cannot produce a negative has not produced a positive.

### 6.3 — Two smaller surprises

- **The census's `11.4286` is not the emitted value.** The float is `11.428571428571429`. A
  downstream equality check against the rounded figure fails. Named in the math note and the tests.
- **The blizzard telegraph's `radius_m 8.0` is the scatter radius, not a danger circle.** Individual
  drops have a **1.32 m** hit radius (`explosion_radius 1.0 + referent body 0.32`), and that number
  is in `BlizzardParams.hit_radius_m` and **not in the trace either**. A renderer drawing the 8.0 m
  circle as the lethal zone overstates the danger by ~6× in area per drop. Not in this cell's scope;
  logging it as a BR-3 candidate.

### 6.4 — The full test suite has a pre-existing red baseline, and I checked rather than assumed

`pytest tests/` returns **10,024 passed / 60 failed / 21 errors** (20 m 53 s). I stashed the three
source edits and re-ran the 11 implicated files on a clean tree: **the same 60 failed / 21 errors**.
All are in `test_cycle12_layer4_convergence`, `test_cycle13_wave5_season_generation`,
`test_cycle12_layer6_t4_wireup`, `test_kit_space_*`, `test_foundation`,
`test_substrate_identity_loader` and three singletons — **generation, kit-space, foundation and
season-generation seams. Zero in `spatial_gauntlet`.** Pre-existing baseline, not a regression.
Flagging the count itself as a finding for whoever owns those seams; it is not mine to fix.

## 0. TL;DR

The 4 marginal-lineage recognition records authored 2026-05-23 (south_am, arctic, oceanic, mesoamerican) plus the n.am.indigenous record share **one cross-cutting substrate-tagging-discipline pattern** with empirical variants:

> **The `cultural_lineage_canonical` tag vocabulary in the current 89.8K-row substrate is conflating "weapon-making cultural tradition of origin" with "geographic region of origin or deployment" or "naming-allusion to an indigenous people."**

Of the 5 indigenous-rare-lineage cases (n.am + south_am + arctic + oceanic + mesoamerican), 4 show the geographic-vs-cultural tagging artifact:

| Lineage | Coverage gap? | Tagging artifact? | Lineage-vocab over-collapse? | Highest-leverage corrective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| south_american_indigenous | Severe (~2/290 cultural) | **Yes (Modern Argentine/Brazilian/Chilean military firearms)** | No (one family loosely) | Sub-carry 9.11-E re-tag |
| arctic_circumpolar | Severe (~0/56 cultural) | **Yes (Russian/Swedish/French missile systems)** | No (Sámi+Inuit+Yupik+Aleut+Chukchi loosely) | Sub-carry 9.11-E re-tag + 9.10-E expansion |
| oceanic | Severe (~6-8/39 cultural) | **Yes (Australian/NZ modern military hardware)** | **Yes (Polynesian + Aboriginal + Melanesian + Micronesian collapsed)** | Sub-carry 9.11-E vocab + re-tag + 9.10-E expansion |
| mesoamerican | Moderate (~12-15/83 cultural) | **Yes (Modern Mexican arms-industry Mendoza/Cabañas/Zaragoza)** | No (Aztec+Maya+Olmec+Mixtec+Zapotec loosely) | Sub-carry 9.11-E re-tag (highest reclamation potential) |
| north_american_indigenous | Severe (~0-1/29 cultural) | **No** (clean control — substrate just doesn't have items) | No | Substrate-expansion-only (carry 9.10-E) |

N.am.indigenous is the **clean-control case**: no coherent cluster home AND no tagging artifact AND no over-collapse — the substrate honestly contains no cultural-indigenous items, full stop. The fix is pure substrate-expansion.

The other 4 cases all show the tagging artifact. The fix involves **upstream tagging discipline (sub-carry 9.11-E)** as a prerequisite — re-tagging existing items separates "Modern Mexican military arms" from "Pre-Columbian Mesoamerican Aztec weapons" within what is currently one lineage tag, so that downstream substrate expansion targets the *correct gap* rather than amplifying both modes proportionally.

This meta-observation is load-bearing for Fate-genre faction-architecture downstream surfaces: **any "cultural-tradition emergence from substrate" mechanism (e.g., D10 Path C, faction-from-clustering-evidence) MUST audit the cluster-rep-list for cultural-tradition-of-origin vs geographic-origin-of-origin BEFORE inheriting the cluster as faction-substrate-evidence.** Otherwise Mexican Mendoza pistols become "Aztec Eagle Knight faction substrate" and Russian MANPADS become "Sámi reindeer-hunter faction substrate" — both clearly wrong and corrosive to the cultural-respect discipline.

## 1. The pattern, named

### 1.1 The collapse

The `cultural_lineage_canonical` field in `weapon_knowledge_entries` (per schema definition: `european`, `east_asian`, `south_asian`, `southeast_asian`, `middle_eastern`, `african`, `north_american_indigenous`, `mesoamerican`, `south_american_indigenous`, `arctic_circumpolar`, `oceanic`, `fantasy_generic`, `sci_fi_generic`, `cross_cultural`, `unknown`) is, in current substrate state, a multi-mode tag:

- **Mode A (intended): "weapon-making cultural tradition of origin."** Substrate examples: Tumi (south_am Pre-Columbian Andean ritual knife); macuahuitl (mesoamerican Aztec obsidian sword); Boomerang (oceanic Aboriginal Australian); katana (east_asian Japanese samurai tradition); Hummingbird Bloodletter (mesoamerican Aztec ceremonial).
- **Mode B (artifact): "geographic region of origin or deployment."** Substrate examples: FAMAE SAF tagged south_am (modern Chilean military arms); Russian 2S1 Gvozdika SPH tagged arctic (cold-weather-deployed Soviet artillery); Australian Canberra Class LHD tagged oceanic (modern naval); Mendoza C-1934 tagged mesoamerican (modern Mexican military arms).
- **Mode C (artifact): "naming-allusion to an indigenous people in a modern-context item."** Substrate examples: VBTP-MR Guarani APC tagged south_am (Brazilian APC named after Guarani people; not Guarani weapon-making); FX-05 Xiuhcoatl rifle tagged mesoamerican (Mexican rifle named after Aztec serpent-deity; not Aztec weapon-making).
- **Mode D (artifact): "cross-tagged metadata error."** Substrate examples: LAHAT Israeli missile tagged south_am (Israeli system tagged into Latin American lineage); Apache Matra French anti-runway missile tagged south_am; Nimr Hafeet UAE ambulance tagged arctic; SVT-40 Soviet rifle tagged mesoamerican.

Modes B + C + D are the "artifact" cluster. They share a structural feature: they capture metadata other than cultural-tradition-of-origin, while sharing the lineage-tag vocabulary with Mode A. The current substrate has Modes A-D coexisting under one tag per lineage.

### 1.2 What this means for clustering

When HDBSCAN clusters on substrate-derived axes (D1 features through D2 PCA through D3 stability gate at k=3), the geometry being clustered is "weapon-form + period + register + kind + wieldability + cultural-lineage-tag" (per Phase E-1 features.md). The cultural-lineage-tag contributes to axis-positioning. If a tag conflates Modes A and B (e.g., 287 south_am-tagged rows are 285 Mode-B modern military and 2 Mode-A Tumi), the *centroid* of the lineage is at "modern military firearms + Latin American geographic origin" in axis space, not at "Pre-Columbian indigenous tradition."

This means HDBSCAN's substrate-led cluster emergence for lineage-tagged sub-pools will find:
- **Coherent clusters at the lineage's centroid** (which is mostly Mode-B-content in 4 of 5 marginal cases)
- **Scattered absorption of Mode-A content** into adjacent cross-cultural / cross-lineage clusters wherever the cultural item's actual axis-position is closer to other cultural items than to its own lineage-tag's centroid

This is exactly what we observe in the 5 records:

- South_am Cluster 87 (94.4% pure, N=36) = lineage-centroid coherence → Mode B/C/D content (Chilean/Brazilian/Israeli modern military)
- South_am Cluster 105 passenger = Mode-A content (Tumi) ride-along with east_asian-dominant cluster
- Arctic Cluster 24 (88.2% pure, N=34) = lineage-centroid coherence → Mode B (Russian/Swedish/French missile systems + cold-weather equipment + cross-tagged UAE)
- Oceanic Cluster 69 passenger absorption = lineage-centroid in cross-cultural mixed-munitions pool (Australian/NZ modern hardware)
- Oceanic Mode-A scatter (Boomerang, Spear Thrower, Club, Kaumaile) = passengers across 5 other clusters
- Mesoamerican Cluster 112 passenger (16.5% within-cluster) = mixed Mode A/B/C/D content with axis-pull from modern Mexican arms
- Mesoamerican Mode-A scatter (macuahuitl variants, Hummingbird Bloodletters, Macehead, Tlaximaltepoztli) = passengers across 5 clusters

The substrate-led clustering is doing exactly what it's supposed to do. The geometry it sees IS the mixed-mode tagging.

### 1.3 Why this is universal across the 4 cases (and asymmetric on n.am)

The 4 cases (south_am, arctic, oceanic, mesoamerican) share these substrate-historical features:

- Their geographic regions had **active modern arms-industries** during substrate-acquisition (Argentina/Brazil/Chile manufacture firearms and military vehicles; Russia/Sweden/France manufacture northern-deployed missile systems; Australia/NZ manufacture modern naval and unmanned defence systems; Mexico manufactures Mendoza/Cabañas firearms)
- Their substrate-acquisition crawls (per `weapon-library-import-wind-down-summary-2026-05-22.md` 89,839-row state) included modern military hardware catalogs
- Their `cultural_lineage_canonical` tagging passes used **geographic-origin-as-cultural-lineage** as a default heuristic when explicit cultural-tradition-of-origin metadata was absent

N.am.indigenous is asymmetric because:
- **No active modern arms-industry by "North American Indigenous" identifying parties.** Items manufactured in the USA are tagged `european` (per the Cluster 69 "Colt CM901 American" example documented in the n.am record § 1.3). The substrate-acquisition crawls did not pick up "Made in Cherokee Nation" or "Made in Navajo Nation" modern military hardware because such hardware does not exist at industrial scale.
- **No naming-allusion items in the substrate.** "Apache helicopter" might be tagged `european` (US military hardware) rather than `north_american_indigenous`; the substrate-curation pass did not allusion-tag.
- The substrate-acquisition therefore did not pull Mode B/C/D content into the `north_american_indigenous` tag bin.
- Result: the 29 n.am.indigenous-tagged rows are Mode-A-only (genuine cultural-tradition items if any) or absent entirely — the record found ~0-1 cultural items, suggesting Mode-A is itself sparse.

N.am.indigenous's asymmetry is a structural feature of US/Canadian arms-industry naming conventions — the lineage-tag was not cross-contaminated because the substrate-acquisition did not have geographic-tag-as-cultural-tradition signals to confuse it with. This makes n.am the **clean control** for the geographic-vs-cultural tagging hypothesis: when the cross-contamination mode is absent, the substrate-honest finding is pure substrate-coverage gap (no cluster, no cultural content).

The 4 affected cases confirm the hypothesis: their substrate-acquisition pulled Mode-B/C/D content into the lineage tag, producing clusters that look like substrate-led wins (high purity isolates) but are actually substrate-tagging-discipline failures dressed up as substrate-led wins.

## 2. Implications for downstream design surfaces

### 2.1 Fate-genre faction architecture (D10 Path C)

Per `canonical/story/fate-genre-recognition-and-mobile-alignment-trajectory-2026-05-23.md`, the substrate-emergent faction architecture is gated on "factions emerge from substrate cultural-mythological clustering." This meta-record adds a **substrate-tagging-discipline prerequisite**:

> **A substrate-led cluster passing as "cultural-tradition X faction substrate" requires rep-audit AT EACH FIRING. A cluster whose top-N reps are Mode B/C/D content (modern military, naming-allusion, cross-tagged metadata) is NOT cultural-tradition faction substrate, regardless of lineage-purity score.**

Cluster 87 ("S. American Indigenous Contemporary Shotgun Cluster") at 94.4% purity looks like a wonderful substrate-led win for an "Andean / Mapuche / Quechua faction." It is not — it is a modern Chilean / Brazilian military firearms cluster. The Fate-genre faction-architecture trajectory must include the rep-audit step or it will produce culturally-disrespectful and historically-inaccurate factions.

### 2.2 Phase E-3 / Spirit Form / cluster-as-design-surface

When Phase E-3 or any future Phase reads cluster labels and uses them as design seeds (e.g., "Spirit Form parameters derived from Cluster 87 = 'South American Indigenous Contemporary Shotgun'"), the design surface must NOT inherit "South American Indigenous cultural-tradition" semantics from the cluster's substrate-descriptive label. The cluster is labelled substrate-descriptively correctly — but a design surface that interprets the label as cultural-tradition has skipped the rep-audit and inherited a wrong premise.

This is the load-bearing reason for the **deferred-commitments framing** across all 5 recognition records. Phase E-3 / Spirit Form designs that hinge on cultural-tradition-of-origin must validate substrate-evidence via rep-audit, not lineage-tag-purity.

### 2.3 Substrate expansion priority (carry 9.10-E)

Naive substrate expansion targeting "raise lineage pool from 29 → 200 rows" amplifies the existing problem if the expansion source has the same Mode-B/C/D content profile. For example: a Mexican government arms-industry crawl would add ~200 Mendoza / Cabañas / Mexican Mauser variants to the mesoamerican lineage, raising the lineage-pool to ~280 rows BUT producing a still-tagging-broken substrate (now the modern Mexican arms-industry dominance is even stronger and the Pre-Columbian sub-pool is still ~12-15 items).

The corrective discipline is **substrate-expansion-by-Mode-A-targeting**: expand crawls explicitly target Pre-Columbian / ethnographic / indigenous-tradition sources (Smithsonian Latin American holdings; Museo Nacional de Antropología; Te Papa Tongarewa NZ; INAH Mexico; Bishop Museum Hawaii; British Museum Pacific collections), NOT "modern country-of-origin arms catalogs."

This requires the substrate-expansion dispatch (legolas Mode B) to be authored with explicit Mode-A-targeting constraints. Sub-carry 9.10-E is currently dormant; when it fires, the dispatch must encode this constraint.

### 2.4 Discipline #18 amendment candidate

The 5 recognition records collectively suggest a possible Discipline #18 amendment (substrate-voting-is-binding):

> The substrate's vote is binding **at the geometry layer** (clustering algorithm output) but NOT necessarily binding **at the semantic layer** (cultural-tradition interpretation of cluster identity). Semantic-layer use of substrate output requires rep-audit at firing.

This may warrant decision-log review or Discipline #18 augmentation. Not commitment-tier yet; observation surfaced here for tracking.

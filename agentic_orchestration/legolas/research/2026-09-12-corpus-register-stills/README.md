## The calibration finding

This is the number the commission was actually after, and it is a **2x split**:

- **Grim Dawn** puts the player at **~17% of frame height**.
- **Hades** puts the player at **~8%** — less than half.

Both are "top-down-ish action games with a visible player character", and they
disagree about character-to-frame scale by a factor of two. So *"match the
corpus"* does not name a single target — **the corpus is bimodal on this axis**,
and which mode our composite sits in is a real choice rather than a lookup.
Flagging it because a composite built to one mode will read as wrong beside the
other, and Matt is about to rule on register while looking at exactly that.

Caveat on the numbers: both are hand-measured off crops, ±10-15 px, and the
Grim Dawn figure includes a wide-brimmed hat worth roughly 25 px of it
(body-only ≈ 160 px, ≈ 14.8%). Rough is fine per commission; do not treat these
as better than two significant figures.

## Cross-cutting observation on the commission's licensing premise

The commission's brief states that *"press kits license editorial/reference
use."* **That premise was not borne out by either kit actually obtained.**
Neither Crate nor Supergiant states any licence, grant, or permitted-use text
alongside its press assets — both simply publish them ungated. Blizzard, the one
publisher here that does gate its kit behind registration, is presumably also
the one whose terms are stated somewhere in the registration flow, which is
exactly the material an unauthenticated read-only probe cannot see.

So the honest position: these two stills are **first-party, publicly offered,
and un-licensed in writing**. That is sufficient for the CLASS D use as scoped
(Matt's private comparison) and is **not** sufficient for anything else.

## Tradition overview

The Tonalpohualli (tōnalpōhualli, "count of days" in Classical Nahuatl) is the Aztec 260-day divinatory calendar, directly parallel in structure to the Mayan Tzolkin — 20 named day-signs cycling through 13 numbers produces 260 unique day-combinations before the cycle repeats. Each day-sign has a presiding deity (tonalteotl) whose power colored the day's fortune, and each trecena (13-day period) was further governed by a separate deity. The Tonalpohualli was the primary instrument of Aztec divination: birth day-signs determined a person's destiny, suitable marriage partners, career paths, and naming practices. The calendar was maintained by trained tonalpohque (day-count specialists) and recorded in tonalamatl (day-count books) including the surviving Codex Borgia, Codex Borbonicus, and Codex Tonalamatl Aubin. Unlike the Tzolkin which survives as a living tradition, the Tonalpohualli was largely disrupted by Spanish colonization (1521 CE) and the forced destruction of Aztec books and priesthood, though it was extensively documented by Franciscan friar Bernardino de Sahagún in the Florentine Codex (~1570 CE).

## Mayan-Aztec parallel structure

The Tonalpohualli and Tzolkin are cognate 260-day systems with identical positional structure. The 20 day-signs align in the same sequence across both traditions, reflecting a shared Proto-Mesoamerican ancestor calendar that predates both Classic Maya and Aztec cultures.

**Year-bearer signs:** Four of the 20 Tonalpohualli signs are also year-bearers (the days that could fall on the first day of the 365-day Xiuhpohualli solar year): **Calli (3), Tochtli (8), Acatl (13), and Tecpatl (18)**. These four signs recur on a four-year rotation and named the year (e.g., "1 Rabbit year"). Ce Acatl (1-Reed) was the birth-year sign of Quetzalcoatl.

**The Aztec Sun Stone (Piedra del Sol):** The most famous Mesoamerican monument, currently at the Museo Nacional de Antropología, Mexico City. The central face is Tonatiuh; the ring immediately surrounding his face shows the four previous world-age suns; the next ring shows all 20 Tonalpohualli day-signs in glyph form. The Ollin (movement) symbol is the central glyph — the name of the current Fifth Sun. Wikipedia Commons has high-resolution images of the full stone and detail shots of the day-sign ring.

2. **Trecena deities not documented** — Beyond the 20 day-sign deities, the Tonalpohualli is also organized into 20 trecenas (13-day periods), each governed by a separate deity. This layer of deity assignment is not captured in the corpus. Phase 2 could add trecena_deity field to the schema.

3. **Codex-specific glyph sources** — The Codex Borgia (pre-Columbian Aztec, Vatican Library), Codex Borbonicus (early colonial Aztec, Bibliothèque de l'Assemblée nationale, Paris), and Codex Tonalamatl Aubin contain the canonical day-sign glyph sequences. Phase 2 could add specific codex page citations for each sign's primary glyph source.

4. **Stellar coordinates absent by design** — Like the Tzolkin, the Tonalpohualli is a calendar-based, not stellar, system. No fixed-star assignments. The exception is Venus-related calculations (eight 65-day Venus periods = five 260-day tonalpohuallis — a 104-year Venus calendar round), but this does not assign individual signs to stars.

5. **SVG glyph URL path verification** — Wikipedia Commons SVG glyph paths follow the [SignName]_glyph.svg pattern. All URLs follow this format convention; live-load verification recommended before production use, as Wikipedia Commons SVG filenames may have case sensitivity issues.

## Tradition overview

The Tibetan zodiac is part of the Tibetan calendar system (Lo-Khak), which integrates the Chinese 12-animal zodiac cycle with Tibetan-Buddhist and Indian astrological frameworks. The 12 Chinese zodiac animals were adopted into Tibet from the 12th century CE via Buddhist transmission routes, alongside the 60-year Rabjung cycle (sexagenary calendar) introduced in 1025 CE by Chandranath and Tsilu Pandit. The Tibetan system differs from the Chinese source in four significant ways: (1) traditional year-sequence begins with Hare, (2) the Dragon incorporates Nāga (Buddhist serpent-spirit) qualities, (3) five elements use Iron rather than Metal, and (4) every sign is enriched with Buddhist-tantric associations from the Kalachakra, Bhavachakra, and tantric deity traditions.

## Cultural differences from Chinese source

### Structural differences:
- **Different starting animal:** Traditional Tibetan zodiac sequence begins with Hare (Yos), not Rat as in Chinese. The Rat (Byi-ba) is 10th in the Tibetan sequence.
- **Five elements — Iron not Metal:** Tibetan five elements are Wood (ཤིང་), Fire (མེ་), Earth (ས་), Iron (ལྕགས་), Water (ཆུ་). Iron replaces Chinese Metal.
- **60-year Rabjung cycle:** The Tibetan Rabjung integrates the sexagenary system with Buddhist epochal time — introduced 1025 CE, attributed to Chandranath and Tsilu Pandit via the Kalachakra tradition.

### Dragon ('Brug) — Nāga enrichment:
The most doctrinally significant variation. The Chinese Dragon (Long) is a purely draconic celestial creature. The Tibetan 'Brug incorporates Nāga qualities — Buddhist serpentine water-spirits who control rain, underground treasures, and sacred ecology. Some Tibetan sources describe this sign as a serpent-dragon hybrid or sea-monster. 'Brug also names Bhutan (Druk Yul = "Land of the Thunder Dragon").

### Bhavachakra (Wheel of Life) — Pig as ignorance:
At position 9, the Tibetan Pig (Phag) carries a unique Buddhist philosophical weight: the pig at the center of the Bhavachakra represents moha (ignorance/delusion), one of the Three Root Poisons driving samsaric rebirth. The three animals at the Bhavachakra's center are pig (ignorance), snake (hatred), and rooster (greed).

## Rabjung 60-year cycle note

The Tibetan Rabjung pairs each of the 12 animals with each of the 5 elements in male (yang) and female (yin) aspects, producing 10 element-gender combinations × 6 animal rotations = 60-year cycle. The cycle begins with male Wood-Rat (Chinese equivalent) year. Each animal's element therefore changes every 2 years, completing the full element cycle in 10 years, and the full animal+element combination cycle in 60 years.

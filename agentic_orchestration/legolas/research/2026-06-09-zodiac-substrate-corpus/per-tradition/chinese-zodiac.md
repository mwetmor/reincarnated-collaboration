## Tradition overview

The Chinese zodiac (十二生肖, shíèr shēngxiào — "twelve birth signs") is a 12-year repeating cycle in which each year is associated with one of 12 animals, tied to the Earthly Branches (地支, dìzhī) of the traditional sexagenary calendar. The system was codified during the Han Dynasty (~200 BCE – 200 CE), though agricultural calendar origins are disputed as earlier. Unlike the Western zodiac, Chinese zodiac signs are not stellar constellations but temporal markers — each animal governs a year, a 2-hour daily period (double-hour), a season, a cardinal direction, and one of the five Chinese elements (Water, Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal) in both a fixed form and a year-cycling form. The Jade Emperor Great Race origin legend — in which animals competed across a river to determine their calendar order — provides a unified mythic narrative for all 12 signs.

## Element and Yin/Yang distribution

**Fixed element distribution:**
- Water: Rat (1), Pig (12) — 2 signs
- Wood: Tiger (3), Rabbit (4) — 2 signs
- Fire: Snake (6), Horse (7) — 2 signs
- Earth: Ox (2), Dragon (5), Goat (8), Dog (11) — 4 signs
- Metal: Monkey (9), Rooster (10) — 2 signs

**Yin/Yang alternation:** The 12 signs strictly alternate Yang/Yin starting with Rat (Yang). Odd positions are Yang; even positions are Yin.

## Great Race narrative structure (Jade Emperor race — position order)

The Jade Emperor summoned all animals to determine a 12-year calendar. Animals had to cross a great river. The order reflects each animal's strategy, character, and circumstance:

1. **Rat** — hitched ride on Ox, leaped off at finish line (cunning)
2. **Ox** — carried Rat, arrived second despite great strength (betrayed generosity)
3. **Tiger** — fought strong currents, arrived third (courage vs. nature)
4. **Rabbit** — hopped on stones and log; Dragon blew wind to help (grace + assistance)
5. **Dragon** — flew but stopped to bring rain to drought village, then helped Rabbit (altruism costs position)
6. **Snake** — hid on Horse's hoof; leaped out to startle Horse at finish (hidden cunning)
7. **Horse** — startled by Snake at finish line; stumbled to seventh (victim of hidden trickery)
8. **Goat** — cooperated with Monkey and Rooster on raft (teamwork)
9. **Monkey** — cooperated with Goat and Rooster on raft (teamwork)
10. **Rooster** — found the raft for the trio (initiative + teamwork)
11. **Dog** — excellent swimmer but played in water too long (present-joy over goal)
12. **Pig** — stopped to eat, fell asleep (abundance/indulgence as last)

## Key structural properties for game design

- **Hours cycle**: The 12 double-hours cover the full 24-hour day in strict sequence — useful for time-of-day gameplay mechanics.
- **Element trines**: Signs share trines based on compatible-signs clusters (e.g., Rat-Dragon-Monkey are all compatible; form a Water-Earth-Metal triad). Trines suggest natural party/faction groupings.
- **Opposing sign**: Each sign has a primary incompatible (opposite) sign — Rat/Horse, Ox/Goat, Tiger/Monkey, Rabbit/Rooster, Dragon/Dog, Snake/Pig — direct conflict pairs.
- **Only mythical sign**: Dragon is the only non-real-world animal — uniquely high status, fits "legendary" class tier.
- **Goat/Sheep ambiguity**: 羊 (yáng) is intentionally ambiguous — either translation is acceptable; recommend "Goat/Ram" as primary for active/combat-adjacent associations.

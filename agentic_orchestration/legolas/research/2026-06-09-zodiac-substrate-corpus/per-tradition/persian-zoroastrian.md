## Tradition overview

The Persian/Zoroastrian calendar month-sign system is not a zodiac in the astrological sense but a sacred calendar structure in which each of the 12 months of the Solar Hijri calendar is governed by a named Yazata (divine being / angelic presence). Each month-name in Persian is derived directly from its presiding Yazata's Avestan name (e.g., Mehr from Mithra, Azar from Atar). Six of the twelve Yazatas are Amesha Spentas — the seven Holy Immortals directly emanated by Ahura Mazda — making the calendar a continuous prayer-cycle through the divine virtues. The tradition originates in Avestan-language religious texts (~1500–600 BCE), was codified in the Sassanid Persian empire (~224–651 CE), and remains the living calendar of Iran today (Solar Hijri months retain their Zoroastrian names).

## Key distinctions from Western zodiac

**This is a sacred calendar, not an astrological zodiac.** The Yazata month-signs are not used for birth-chart personality analysis as Western zodiac signs are. The correspondence to Western zodiac sectors (Aries, Taurus, etc.) is approximate — based on calendar alignment at Nowruz (Persian New Year ≈ spring equinox ≈ Western Aries season) — not on precise ecliptic degree divisions. The Persian system is a **devotional-liturgical calendar**: each day also has a Yazata name (the Persian calendar has 30 named days, many also Yazata names), and when the day-name matches the month-name a special festival occurs (e.g., Azar-month Azar-day = major fire festival).

**Six of twelve months are Amesha Spentas** (Holy Immortals): Ordibehesht (Asha Vahishta), Khordad (Haurvatat), Mordad (Ameretat), Shahrivar (Kshathra Vairya), Bahman (Vohu Manah), Esfand (Spenta Armaiti). Dey is unique as the only month dedicated directly to Ahura Mazda (the supreme deity, not technically an Amesha Spenta). The remaining months honor important but non-Amesha-Spenta Yazatas: Farvardin (Fravashis), Tir (Tishtrya/Sirius), Mehr (Mithra), Aban (Apas/Anahita), Azar (Atar).

**Tishtrya (Tir) is astronomically specific**: Tishtrya is identified with Sirius (not the Cancer sky sector in general) — making this one of the only Persian month-signs with a precise stellar identity beyond the approximate Western zodiac equivalence.

**Mithra's Roman derivative (Mithraism)**: The Yazata Mithra (Month 7, Mehr) became the centerpiece of Roman Mithraism — a mystery cult widespread in the Roman legions. The Roman tauroctony (bull-slaying) iconography is a Roman derivative, NOT present in Avestan Zoroastrian tradition, where Mithra's role is covenant-keeper and sun-adjacent deity.

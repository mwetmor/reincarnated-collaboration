## Tradition overview

MUL.APIN ("The Plough Star") is a Babylonian astronomical compendium preserved on two cuneiform tablets. The most complete copies date to ~700 BCE but the astronomical tradition they encode is estimated to be ~1800–1000 BCE. The tablets contain three catalogues of stars and constellations: the "path of Enlil" (northern sky), the "path of Anu" (equatorial belt), and the "path of Ea" (southern sky), plus a list of 17–18 asterisms crossed by the Moon — the direct precursor to the Western zodiac.

The 12 signs of the Western zodiac derive almost directly from the MUL.APIN "path of the Moon" asterisms. Where modern astronomy tracks the Sun's apparent annual path (the ecliptic), Babylonian astronomers tracked the Moon's path — practically the same sky band, observed monthly rather than annually. The Babylonian system was transmitted through Hellenistic Greek astronomy (via Alexander's conquest of Persia ~330 BCE) to become the Western zodiac as codified by Ptolemy.

## Key substrate details

- **Dumuzi / Tammuz** (MUL.IKU associated): Oldest recorded death-and-resurrection deity tied to astronomical observation. Dumuzi the shepherd descends to the underworld each winter; Inanna descends to rescue him. The seasonal agricultural sacrifice archetype predates Greek Persephone myth by ~1000 years. The MUL.IKU → Aries correspondence preserves this agricultural-sacrifice origin.
- **MUL.GAL.GALLIM "The Seven"** (Pleiades): The most auspicious Babylonian asterism — the Pleiades herald calendar year-start and agricultural seasons. Cross-reference: Chinese Xiu Mǎo #18, Arabic Manazil Al-Thurayya #3, Vedic Nakshatra Krittika #3, Hawaiian Makali'i, Maori Matariki (9 stars) — the most cross-tradition attested asterism in the corpus.
- **MUL.SUḪUR.MAS** (Goat-Fish → Capricorn): One of the most distinctively Mesopotamian composite creatures — half-goat, half-fish body. Associated with Enki/Ea, god of wisdom and freshwater. This creature is unique to Mesopotamian cosmology; the modern Capricorn goat-fish preserved it directly.
- **Pabilsag** (MUL.PA.BIL.SAG → Sagittarius): A specifically Babylonian deity — a composite centaur-archer with scorpion tail. Not simply a human archer. Pabilsag's scorpion tail may explain the design overlap with Scorpius in some ancient depictions.

## Tradition overview

The Dogon people of the Bandiagara Escarpment in Mali have one of the most discussed — and most controversially documented — astronomical traditions in world ethnography. The core of the controversy: Marcel Griaule's 1930s–1950s fieldwork produced extraordinary claims about Dogon pre-telescopic astronomical knowledge, specifically that they knew of Sirius's invisible white dwarf companion (Sirius B). Walter van Beek's 1991 restudy in *Current Anthropology* found no evidence of this knowledge in the Dogon communities he consulted, leading to the current academic consensus that Griaule's documentation was unreliable. What is NOT contested: Sirius (Sigi tolo) is of genuine religious significance to the Dogon, and the Sigui ceremony is a real and documented 60-year ceremonial event of major cultural importance. The Dogon are a living people with active cultural traditions; their astronomical knowledge that predates Griaule's interpretation remains an open research question.

## Griaule controversy — mandatory summary for all Dogon work

Every Dogon entry in this corpus carries the mandatory Griaule controversy flag. The key facts:

**What Griaule claimed:** The Dogon possessed detailed pre-telescopic knowledge of Sirius B (white dwarf companion to Sirius, invisible to naked eye), including its density, orbital period (~50 years — Griaule's figure), and a third star (Sirius C, existence of which was not confirmed by subsequent astronomy). Griaule published this in "A Sudanese Sirius System" (1950) and "Le Renard pâle" (1965).

**Van Beek's restudy (1991):** Van Beek returned to the same Dogon communities (Sangha region, Mali) and found that Dogon people he consulted did NOT describe Sirius as having a companion star. He concluded that the knowledge in Griaule's account was likely introduced to Dogon informants by Western visitors who had discussed modern astronomy in the area before or during Griaule's fieldwork, and/or reflected Griaule's interpretive framework rather than Dogon knowledge.

**Academic consensus:** Griaule's specific Sirius B claims are not accepted as reliable by mainstream academic anthropology and ethnoastronomy. James Clifford's (1983) methodological critique of Griaule's general approach reinforces this verdict.

**What remains true:** Sirius is of genuine religious and ceremonial significance to the Dogon. The Sigui ceremony is a documented reality, filmed by Jean Rouch (1967–73) and documented by multiple ethnographers.

**Corpus consequence:** Low confidence ratings for Po tolo (Sirius B knowledge claim); medium confidence for Sigi tolo (Sirius religious significance) and Sigui (ceremony itself). All entries carry the flag.

The Nommo cosmological detail has even heavier Griaule-contamination than Po tolo; van Beek (1991) specifically questioned Nommo cosmological claims. Excluded from corpus. ## Design notes for game substrate

The Dogon entries are unusual in this corpus because their confidence is explicitly LOW-to-MEDIUM for the astronomical content specifically. For game-design purposes, the *thematic layer* remains valuable:

- **Sigi tolo:** the brightest star; divine centre; ceremonial axis — regardless of the Griaule controversy, these are the Dogon's *expressed* relationship with Sirius
- **Po tolo:** the dense invisible companion — the "heavy star" / "smallest star" — is a compelling archetype (concentrated mass, hidden companion, unseen partner to the brilliant)
- **Sigui cycle:** the 60-year renewal — generational time, ancestral return, slow cosmic rhythm

These thematic materials can be used for substrate-generation without asserting the contested astronomical claims as fact.

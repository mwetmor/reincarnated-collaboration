## Tradition overview

"Native American" describes hundreds of distinct peoples with distinct languages, cultures, cosmologies, and sky-knowledge traditions. There is no single "Native American astronomy" any more than there is a single "Eurasian astronomy." This corpus documents per-tribe, using publicly-published institutional and academic material only. The five entries span three distinct cultural regions: Great Plains (Pawnee Skidi Band, Nebraska), Southeast / Southern Appalachian (Cherokee), and Great Lakes (Anishinaabe / Ojibwe). Sacred-restricted ceremonial astronomical knowledge is explicitly excluded throughout; any entry in this corpus sources only from publicly-published museum artifacts, Bureau of American Ethnology publications, or academic books authored in consultation with tribal communities.

## Key artifact note: Skidi Pawnee Star Chart (Field Museum #16979)

The Skidi Pawnee Star Chart — painted on elk hide, now held at the Field Museum of Natural History in Chicago (Catalog #16979) — is one of the few surviving Native American astronomical artefacts and one of the most significant. Von Del Chamberlain's 1982 analysis is the definitive academic study. The chart's public-museum status makes it an especially clear-cut example of publicly-available institutional record. For the game corpus, the star-chart artifact itself is a visually compelling cultural object: a map of the sky on elk skin, demonstrating that sky-knowledge was important enough to render in permanent material form.

## Pleiades cross-tribal note

The Pleiades (M45) appears in two separate entries for different tribes (Cherokee: Ani Tsutsa / The Boys; Anishinaabe: Manidoominensag / Spirit Seed People) with entirely distinct stories. This is the correct pattern: the same prominent sky-object receives different readings in different tribal traditions, and each reading is its own entry. Do NOT conflate them. The Pleiades also appears in the Aboriginal Australian corpus (Kulkunbulla / Two Boorong Brothers) and the Inuit corpus (Sakiattiat) — again with distinct readings. The universal cross-cultural recognition of the Pleiades is itself a documented ethnographic phenomenon; its distinct tribal readings are the game-design material.

## Design notes for game substrate

The Native American per-tribe entries in this corpus offer strong thematic material:

- **Council of Chiefs (Pawnee):** Stars as living chiefs in council — governance, circular order, star-beings as persons with agency
- **Star-That-Does-Not-Walk-Around (Pawnee):** Precise, descriptive, un-mythologised — the pole star as the *fact* of constancy rather than a mythological figure
- **Ani Tsutsa (Cherokee):** Transformation through ascension; boys who could not be held to earth; the pine tree that remained; seven minus one
- **Fisher Stars (Anishinaabe):** Heroic animal sacrifice for seasonal change; the hunter who becomes the hunted who becomes the sky; wounded tail still visible
- **Manidoominensag (Anishinaabe):** Spirit seeds — concentrated potential in winter form; sky mirroring the dormant earth

The Skidi Pawnee cosmology — in which stars are living beings who created the first humans — is a particularly strong thematic departure from Western astronomical traditions and has high substrate-differentiation value.

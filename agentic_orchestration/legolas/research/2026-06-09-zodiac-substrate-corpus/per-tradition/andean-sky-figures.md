## Tradition overview

The Andean sky-figure tradition — primarily from Quechua (Inca) sources, with Aymara parallels — is one of the world's most distinctive astronomical traditions due to its systematic use of DARK-CLOUD CONSTELLATIONS: figures defined by the dark rifts in the Milky Way (interstellar dust lanes blocking background starlight) rather than by star patterns. This tradition, documented through Gary Urton's 1981 fieldwork in Misminay (Peru), represents an independent parallel development to all star-pattern-based traditions worldwide. The dark animals of the Milky Way (llama, fox, serpent, toad, tinamou, condor) constitute a complete celestial ecology mirroring the actual Andean highland ecosystem. Alongside the dark-cloud figures, the tradition also includes stellar constellations — most importantly the Chakana (Southern Cross as sacred cosmological cross), the Pleiades cluster (agricultural oracle), and the Llama's Eyes (α and β Centauri). Culture specificity is maintained throughout: Quechua and Inca are attributed per source; the tradition is not collapsed to generic "Andean."

## The dark-cloud tradition — what makes it distinct

The Andean dark-cloud constellation system is unique in documented world astronomy. Virtually all other known star traditions — Western, Chinese, Japanese, Indian, Arabian, Aztec, Mayan — identify sky-figures by connecting star-points into outlines. The Andean tradition identified figures in the dark patches between stars: the silhouettes created by interstellar dust lanes blocking the dense star background of the Milky Way. These dark figures:

1. **Are only visible under clear, dark skies with the full Milky Way visible** — they require no individual stars to be identified; the shape is the absence.
2. **Represent animals from the actual Andean ecosystem**: llama, fox, serpent, toad, tinamou, condor — the highland pastoral and wild fauna of the Andes.
3. **Formed a complete celestial ecology** mirroring the earthly Andean ecosystem, with the Milky Way as the sky-equivalent of a highland valley (including pastoral animals, predators, prey, and birds).
4. **Were used for practical agricultural timing** alongside the stellar figures: the Yacana's orientation, the Pleiades' brightness, and the Chakana's position together formed a coordinated sky-calendar.
5. **Are a living tradition** — Gary Urton documented active observation practices in Misminay (Peru) in the 1970s, and contemporary Quechua and Aymara communities continue the practice.

## The Pleiades as agricultural oracle — scientific validation

Entry andean-008 (Collca/Suchu) includes a notable scientific validation footnote. Orlove, Chiang & Cane (2000) published in Nature confirmed that Andean communities' assessment of the Pleiades' brightness and apparent size at June heliacal rising is a genuine empirical El Niño forecasting system:

- Cloud cover over the Pleiades in June (Southern Hemisphere winter) is a proxy for El Niño conditions
- El Niño conditions predict Andean rainfall 6 months later (December–February)
- Thus the traditional practice of reading Pleiades visibility in June to forecast the coming summer's crop yield is scientifically valid
- This is the only traditional astronomical practice in the current corpus with a peer-reviewed Nature validation

This makes Collca/Suchu (andean-008) a particularly strong substrate candidate for game mechanics involving prediction, foresight, and empirical knowledge.

## Cosmological structure of the Andean system

The Andean sky tradition has a layered structure that is worth capturing for substrate-vector purposes:

**Three-world cosmology (pacha tripartite):**
- Hanan pacha (upper world) — sky, stars, Chakana, Kuntur (condor)
- Kay pacha (middle world / present) — Milky Way dark animals, agricultural figures, Collca
- Ukhu pacha (lower world) — Mach'acuay (serpent), water sources, earth

**Sacred animal trinity:**
- Kuntur (condor) — upper world / sky
- Puma — middle world / earth (no sky figure documented in this crawl; puma = Cuzco city layout)
- Amaru/Mach'acuay (serpent) — lower world / water

**Structural links in the sky:**
- α and β Centauri serve dual roles: Yacana's eyes (dark-cloud system) AND Pointer Stars for Chakana (stellar system) — the two systems are connected through these two stars
- The Coalsack Nebula is the Yacana's head region AND adjacent to the Chakana (Southern Cross) — spatial proximity creates cosmological linkage
- The Pleiades (Collca) are the above-horizon anchor of the agricultural calendar; the Yacana and other dark figures are the below-horizon (Milky Way) seasonal system

5. **Pre-Inca cultures** (Tiwanaku, Wari, Chavín) — archaeological evidence suggests similar sky traditions; these predate the Inca and require separate sourcing.

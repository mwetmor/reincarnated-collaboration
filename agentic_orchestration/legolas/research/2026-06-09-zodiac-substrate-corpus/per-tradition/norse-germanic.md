## Tradition overview

Norse astronomical tradition differs structurally from the other corpus traditions — it is not a zodiacal system (not a list of 12 or 28 sequential signs) but a cosmographic mythology in which sky objects are personified divine beings and cosmic structures. The Prose and Poetic Eddas (13th century Icelandic compilations of much older Norse oral tradition) provide the primary textual sources. The sky figures documented are theologically significant cosmic entities, not a systematic sign-cycle.

Key structural features:
- **Inverted Sun/Moon genders**: Sól (Sun) is female; Máni (Moon) is male — the OPPOSITE of the Greek, Roman, Egyptian, and most Indo-European assignments. This inversion is one of the most distinctive Norse cosmological features.
- **Eschatological sky**: Norse sky figures are in motion — the wolves Sköll and Hati pursue Sun and Moon; at Ragnarök they are caught and devoured. This is a sky tradition with an embedded end-of-world timeline.
- **Runic cosmology**: Elder Futhark rune associations link sky figures to the runic alphabet used for divination and inscription.

## Key substrate highlights

**Sól / Máni gender inversion**: In virtually every other Indo-European and Near Eastern sky tradition, the Sun is male (Sol Invictus, Ra, Apollo, Surya, Inti) and the Moon is female (Luna, Selene, Chandra, Mama Quilla). Norse reverses this — Sun is female Sól, Moon is male Máni. This inversion gives Norse entries a strong distinctive flavor for game design: a "light-bringer" who is feminine, a "night-keeper" who is masculine.

**Aurvandill and the named star**: The only instance in the corpus of a star explicitly named after a human body part (frozen toe). The star identity remains debated among Norse scholars — some say Rigel, others Aldebaran, others identify it as a circumpolar star. Tolkien used this myth as the direct source for Eärendil's star in *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Silmarillion*.

**Yggdrasil cosmographic centrality**: The World Tree is the most structurally important figure in Norse mythology — it connects the 9 worlds, shelters the gods, and will fall at Ragnarök. If the Reincarnated game ever adds a Norse-tradition spirit tree or cosmic axis mechanic, Yggdrasil is the canonical design source.

**Karlsvognen ↔ multi-tradition Ursa Major**: Same asterism appears as: Norse Karlsvognen (the Wain), Inuit Tukturjuit (the Caribou), Anishinaabe Fisher Stars (Ursa Major), and implicitly in many traditions as "the seven stars." This is a Tier-2 cross-tradition asterism.

## Cultural sensitivity

Ásatrú and Heathenry (modern Norse neo-paganism) are living religious communities. The corpus entries use academic/mythological sources only — Prose Edda, Poetic Edda, Simek (1993) — which are public domain scholarly texts. The mythological content documented here is not sacred/restricted; it is published literature. Cultural sensitivity: none for corpus documentation purposes.

Living community note: avoid presenting Norse mythology as "dead" tradition if game framing goes deep into this tradition — practitioners exist. Framing as "ancient Norse tradition" (rather than "dead religion") is appropriate.

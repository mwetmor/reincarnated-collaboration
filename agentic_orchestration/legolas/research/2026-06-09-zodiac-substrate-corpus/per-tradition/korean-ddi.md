## Tradition overview

The Korean Ddi (띠) is the Korean adoption of the Chinese 12-animal zodiac, received during the Three Kingdoms period (~1st–7th century CE) via Chinese cultural transmission. Unlike Japanese which modified position 12, Korean Ddi makes only the Sheep/Goat clarification at position 8, otherwise retaining the Chinese animal set exactly. However, Korean culture has layered the zodiac with distinctly Korean practices: the Ddi is used extensively in gunghap (궁합) matchmaking compatibility assessments, the tiger (In) is Korea's national symbol, and the pig dream (돼지꿈) is the single most auspicious dream omen in Korean folklore.

## Cultural differences from Chinese source

- **Position 8 — Sheep (Yang) specified:** The Korean word yang (양) encompasses both sheep and goat (mirroring the Chinese 未 ambiguity), though modern usage renders as sheep. Wikipedia explicitly notes this linguistic overlap.
- **Gunghap matchmaking:** Korean Ddi is the most deeply embedded East Asian zodiac in contemporary matchmaking practice — individuals routinely cite their ddi in social contexts and consult zodiac compatibility for marriage decisions.
- **Tiger as national symbol:** Unlike Japan or Vietnam, Korea elevated the zodiac Tiger (In/Horangi) to national symbol status — sports teams, the 1988 Seoul Olympics mascot Hodori. The tiger's actual extinction in Korea in the early 20th century adds cultural poignancy.
- **Pig dream omen (돼지꿈):** Korean folk belief holds the pig dream as the most auspicious possible omen — predicting incoming wealth and prosperity. Recipients buy lottery tickets immediately. This is uniquely Korean.
- **Jindo dog:** The Jindo (진돗개), a native Korean breed designated a Korean National Treasure, perfectly embodies the Sul (Dog) sign's legendary loyalty qualities — reportedly returning to original owners from great distances.
- **Dragon King mythology:** Korean Jin (Dragon) connects to the Yongwang (용왕, Dragon King) underwater kingdom folk tradition — more specific than Chinese dragon generality.
- **Korean macaque absence:** Sin (Monkey) has lower confidence because Korea has no native monkeys; monkey symbolism is entirely Chinese-derived, not ecologically grounded.

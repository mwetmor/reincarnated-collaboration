## Tradition overview

The Polynesian navigator star traditions represent two distinct cultural bodies of sky knowledge from the Central and South Pacific. Hawaiian star knowledge centers on navigation: the star-path (kilo hoku) system used by deep-ocean voyagers crossing thousands of miles of open Pacific, with Hokule'a (Arcturus) as the zenith star marking the latitude of the Hawaiian archipelago. Maori star knowledge from Aotearoa/New Zealand centers on the seasonal ceremonial calendar anchored by Matariki (Pleiades) — a tradition so central to contemporary Maori identity that New Zealand established it as a national public holiday in 2022. Both traditions encode sophisticated empirical knowledge of the sky in culturally-specific frameworks. Hawaiian and Maori entries are kept strictly distinct in this corpus — they share some star objects (Pleiades, Scorpius) but with entirely different names, meanings, and functions.

## Cross-tradition shared objects (distinct entries)

The Pleiades cluster (M45) appears in BOTH Hawaiian and Maori traditions — and independently in the Andean corpus — with entirely distinct names and meanings:

| Tradition | Name | Function | Season trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hawaiian | Makali'i | Makahiki harvest festival / year-start | November heliacal rising (N. Hemisphere) |
| Maori | Matariki | Maori New Year / ancestor remembrance | June heliacal rising (S. Hemisphere) |
| Andean | Collca / Suchu | Agricultural yield forecast | June heliacal rising (S. Hemisphere) |

The different trigger seasons reflect different hemispheres: Hawaiians are in the Northern Hemisphere (~20°N); Maori and Andean communities are in the Southern Hemisphere (~35–47°S for NZ; ~13–22°S for Andes). The June Maori/Andean heliacal rising is the cluster appearing in the pre-dawn winter sky — six months offset from the November Hawaiian appearance.

## Key navigation functions documented

**Hawaiian navigation star functions:**
- **Zenith stars** (Hokule'a/Arcturus): when a star is at zenith, navigator reads their latitude
- **Pole stars** (Hoku Pa'a/Polaris): altitude above horizon = observer's latitude in Northern Hemisphere
- **Direction stars** (A'a/Sirius, Ke Ali'i/Canopus): rising and setting azimuth indicates compass direction
- **Pointer stars** (Na Hiku/Big Dipper → Polaris): indirect north-finding when pole star not obvious
- **Seasonal markers** (Makali'i/Pleiades): heliacal rising triggers calendar events
- **Mythological anchors** (Ka Makau/Scorpius): cultural narrative connects sky shape to origin stories

**Maori navigation and calendar functions:**
- **New Year herald** (Matariki/Pleiades): midwinter heliacal rising triggers the year cycle
- **Nine-domain calendar** (Matariki 9 stars): each star governs an aspect of the natural world for the coming year
- **South pole pointer** (Mahutonga/Southern Cross): long axis extended → south celestial pole
- **East-west compass** (Tautoru/Orion's Belt): rises due east, sets due west from any latitude
- **Chief of stars** (Rehua): lord of celestial hierarchy; summer abundance
- **Milky Way origin** (Tama-rereti): cosmological framework for the galactic band as scattered-star path

## Excluded / not sourced per HIGH sensitivity protocol

1. **Specific voyage star-path sequences** (kilo hoku routes) from PVS materials that the PVS itself frames as "not for general public distribution" or appears only on restricted-access pages — navigation-path sequences are transmission-restricted knowledge within the PVS's own framing.

2. **Iwi-specific oral traditions** not confirmed in published institutional sources (Te Papa, Best 1922, Bishop Museum). Third-party non-institutional web sources (practitioner blogs, spiritual tourism sites) were not used per HIGH sensitivity protocol.

3. **Stars marked as restricted or sacred in source material.** Any reference on non-institutional sites to stars being "used only in wānanga (teaching house) contexts" or "not for public sharing" was excluded.

4. **Hula-associated star knowledge** from practitioner sites without institutional backing — excluded per protocol.

5. **Tongan, Samoan, and Tuvaluan star traditions** — these are distinct Polynesian cultures not represented in the current crawl scope (Hawaiian + Maori only). Flagged for Phase 2 scope expansion.

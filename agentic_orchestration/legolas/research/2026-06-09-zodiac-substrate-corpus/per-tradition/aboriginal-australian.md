## Tradition overview

Aboriginal Australian sky-knowledge is one of the oldest and most culturally rich ethnoastronomical traditions on Earth — with continuous practice likely spanning tens of thousands of years. It is critically distinct from Western astronomical tradition in two ways: first, it is country-specific (each Aboriginal people's sky-tradition is their own, tied to their land and law); second, many traditions recognise *dark-cloud constellations* — figures defined by the dark rifts of the Milky Way (regions where interstellar dust obscures background stars) rather than by the positions of bright stars. This is a paradigmatically different mode of sky-reading, unique to Peoples of the Southern Hemisphere who can see the dense central Milky Way. The best-known dark-cloud figure is the Emu in the Sky, documented for the Kamilaroi and other peoples. Duane Hamacher and Ray Norris have established Australian cultural astronomy as a rigorous academic field since 2010. AIATSIS (Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies) is the primary institutional authority for what is publicly accessible vs. restricted.

## Tradition notes: dark-cloud paradigm

The dark-cloud constellation paradigm is a significant game-design opportunity. Unlike every other tradition in this corpus, Aboriginal Australian dark-cloud figures are defined by *negative space* — by the dark rifts in the Milky Way, not by bright star connections. This means:

- The visual assets needed are different: Milky Way photographs with rift annotation, not star-connection line diagrams
- The figures are only visible from the Southern Hemisphere (or low southern latitudes)
- The figures are exceptionally ancient — the Coalsack has been in approximately the same position for many thousands of years
- The seasonal calendar use (emu orientation = emu season) reflects sophisticated practical ethnoastronomy at the sky–ecology interface

For game substrate purposes: the dark-cloud paradigm suggests a *shadow* or *void* archetype — power defined by absence, by negative space, by what-is-not-there. This is distinct from every other tradition documented.

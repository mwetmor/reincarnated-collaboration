### 1. Emu in the Sky — Annotated Milky Way photograph

**Entry:** `aboriginal-kamilaroi-001`
**Suggested URL:** `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Emu_in_the_sky.jpg/1200px-Emu_in_the_sky.jpg`
**Wikipedia article:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu_in_the_Sky
**Description:** Photograph of the Milky Way from the Southern Hemisphere showing the dark rifts of the galactic plane; the Emu in the Sky figure is typically annotated over the dark lane from the Coalsack Nebula (near Southern Cross) to the Sagittarius/Scorpius region. The Coalsack is visible as a dark patch adjacent to the bright stars of the Southern Cross.
**License:** CC-BY-SA (Wikipedia Commons)
**Verified loads:** Confirm by visiting the Wikipedia article — images on the Wikipedia Emu in the Sky page are freely licensed.
**Key visual information:**
- The figure is entirely defined by darkness (dark nebulae / dust lanes in the Milky Way)
- The Coalsack dark nebula (adjacent to the Southern Cross) = emu's head
- The dark lane extending through Centaurus toward Scorpius = body
- Best photographed from Southern Hemisphere in April–May (when the emu is at the right orientation for egg-laying season interpretation)
- The Southern Cross (Crux) is the most recognisable adjacent landmark for orienting the image
**Cultural sensitivity note:** This image is of the Milky Way — not of sacred objects, ceremonial items, or living elders. No sensitivity restriction on a photograph of the night sky.

**Alternative / additional images:**
- Wikipedia Commons search "Coalsack Nebula" — multiple freely licensed photos showing the dark patch adjacent to Southern Cross
- Wikipedia Commons search "Milky Way Southern Hemisphere" — additional freely licensed panoramic photos
- Hamacher & Frew (2010) paper contains figures; these require journal access and fair-use consideration

Rock art images from the Lightning Brothers site (Delamere Station, Northern Territory) may carry cultural protocols for reproduction beyond standard CC licensing. The site contains sacred images. - AIATSIS (Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies)

## General visual notes for Aboriginal Australian tradition

**Dark-cloud constellation photography guidance:**
The Emu in the Sky and other dark-cloud figures require photographs of the *Milky Way* taken from the Southern Hemisphere, ideally with the camera pointed along the galactic plane from ~Dec −30° to −65°. Key Southern Hemisphere reference points:
- The Southern Cross (Crux) is at RA 12h, Dec −60° — the key landmark adjacent to the Coalsack
- The Coalsack is the dark patch directly adjacent to (and slightly southeast of) the Southern Cross
- The Milky Way rift runs from the Coalsack through Centaurus, toward Scorpius and Sagittarius

Wikipedia Commons search terms for freely-licensed Milky Way Southern Hemisphere images:
- "Milky Way Southern Hemisphere"
- "Coalsack Nebula"
- "Emu in the Sky"
- "Southern Cross Milky Way"

**Note on Google Sky / Stellarium:**
Stellarium (open-source planetarium software) can generate annotated sky charts at any location, date, and time — including Southern Hemisphere views showing the Milky Way rift with the Coalsack and galactic plane. Stellarium is freely licensed for educational use. Generated Stellarium images showing the Emu's position could be produced for the corpus with proper attribution.

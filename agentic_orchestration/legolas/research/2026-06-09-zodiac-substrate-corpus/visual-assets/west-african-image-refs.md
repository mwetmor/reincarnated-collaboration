**Entry:** `west-african-dogon-001`
**Primary URL:** `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Sirius_A_and_B_artwork.jpg/1200px-Sirius_A_and_B_artwork.jpg`
**Wikipedia article:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius
**Description:** Artist's impression of the Sirius binary system — Sirius A (the brilliant main-sequence star) and Sirius B (the small white dwarf, visible as a faint point to the lower left). NASA/ESA commissioned artwork.
**License:** Public domain (NASA artwork)
**Key visual information:**
- Sirius A appears as a brilliant blue-white star
- Sirius B is the tiny, faint white point in the image — invisible to the naked eye (magnitude 8.44)
- The visual contrast between the two captures the essence of the Po tolo controversy: how could the Dogon have known about an invisible companion?
- For game use: this image effectively communicates the dense, concentrated nature of Sirius B (the "heaviest, smallest star" of Griaule's Dogon account)

**Additional Sirius images:**
- Hubble Space Telescope actual photograph: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Sirius_A_and_B_Hubble_photo.jpg/1200px-Sirius_A_and_B_Hubble_photo.jpg`
  - License: Public domain (NASA/ESA HST)
  - Description: HST image showing Sirius A (brilliant overexposed center) and Sirius B (tiny dot, lower left) — actual photograph, not artwork

**Cultural note:** The images used are of the star, not of Dogon cultural objects. No sensitivity concern with astronomical photographs of Sirius.

## General visual notes for West African tradition

**Griaule controversy and image use:**
The Griaule controversy applies to claims about Dogon astronomical knowledge, not to images of Dogon people, ceremonies, or landscape. Photographs of the Bandiagara Escarpment, Dogon architecture, and ceremonial objects (per sensitivity guidance above) can be used without implicating the astronomical controversy.

**No "Dogon zodiac" imagery exists:**
There are no ancient Dogon sky charts or astronomical diagrams in the public record comparable to the Egyptian Dendera zodiac or the Skidi Pawnee star chart. The Dogon astronomical tradition, per Griaule, was transmitted orally and in ceremonial context — not in permanent visual artefact form. This means image assets for Dogon sky-tradition are necessarily indirect (photos of Sirius, ceremonial contexts, landscape) rather than direct sky-charts.

## High-priority Phase 2 targets

### Korean Tiger (In) — most important image target:
Korean minhwa tiger-magpie paintings are among the most iconic Korean art images, widely available on Commons. This is the highest-value image in the Korean corpus.
- Search: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Search?search=Korean+tiger+minhwa
- The tiger-magpie composition (까치호랑이, Kkachi Horangi) is the canonical Korean folk art tiger image
- Multiple Joseon-period tiger minhwa are in public domain

### Korean Jindo Dog (Sul) — culturally important:
The Jindo dog is a Korean National Treasure; official photographs are available.
- Wikipedia article: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Jindo
- Commons search: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Jindo_(dog)

### Goryeo Celadon Dragon (Jin):
Goryeo celadon with dragon motifs are in major museum collections.
- The Metropolitan Museum: https://www.metmuseum.org (search "Goryeo celadon dragon")
- Many pieces in public domain (pre-1928)

### Korean minhwa general category:
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Minhwa
- Most productive category for Korean zodiac visual sourcing

### Hodori (1988 Olympics tiger mascot):
- The Hodori mascot is widely documented; copyright status for recent Olympics materials may be restricted
- Phase 2: check IOC license terms before using in production context

## Notes on image availability

- Korean zodiac does not have a dedicated Wikipedia article — all sourced from Chinese zodiac comparison tables
- Korean folk painting (minhwa) is the primary authentic image tradition for Korean zodiac — significantly more available than Japanese ema-style images
- The tiger (In/Horangi) has the richest image availability across all Korean zodiac signs due to its national symbol status
- Pig (Hae) imagery related to pig dreams (돼지꿈) is primarily found in illustrated books and may require Korean-language source searches

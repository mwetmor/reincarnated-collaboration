## Dresden Codex — source for Mayan glyph imagery

The Dresden Codex (Dresdner Kodex) is the oldest surviving Mayan codex, dating to approximately the 11th–12th century CE (a copy of an earlier Classic period text). It contains Venus almanacs, eclipse tables, and ritual calendars including Tzolkin day-sign imagery. The full facsimile is in the public domain.

**Dresden Codex Wikipedia page:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden_Codex
**Wikipedia Commons facsimile pages:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Dresden_Codex

The Dresden Codex is held by the Saxon State Library (SLUB Dresden, Germany). High-resolution scans are available through SLUB Digital Collections.

## Phase 2 visual asset priorities

1. **Full comparative glyph set** — The 20 Wikipedia Commons PNGs are sufficient for substrate corpus purposes. For production visual assets, higher-resolution Dresden Codex sourcing would be preferable.

2. **Madrid Codex (Codex Tro-Cortesianus)** — The Madrid Codex is the longest surviving Maya codex and contains Tzolkin day-sign imagery. It is held by the Museo de América in Madrid. Facsimile available via Wikipedia Commons: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Codex

3. **Stela and monument carvings** — Classic Maya stelae at Copan, Tikal, Quiriguá, and Palenque contain date inscriptions with Tzolkin day-sign glyphs carved in stone. INAH and the University of Texas AILLA archive have photography.

4. **Living ceremonial context photography** — K'iche' day-keeper (ajq'ij) ceremony photography exists in academic/documentary form. For production use, direct rights acquisition from individual photographers recommended; do not use without attribution.

**Full-resolution commons page:**
```
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zodiaque_de_Dend%C3%A9ra_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Antiquit%C3%A9s_Egyptiennes_D_38_%3B_E_13482.jpg
```

**Description:** The Dendera Zodiac as displayed at the Louvre (Musée du Louvre, Antiquités Egyptiennes D38; E13482). Bas-relief from the ceiling of the Osirian chapel at the Hathor Temple, Dendera, Egypt. Ptolemaic period, approximately 50 BCE. Removed from Egypt in 1821 by Claude Lelorrain; installed in the Royal Library of France 1822; transferred to the Louvre in 1922 where it resides today.

**What it shows:** Circular zodiac combining:
- Inner ring: 12 Babylonian/Greek zodiac signs
- Middle ring: Greco-Egyptian versions of the 12 signs
- **Outer ring: 36 decan figures in procession** — the primary visual reference for Egyptian decans
- Four women and four falcon-headed pairs at 45° intervals holding up the sky disc
- North pole center with Ursa Minor depicted as a jackal

**Physical characteristics:**
- Material: Sandstone bas-relief
- Original location: Ceiling of pronaos of the Osirian chapel, Hathor temple at Dendera
- Current location: Louvre, Paris — Room 12, Ground floor, Sully wing
- Accession: D38 / E13482

**License:** Public Domain (ancient artifact ~50 BCE; Louvre accession)

**Source:** Wikipedia Commons via Wikipedia:Dendera_zodiac

**Verified loads:** true (confirmed from Wikipedia crawl 2026-06-09)

## Related Egyptian astronomical image — Seti I tomb astronomical ceiling

**Wikipedia article:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_ceiling_of_Senemut_Tomb

**Note:** The Wikipedia:Decan article features an image from the "Astronomical ceiling of Senemut Tomb showing various decans, as well as the personified representations of stars and constellations." This is an earlier (New Kingdom) example of decan representation predating the Ptolemaic Dendera Zodiac. The Senemut tomb belongs to Senenmut, chief steward of Queen Hatshepsut (~1473-1458 BCE). The astronomical ceiling shows decans in their earlier diagonal-star-table format.

**Search term for Wikipedia Commons:** "Astronomical ceiling Senemut tomb" or "Seti I astronomical ceiling"

## Usage notes for Reincarnated substrate

For the corpus entries, the Dendera Zodiac image is used as a **shared reference** for all 36 decans rather than per-decan images, because:

1. The Dendera Zodiac is "the only complete map that we have of an ancient sky" (John H. Rogers, cited in Wikipedia:Dendera_zodiac) — it genuinely shows all 36 decans together.
2. No isolated per-decan images exist in public Wikipedia Commons for individual decan figures.
3. Individual decan figure photos would require high-resolution close-ups of the Louvre relief — available in the Louvre's own digital archive (collections.louvre.fr) for Phase 2.

**Phase 2 image sourcing options:**
- Louvre digital collections: https://collections.louvre.fr (accession D38/E13482)
- British Museum Egyptian collection: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection (search "decan")
- Metropolitan Museum of Art digital collection: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection (search "Egyptian astronomical")
- Walters Art Museum open-access: https://art.thewalters.org

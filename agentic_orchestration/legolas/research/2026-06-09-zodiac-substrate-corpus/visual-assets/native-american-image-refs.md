### 1. Council of Chiefs (Pawnee Skidi) — Corona Borealis star map

**Entry:** `native-american-pawnee-skidi-001`
**Primary URL:** `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Corona_Borealis_constellation_map.svg/1200px-Corona_Borealis_constellation_map.svg.png`
**Wikipedia article:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_Borealis
**Description:** IAU official constellation map of Corona Borealis; shows the semicircular arc of ~7 stars that forms the Northern Crown. The "Council" reading is immediately visually intuitive — the arc forms a natural meeting-circle.
**License:** CC-BY-SA (IAU / Wikipedia Commons)
**Key visual information:**
- The semicircular arc shape is one of the most distinctive of any constellation — immediately readable as a circle or ring
- Alphecca (α CrB, magnitude 2.21) is the brightest star, sitting at the centre of the arc
- The ring opens toward the south (the 'gap' is at the bottom of the arc)
- Visible in the evening sky from late spring through summer in the Northern Hemisphere — its appearance corresponds to the Skidi Pawnee summer ceremonial season

**Additional visual for Council context:**
- A photograph of a Native American council circle or traditional gathering (if freely licensed) could supplement the star map as cultural context
- CONSTRAINT: any such image must specify the tribe and be from a public documentary context; avoid generic "Native American" stock imagery

### 2. Star-That-Does-Not-Walk-Around (Pawnee) — Polaris photograph

**Entry:** `native-american-pawnee-skidi-002`
**Primary URL:** `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Polaris_alpha_ursae_minoris.jpg/1200px-Polaris_alpha_ursae_minoris.jpg`
**Wikipedia article:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris
**Description:** Photograph of Polaris (α Ursae Minoris), the north celestial pole star.
**License:** CC-BY-SA (Wikipedia Commons — confirm specific file license)
**Key visual information:**
- Polaris (magnitude 1.97) is a moderately bright star; it appears unremarkable in a single photograph
- Its distinction is behavioural, not visual: it does not appear to move as other stars rotate
- For game production: a star-trail photograph showing Polaris at the fixed centre with other stars tracing circular arcs around it is far more visually compelling

**Star-trail Polaris supplement (strongly recommended):**
- Wikipedia Commons search "Polaris star trails" — long-exposure photographs showing all stars rotating as arcs around the stationary Polaris; multiple CC-BY-SA options
- Example: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Circumpolar_Star_Trails_over_Gemini_Observatory.jpg/1200px-Circumpolar_Star_Trails_over_Gemini_Observatory.jpg`
  - Description: Long-exposure star trail photograph showing circular arcs around Polaris
  - License: CC-BY-SA (check specific file)
- The star-trail image *demonstrates* the Skidi Pawnee name — "the star that does not walk around" — in a single photograph

**Entry:** `native-american-cherokee-001`
**Primary URL:** `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Pleiades_large.jpg/1200px-Pleiades_large.jpg`

### 4. Fisher Stars (Anishinaabe) — Ursa Major / Big Dipper star map

**Entry:** `native-american-anishinaabe-ojibwe-001`
**Primary URL:** `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Ursa_Major_constellation_map.svg/1200px-Ursa_Major_constellation_map.svg.png`
**Wikipedia article:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Major
**Description:** IAU official constellation map of Ursa Major; shows the Big Dipper (Plough) asterism. The Fisher (pine marten) body maps to the Dipper bowl; the tail (injured by arrow) maps to the handle, with Alkaid (handle tip) as the tail-tip wound.
**License:** CC-BY-SA (IAU / Wikipedia Commons)
**Key visual information:**
- The Big Dipper / Plough is circumpolar from Great Lakes latitudes (~45°N+)
- The handle's three stars (Alioth, Mizar, Alkaid) form the Fisher's tail
- Mizar has a naked-eye companion (Alcor) — a double star system; potentially visually interesting detail
- For game: Big Dipper + annotation showing Fisher body (bowl) and tail (handle) would be an effective custom asset

**Fisher (pine marten) animal supplement:**
- Wikipedia Commons search "Fisher animal" / "Martes pennanti" — photographs of the Fisher (a large member of the weasel family); multiple freely-licensed options
- The Fisher is a real North American animal: dark brown, larger than a mink, a forest-dwelling predator; its aggressive nature and climbing ability are consistent with the mythic hero character
- NOTE: "Fisher" (Martes pennanti) is not a marten in the strict species sense but is in the Mustelidae family; it is called a fisher in English though it rarely eats fish

## PRIORITY PHASE 2 ASSET: Skidi Pawnee Star Chart (Field Museum Catalog #16979)

**Status:** NOT yet included in corpus visual assets. Requires explicit Field Museum permission for image use.

**Why this matters:**
The Skidi Pawnee Star Chart is one of the most significant Native American astronomical artefacts that exists. It is a sky chart painted on elk hide, held at the Field Museum of Natural History in Chicago (Catalog #16979). Von Del Chamberlain's 1982 analysis reproduces images of the chart in an academic publication context.

**Path to obtaining the image for corpus use:**
1. Field Museum of Natural History online collections: https://www.fieldmuseum.org/
   - Search catalog number 16979 in the Field Museum online collection database
   - Check if the museum's digital image is publicly accessible or requires rights agreement
2. Contact Field Museum rights/reproductions department:
   - Email: photos@fieldmuseum.org (Field Museum image licensing)
   - Request academic / educational use permission for the star chart image
3. Chamberlain (1982) "When Stars Came Down to Earth" contains reproduction images in an academic publication context; reproducing these in a game product context requires publisher rights clearance

**Visual description (for reference without the image):**
The chart is painted on a tanned hide approximately 34cm × 40cm. It shows circular objects (stars) in various configurations painted in colour. Several recognisable patterns correspond to known asterisms (the Council of Chiefs / Corona Borealis circle is reportedly visible). The chart is not a 1:1 map of the sky; it likely represents ceremonially significant star-groupings rather than a navigational chart. Chamberlain (1982) provides the detailed interpretation.

**Alternative archival path:**
The Smithsonian NMAI digital collections or the Smithsonian Institution Archives may have photographic records of the Pawnee star chart. Search: https://americanindian.si.edu/explore/collections

## General visual guidance for Native American tradition

**Tribal specificity is load-bearing for image use:**
Any photograph, artwork, or cultural image included in a Native American corpus entry must be attributed to the specific tribe — not to "Native American" generically. Mixing tribal visual cultures (e.g., using a Sioux headdress image for a Pawnee entry) is a significant cultural error. Use only:
- Astronomical images (star maps, constellation photographs) — these are tribe-neutral
- Species photographs (animals, birds) — these are neutral
- Tribal-specific cultural images only when the tribe is confirmed AND the image is cleared for use

**Great Plains sky photography (Pawnee context):**
- Wikipedia Commons search "Great Plains night sky" / "Nebraska night sky" / "Kansas night sky" — some freely-licensed options
- The Great Plains provides some of the darkest skies in North America; long-horizon views under an immense star-filled sky characterise the Skidi Pawnee astronomical environment

**Great Lakes forest photography (Anishinaabe context):**
- Wikipedia Commons search "Great Lakes forest" / "Minnesota forest" / "Ontario boreal forest" — many CC-BY-SA landscape photographs
- The Fisher (pine marten) lives in boreal forest; the Great Lakes winter sky is the astronomical setting for the Fisher Stars and Manidoominensag entries

**Appalachian Mountain photography (Cherokee context):**
- Wikipedia Commons search "Appalachian Mountains" / "Smoky Mountains" — many CC-BY-SA landscape photographs
- Cherokee homeland was the Southern Appalachians; the mountain forest setting contextualises Ani Tsutsa

**Note on commercial use:**
For commercial game development, all photographic assets should be confirmed as CC0 / public domain, or formally licensed. CC-BY-SA images in a commercial context require attribution (keep in credits). Wikipedia Commons files marked CC-BY-SA are permissible with proper attribution; confirm the specific license for each file before production use.

## Critical context: dark-cloud figures lack dedicated imagery

The 6 dark-cloud constellation entries (andean-001 through andean-006) represent a fundamental challenge for visual asset sourcing: **they are defined by absence of stars**, not by star patterns. There is no IAU diagram for the Yacana, no conventional constellation outline image. The best available visual references are:

1. **Wide-field Milky Way photographs** showing the dark rift — the actual sky region where the dark figures are found
2. **CruxCC photograph** — Southern Cross with adjacent Coalsack dark nebula, which is Yacana's head region
3. **Annotated dark-cloud constellation diagrams** from academic publications (Urton 1981 includes hand-drawn diagrams; no public-domain digital version found in this crawl)

The stellar entries (Chakana, Collca, Orqa) have conventional star images available.

## Missing images — critical Phase 2 targets

### Priority 1 (high value for substrate use)
1. **Annotated Yacana dark-rift map** — an overlay showing the Yacana outline on the actual Milky Way photograph. This would be the most important asset for the entire Andean dark-cloud collection. Sources: Urton (1981) has a diagram (copyrighted); a new annotated Wikipedia-style diagram would need to be created and licensed CC-BY. Alternatively, check if any academic ethnoastronomy papers have released annotated sky maps under CC licenses.

2. **Chakana stepped-cross symbol** — the geometric 12-step cross design that is the artistic representation of the Chakana. Wikipedia's Chakana article includes this; verify its license. This symbol appears throughout contemporary Andean art and design.

3. **Andean condor in flight showing wingspan** — dramatic visual for the Kuntur entry. Multiple CC-licensed condor flight photos exist on Wikipedia Commons; specific URL verification needed.

### Priority 2 (enhancing visual coverage)
4. **Andean llama** — a photograph of a real Andean llama to anchor the Yacana entry's animal identity. Strong CC-licensed images available on Wikipedia Commons.

5. **Inca Pleiades observation context** — any photograph of Andean highland landscape at night with Milky Way would strengthen the agricultural-oracle context of the Collca entry. CC-licensed night-landscape photography from Peru or Bolivia needed.

6. **Tiwanaku Chakana stonework** — archaeological photograph showing the stepped-cross symbol in Tiwanaku stone carving. This would establish the pre-Inca antiquity of the symbol. Wikipedia Commons likely has Tiwanaku site photographs; verify availability.

### Priority 3 (secondary enrichment)
7. Animal photos for each dark-cloud figure: Andean fox (culpeo), serpent, toad/frog, tinamou. All available on Wikipedia Commons with appropriate CC licenses; specific URLs need verification.

## Recommendation for substrate use

For the dark-cloud figures, the most impactful visual representation strategy would be:

1. **Base layer**: `Milky_Way_Arch.jpg` — the actual sky (astrophotograph)
2. **Overlay layer**: Animal silhouette (llama, fox, condor, etc.) superimposed as a dark silhouette matching the rift shape — this recreates the experience of "seeing" the dark figure in the sky
3. **Eye layer** (for Yacana only): two bright points at α/β Centauri positions to show the luminous eyes in the dark body

This three-layer visual approach would be unique to the Andean entries in the substrate corpus — no other tradition in the current corpus has this dark-figure visual paradigm.

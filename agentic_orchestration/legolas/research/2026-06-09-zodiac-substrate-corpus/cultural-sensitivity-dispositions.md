# Cultural Sensitivity Dispositions — Zodiac Substrate Corpus

**Commission:** 2026-06-09-zodiac-substrate-corpus
**Legolas Mode B crawl**
**Protocol authority:** dispatch § 4.2

---

## Protocol summary

| Level | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| none | Publicly published since antiquity; scientific/IAU | Standard crawl |
| low | Some esoteric content; academic sources adequate | Source from academic/museum; flag specific entries |
| medium | Ceremonial content; cultural-heritage framing needed | Academic + cultural-heritage institutions; cite permission framing |
| high | Sacred-adjacent; restricted in some contexts | Academic + cultural-heritage with explicit sensitivity acknowledgment; flag every entry |
| restricted | Sacred/ceremonial per institutional designation | DO NOT include; file exclusion disposition here |

Default: flag higher + exclude if in doubt.

## Per-tradition dispositions

### Western zodiac (12 signs)
**Level:** none
**Rationale:** Publicly published since Babylonian antiquity; Hellenistic codification; Roman, Islamic, and modern Western iterations all in mainstream academic record. No sacred-restricted content.
**Source authorization:** Wikipedia + IAU + Britannica + academic ethnoastronomy. No restriction applies.

### Western IAU constellations (88)
**Level:** none
**Rationale:** IAU-designated scientific catalog. Entirely public domain.
**Source authorization:** IAU + Wikipedia + Stellarium.

### Mesopotamian (MUL.APIN + predecessor traditions)
**Level:** none
**Rationale:** Ancient texts in academic record; cuneiform tablets publicly held in British Museum + Louvre. No living sacred tradition.
**Source authorization:** Wikipedia + British Museum + academic Mesopotamian studies.

### Celtic tree zodiac (Coelbren/Ogham-adjacent, 13 trees)
**Level:** low
**Rationale:** Partially modern-neopagan reconstruction (Robert Graves White Goddess 1948, largely post-Victorian synthesis). Historical Celtic Ogham tradition is academically documented but the "Celtic zodiac" as zodiac system is substantially modern synthesis. Flag reconstruction status on every entry.
**Source authorization:** Wikipedia + academic Celticist sources. Robert Graves as secondary with explicit caveat.

### Norse/Germanic (runic zodiac + constellation tradition)
**Level:** low
**Rationale:** Partially modern reconstruction. Old Norse Eddic sources (Völuspá, Grímnismál) are academically published. Runic zodiac as 12-sign system is partially medieval + partially modern interpretation. Flag reconstruction status.
**Source authorization:** Wikipedia + Old Norse academic + Eddic texts via academic translation.

### Polynesian navigator traditions
**Level:** high
**Rationale:** Polynesian navigator star-knowledge is living cultural tradition; some knowledge is explicitly lineage-transmitted and culturally restricted. SOURCING CONSTRAINT: Polynesian Voyaging Society + Bishop Museum + Te Papa (Maori) + publicly-published academic ethnoastronomy ONLY. If specific navigator star-sets are marked "not for public transmission" in source material, exclude and note here.

### Aboriginal Australian sky traditions
**Level:** high → restricted for specific content
**Rationale:** Aboriginal Australian sky-lore varies by country (Yolngu, Wardaman, Boorong, Adnyamathanha are distinct peoples). AIATSIS (Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies) designates some sky-figures as sacred-restricted; these MUST NOT appear in corpus.
**Protocol:** Source ONLY from publicly-published academic ethnoastronomy (Duane Hamacher's work; AIATSIS public cultural record; Norris & Hamacher 2011 "The Astronomy of Aboriginal Australia" as primary academic anchor). Do NOT source from "sacred knowledge" sites. If any sky-figure is marked restricted per AIATSIS in source material, note here and exclude.
**Known exclusion consideration:** Specific Wardaman and Yolngu ceremonial sky-figures may be restricted. Academic publications discuss publicly-known figures (Emu in the Sky, Boorong star-lore per Hamacher & Frew 2010); these are eligible.
**Cultural-specificity requirement per § 4.3:** MUST specify per-Aboriginal-people tradition (Yolngu / Wardaman / Boorong / Adnyamathanha / Kamilaroi etc.) — NOT collapsed to "Australian Aboriginal."
**Source authorization:** Academic ethnoastronomy (Hamacher, Norris, Dobos-Barnes) + AIATSIS publicly-accessible records ONLY.

### Inuit/Arctic Circumpolar traditions
**Level:** high
**Rationale:** Living cultural traditions; some knowledge is community-transmitted. Academic ethnoastronomy is the appropriate source layer.
**Cultural-specificity requirement per § 4.3:** MUST specify per-Arctic-tradition (Inuit / Sámi / Yupik / Aleut / Chukchi — DISTINCT traditions, do not collapse to "Arctic").
**Source authorization:** Smithsonian Arctic Studies + academic ethnoastronomy with indigenous-engagement framing.

### Andean sky traditions (Quechua/Aymara/Inca)
**Level:** medium
**Rationale:** Dark-cloud constellation tradition (Yacana, etc.) is academically documented through ethnoastronomy. UNESCO World Heritage records publicly accessible. Ceremonial context carries medium sensitivity.
**Cultural-specificity requirement per § 4.3:** MUST specify Quechua / Aymara / Inca-specific (these are DISTINCT traditions).
**Source authorization:** Wikipedia + academic Andeanist (Gary Urton "At the Crossroads of the Earth and Sky") + UNESCO World Heritage records.

### West African traditions (Dogon + broader)
**Level:** high
**Rationale:** Dogon Sirius tradition carries specific sensitivity: (1) Marcel Griaule's fieldwork (1930s-1950s) has been contested by later anthropologists; (2) Sirius lore relates to Sigui ceremony that has restricted-knowledge aspects. NON-DOGON traditions (Yoruba, Akan) are less academically-documented in ethnoastronomy context but are public cultural traditions.
**Protocol:** Include Dogon public astronomical tradition only from peer-reviewed academic sources that explicitly address the Griaule controversy. Flag the Griaule controversy on every Dogon entry. Do NOT include entries derived solely from Griaule's contested claims without academic corroboration.
**Source authorization:** Peer-reviewed academic ethnoastronomy + cultural-heritage institution sources. No pop-sci or Griaule-derivative-only entries.

### Native American sky traditions (multi-tribal)
**Level:** high → restricted for specific content
**Rationale:** NO SINGLE "Native American zodiac" exists — each tribal tradition is distinct. CRITICAL anti-pattern per § 4.3: never collapse to "Native American" label. Some tribal star-knowledge is sacred-restricted (ceremonial Navajo and Lakota astronomical knowledge specifically carries transmission restrictions in published institutional guidance).
**Protocol:** Per-tribe ONLY. Source ONLY from: Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian + tribal cultural-heritage offices' publicly-accessible records + academic ethnoastronomy with explicit tribal-permission framing. If tribe-specific source marks knowledge as restricted, note here and exclude.
**Tribes with documented public ethnoastronomy:** Pawnee (Chief Star Chart — publicly documented), Cherokee (public astronomical tradition), Anishinaabe (Manidoominensag Pleiades — publicly documented), Cree (Fisher Stars — academically documented). Lakota and Navajo ceremonial astronomical knowledge carries higher restrictions; source only from publicly-published academic context.
**Source authorization:** NMAI + academic ethnoastronomy with explicit tribal-engagement framing.

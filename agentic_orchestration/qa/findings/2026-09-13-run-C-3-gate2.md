- **The rule it re-scopes is explicit.** Charter § 8: *"HALTs (to Matt; the run does not decide)"*. Charter § 13: *"HALT conditions write a HALT packet and stop"*. Matt's GO (C-1 ledger R-57, consequence field): *"Matt is not present during the run: HALT conditions write a HALT packet and stop."* desirable-run-pattern § 4 classes *"his named halt rules"* as **commitment-boundary HALTs — keep forever**.

R-C3-9 also frames the question as *"if Matt reads § 8 as a whole-run stop"*. That framing understates it: § 13 and R-57 are not ambiguous.
- **Resources consumed after the halt cannot be refunded by a veto.** 16 of the run's 24 Astra images were spent after `06:07:25Z` (all of P5). `images_used` = 24 = the sum of `bursts[].image_calls`, which I verified.

The 402 shows the balance is metered. The 11 clips over estimate consumed the balance that then ran out. R-C3-9 and the HALT packet do not revisit that premise. If this precedent stands, any future conductor can re-scope any halt it can argue is resource-class. Principle 5 applies: a BLOCK here is cheap, and the precedent is not.

## 2. R-C3-1 — derived facing table over the R-43 E geometry — **HOLDS (INFO)**

### What I found
- **The physics is correct.** Take screen-right = +x, up = +y, toward the viewer = +z. For a figure facing +x, right = front × up = x̂ × ŷ = +ẑ, which points toward the camera. A right-handed staff therefore sits in the NEAR hand on E, and the left-hip satchel is FAR. Check with the S pose: front = +z gives right = ẑ × ŷ = −x, which is screen-left, matching C-1 R-15 (*"her right hand is on screen-left"*).
- **The quoted evidence is verbatim.** `briefs/K2p-gen-E.task.json` contains *"only its left side is visible; the satchel on the left hip is toward the camera"*. `briefs/K2p-jdg-E.task.json` contains *"she faces screen-right, so her LEFT side is NEAR the camera and her RIGHT side is FAR"*. Both are inverted against `runs/C-1/k2_facing_table_derived.json` (E: *"her RIGHT arm and the staff are in FRONT, nearest the camera"*), and `runs/C-3/k2c_facing_table.json` agrees with the derived table.
- **The eye check agrees.** `artifacts/P2p-turnaround/turnaround_strip.png`: S has the staff screen-left and the satchel screen-right. E, SE and NE have the staff in front and the satchel hidden. W, SW and NW have the satchel near and the staff behind. N has the staff screen-right. All are consistent with the derived table.
- **Authority.** R-43 was *"Matt (question) + gandalf (conductor)"*. Matt asked the question and the conductor, advised by a judge given the inverted brief, answered it. R-C3-1 therefore corrects a conductor ruling, not a Matt geometry ruling. However, charter fork E's text (*"judge-verified with the R-43 geometry"*) is in the Matt-ruled fork table, so Matt still needs to ratify. That is correctly placed on the C-4 front page.
- **INFO: the judge is not independent evidence here.** K2c-jdg-01 was given the derived table, which is the priming hazard R-15 itself names. Its independent tell is the hidden control, mirror(W) claimed as E, which it caught (mirrored true, light 2). That control separates a *pixel mirror* (inverted light) from a true frame. It cannot separate a *generatively* wrong-hand body with correct light, which is exactly what an R-43-geometry mint would be. So "geometry 5/5" confirms consistency with the brief. The physics derivation is what settles derived vs R-43.

The C-1 conductor scripts (`seed.py`, `mirror.py`, `wave.sh`) are orchestration and image composition; none is an estimator. `paint_sheet.py` and `pad_seed.py` fit the `seed.py` precedent; the four scripts above do not. The lane's "instrument before candidate" freeze protocol exists precisely so that measurements come from tested, frozen instruments.

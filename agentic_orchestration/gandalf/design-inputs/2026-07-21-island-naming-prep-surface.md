## 1. THE REGISTER — what a name must fit (read before the candidates)

The project already has a **ratified naming lexicon** (story-keystone § 18). It establishes the death-faith register with worked examples and — critically — a **built-in vocabulary-commonness discipline**. The registers below are drawn FROM that lexicon so the island names sit in the same world as the title, the creed, and the cosmology:

| Register | The lexicon's own precedent | What it evokes |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Latin liturgical** | *Mete. Morere. Resurge.* (the holy words); *Pax / Quies / Lethea* (the creed's dream) | the major cult's crusade-tongue; temple inscription; the sacred register |
| **B — Necro-geographic** | *the Necroverse / Necropolis / Necrocosm* | the necromancer-cosmos grit; place-in-the-world naming |
| **C — plain-mechanical-descriptive** | the working labels themselves (MELEE-STRIKE, etc.); the axis names PERFORM/DEPLOY/EMBODY/LAUNCH | honest, legible, no lore tax — what the axis IS |
| **D — genre-homage** | (none in-lexicon; new) D3 set names, PoE keystone names, LE mastery names | a nod to the ARPG canon these kits come FROM |

**The vocabulary-commonness law (RULED HISTORY — apply it as a hard filter).** The project has demoted obscure vocabulary before: `pall` / `miasma` / `rime` were demoted from element names for average-player-vocabulary failure (MEMORY.md, D1 element-pool 2026-05-12), and § 18 itself cautions "'Necro-' is saturated in games" and explains *Pax* works ONLY "because it's immediately legible as 'peace' to any player." **The test:** would an average ARPG player read this name and know roughly what it means, or does it send them to a dictionary? Latin liturgical names carry the highest risk here — I mark every candidate that trips it with **⚠ VOCAB-RISK**.

**One structural truth from the data that shapes naming (survey-mode — what IS):** the atlas islands are **minority coasts around a generic mainland** (archipelago-mock: named cores = 28% of kits; 27% are "drifters" resembling no family). Island names should read as *distinctive territories*, not as an exhaustive taxonomy of all builds — a name like "The Common Field" would misdescribe the geography. The named islands are the *interesting* places.

## 3. FORK 1 (for Matt) — one grammatical system, or vary per island?

**The question:** should all eleven island names share a single grammatical/register system, or should each island get whichever register fits it best?

| Option | What it looks like | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **1A — one system, all geographic-poetic** | The Maelstrom · The Standing Stones · The Deadfall · The Conduit · The Nimbus · The Molting · The Wasting · The Volley · The Legion · The Kindling · The Close Ground | **PRO:** reads as ONE coherent map — an atlas of named territories, which is literally what the archipelago metaphor promises; the "The ___" cadence unifies. **CON:** forces some islands into a weaker word than their best-fit register would give. |
| **1B — one system, all Latin liturgical** | Manus · Custos · Laqueus · Radius · Aura · Versipellis · Tabes · Sagitta · Legio · Furor · Turbo | **PRO:** maximal death-faith gravitas; reads as the crusade's own map. **CON:** **fails the vocabulary-commonness law wholesale** — most of these are dictionary-trips (Versipellis, Tabes, Laqueus, Custos, Sagitta); Turbo and Radius actively collide with modern meanings. The ruled history (pall/rime demotion) argues HARD against this. Not recommended as a full system. |
| **1C — one system, all plain-mechanical** | The Melee Coast · The Meter · The Molt · The Blight · The Volley · The Legion · The Spin · The Emplacement · The Snare · The Channel · The Field | **PRO:** zero vocab tax; maximally legible; honest to the axes. **CON:** flavorless — squanders the death-faith register the project spent § 18 establishing; an atlas UI of plain labels reads like a spreadsheet, not a world. |
| **1D — vary per island (register follows mechanic)** | pick the best-fit register per island from § 2 (e.g., The Molting [necro] · The Standing Stones [geographic] · The Kindling [keystone-echo] · The Volley [plain] · The Legion [martial-Latin]) | **PRO:** every island gets its strongest name; the death-faith register appears where it lands hardest and steps back where plain wins. **CON:** less visually-uniform as a set; risks feeling ad-hoc unless a light through-line (e.g., all "The ___") is imposed. |

**My lean: 1A or 1D with a shared "The ___" prefix as the unifying through-line.** The archipelago metaphor WANTS geographic coherence (these are islands on a map), so the "The [Territory]" cadence unifies even when the register underneath varies (1D). Pure Latin (1B) is disqualified by the project's own vocabulary law. Pure-plain (1C) wastes the register. **The strongest single system is 1A geographic-poetic** — it seats every island as a place, fits the archipelago surface natively, and carries death-faith weight (The Wasting, The Deadfall, The Molting) without the Latin vocab tax. **But Matt rules** — the register-coherence-vs-best-fit tradeoff is exactly the taste call reserved for the sitting.

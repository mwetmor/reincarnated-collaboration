## §1 — MATH NOTE

### 1.1 — The GD composition, exact (all M)

**Source (EXT-A §4.1, EXT-B §3.2/§3.4; `records/game/gameengine.dbr`, `parameters_character.tpl`,
`controllermonster.tpl`):**

```
rate(actor) = characterAttackSpeed                       # creature/PC record, animation-rate multiplier
            × (1 + characterBaseAttackSpeed)             # WEAPON record only; signed offset, more-negative = slower
            × (1 + Σ characterAttackSpeedModifier / 100) # ALL sources SUMMED, not chained
            × (0.5 if dual-wielding else 1.0)            # gameengine.dwWeaponSpeedFactor

rate_pct   = 100 × rate, CLAMPED:  player [20, 200] · monster [20|30|40, 500] · boss [50, 500]

seconds_per_swing(actor) = T_base(actor) / rate(actor)                      # players
                         = T_base(actor) / rate(actor) + U(minSwingPause,   # monsters: the
                                                           maxSwingPause)   #  controller's AI idle
```

`T_base` — the absolute animation length in seconds — **is not in the corpus.** EXT-A §4.2 / CA-2
and EXT-B's gap ledger both terminate the probe at the same wall: `anm_*.dbr` carries 725/906
fields, all dimensionless multipliers and weights; a four-archive field-name sweep for any absolute
attack interval returns only slow-debuff durations, the `specialAttack*Timeout` family, and
`timeBetweenAttacks`; the durations live in `resources/Animations.arc`, and the pinned corpus ships
no `.arc` but `Text_EN`. **`T_base` is named build assumption A-M4-1 (§Z.2), grade D, measurer
named.**

### 1.2 — D-3 ADJUDICATED (WR1 §8.7): the two extractions do not conflict — they measured different terms

EXT-B answers monster cadence via the controller's `minSwingPause`/`maxSwingPause` (Primordian
**0.30 / 0.40 s**); EXT-A grades the swing interval CANNOT-ANSWER. **Both are right, and M-4 needs
both.** `swing_pause` is the AI's idle *between* swings; `T_base / rate` is the swing *itself*.
EXT-B says so explicitly (§3.4: *"the pause term is fully M"*, the animation term is U).

> **R-M4-1 (veto-open) — D-3 resolves as ADDITIVE DECOMPOSITION, not contradiction.** The monster
> attack interval is `T_base/rate + U(minSwingPause, maxSwingPause)`. The pause term is MEASURED and
> ships now; the animation term is A-M4-1 and ships as a declared constant. The `CANNOT-ANSWER`
> grade attaches to `T_base` alone and must not be quoted against the pause.

### 1.3 — A contradiction INSIDE EXT-A, named (small, but it is a spec's job to catch these)

EXT-A §4.1 assembles the modifier term as `(1 + Σ%AS/100)` — **additive summation across sources**,
which matches the field's own semantics (*"summed from every source"*). EXT-A §4.4 then reports
Primordian under `primordian_icearmor` as *"effective **1.215** for 12 s"*, which is
`0.900 × 1.35` — **multiplicative chaining** of the same two modifiers the formula says to sum.

Additive: `1.00 × (1 + (−10 + 35)/100) = **1.250**`. Multiplicative: `**1.215**`. Gap **2.88 %**.

> **R-M4-2 (veto-open) — the ADDITIVE form governs; EXT-A §4.4's 1.215 is an erratum.** Grounds:
> (a) EXT-A's own assembled formula, (b) the field is a percent modifier and GD sums percent
> modifiers of a class, (c) EXT-B §3.4 reports the pak modifier as an array of percents, not a
> factor. **Non-load-bearing** on any gate (2.88 % on a 12-in-44 s window on one actor); recorded so
> a builder does not silently pick the other one. Confirmable by legolas at zero cost if desired.

### 1.5 — Two independent corroborations that `T_base` is near 1 s, and one falsification of the current model

**(i) The cap falsifies the current model outright (M).** `playerAttackSpeedCapMax = 200.0`
(`gameengine.dbr`, both extractions). The fastest a GD player may swing is `T_base / 2.0`. The sim
runs 0.100 s. **The sim's player cadence is outside Grim Dawn's own legal range for every
`T_base > 0.200 s`** — i.e. unless a werewolf claw swing takes less than a fifth of a second, the
sim is not merely mis-tuned, it is off the game's dial. Nothing in the corpus suggests 0.2 s: the
controller pause census spans 0.10–1.80 s and `combatIdleTime = 1.2 s`.

**(ii) The death-window duty cycle (grade C — consistency check, NOT a measurement).** KC1 §14.17,
frames 280→281, 81 s of wall time: **+19 claws, +9 charge, +59 kills.** Wall time includes travel,
looting and menu, so this bounds a *duty cycle*, never a cadence — stated so it cannot be misused.
At the spec's 0.8889 s/press, 19 presses = **16.9 s** of swinging in 81 s = **20.8 % combat duty
cycle**, ordinary for ARPG play. At the sim's 0.100 s, 19 presses = **1.9 s** = **2.3 %** duty cycle
— 59 kills in under two seconds of swinging. **The current model is not merely fast; it is
implausible against the fixture's own press census.**

**(iii) Where this shows up in the band table (VER §A-5.1, quoted not re-derived).** Removing the
arc geometry moved sim `A 1.7143 → 1.4150` and `B 1.0769 → 1.2714` at constant kills — *"both are
readings of the same thing: how many kills land inside one 0.5 s bin."* At 10 presses/s, a 0.5 s bin
holds **five** player presses. At 1.125 presses/s it holds **0.56**. The bin-occupancy statistic is
the cadence, seen sideways.

**What changes**

**What stays frozen**

> **R-M4-4 (veto-open) — `TICK_SIZE` is NOT TOUCHED.** The 0.100 s quantum is engine-wide
> (`spatial_engine.py:110`, *"matches 1D fight_engine.py"*). Retuning it to serve a GD-fidelity
> calibration would move every timed statistic in every battery the engine has ever run. M-4 lands
> entirely inside the scenario layer's cadence values. **Changing `TICK_SIZE` is a HALT, not a build
> decision.** Note the corollary: with a 0.100 s quantum, cadences quantize — 0.8889 s resolves to
> 9 ticks (0.900 s), a **1.25 % rounding**, which is below A-M4-2's own 11 % band and therefore
> immaterial. Declare it; do not chase it.

**Discipline #12 surface, named loudly:** this is the single largest behavioural change in the wave.
Player DPS falls ~8.9× (press rate 10.0 → 1.125). **Every pre-wave `_fix3` figure becomes
non-poolable with every post-wave figure** — the SS-1 non-poolability lesson recurring for the third

to move off 150/150. **A wave that lands M-4 and still reports 150/150 wins at unchanged kill-times
has not landed M-4** — that is the strongest smoke test available and it costs one battery cell.

### 9.2 — Why link 3 is FREE, and this is the whole argument for the landing

`resolve_spatial_hit` calls `resolve_skill` **unconditionally and identically** and only then
branches on the flag:

```
total_damage, _events = resolve_skill(...)        # spatial_resolver_adapter.py:459-462
if return_events:                                 # :464
    return float(total_damage), _events
return float(total_damage)                        # :465
```

> **R-M6-1 (veto-open) — turning `return_events=True` on at the player offense site consumes no RNG,
> adds no branch before the roll, and CANNOT move a number.** The events list is constructed on
> every call today and discarded. This is a *return-shape* change, not a behavioural one. It is
> therefore correct to land it **UNGATED** (no new flag), unlike link 4. **Gate-2's acceptance test
> is byte-identity of the full battery digest** — if any digest moves, R-M6-1 is falsified and the
> landing halts rather than being explained.

*(Note the asymmetry with the mob→player site: that one IS flag-gated, because Wave-C dispatch does
downstream work off the events. M-6 does no downstream work — it labels.)*

### 9.3 — What the label is worth, stated with the numbers rather than asserted

- **N-4 / N-5 become crit-decomposable.** Both are damage-tempo bands (VER §A-6: N-5 **MISS** at
  1.14 vs the fixture's 2.41; N-4 CUT-DEPENDENT). The fixture side has a crit count — **66 crits**
  (KC1 §14.16) — and the sim side currently has *none observable*, so the comparison cannot be
  decomposed at all. That is the block VER §A-8.1 row 6 records.
- **G-C gains a real presentation channel.** The baton's consumer renders floaters (KF-5's
  `pct`/`expected` fields exist for exactly that). A crit flag is the difference between a number
  and a *beat* — and in this genre the crit tell is load-bearing: Diablo II's yellow crit numerals
  and Diablo III's larger/orange crit floaters are the canonical example of a purely presentational
  label doing combat-legibility work. drax cannot render what the trace does not carry.
- **M-8b is unbuildable without it** (R-X-4).

### 9.4 — Schema additivity: the precedent is on file, use it

KF-5 (2026-07-23) added five gauge keys to the `damage` event as **v1-additive, no schema bump**
(`replica_frame_emitter.py:264-288`, math note `kf5-expected-pct-2026-07-23.md`). M-6 adds
`crit: bool` (and `crit_multiplier: float|None`) by the same rule: present on every `damage` record,
never NaN/inf, `False`/`None` where unknowable (DoT ticks — a DoT tick is not attributed to a
discrete cast and cannot crit; `dot()` already carries the gauge keys as explicit nulls for exactly
this reason). **`replica-frame/v1` stays v1.**

> **R-M6-2 (veto-open) — a MIGRATION entry is OWED to drax even though the schema does not bump.**
> The baton is a cross-seam artifact and drax's renderer is the other consumer. The
> `TelegraphSpec`-MIGRATION precedent (parallel spec set, WR1 §8.8) applies: additive-but-consumed
> fields get an entry. A silent additive field is how a consumer ends up rendering a default.

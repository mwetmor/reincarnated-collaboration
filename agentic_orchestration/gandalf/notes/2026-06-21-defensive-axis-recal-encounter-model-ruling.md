## PART 2 — ENCOUNTER-MODEL SHAPE RULING (the recal wave's design input)

This is the forward-looking part. gamora found a knob-set that hits a target; I now rule whether that target IS the design intent, and rule the surrounding encounter-model shape so the production wave calibrates against a RULED target.

### 2.1 RULING — calibration TARGETS: RATIFIED (with one named refinement)

**The graded shape `glass cannon ~0.6–0.8 survive+kill, bruiser ~0.95+` is RATIFIED as the design target.** Reasoning, by genre anchor:

- **The spread BETWEEN the poles is the entire point.** A glass cannon that survives 1.0 is the current blindness; a glass cannon that survives 0.0 is over-punished (the D4-launch one-shot meta we are explicitly avoiding). ~0.6–0.8 is "viable but high-variance" — the **D3 speed-GR glass wizard** and the **PoE one-shot-or-be-one-shot assassin**: both ship, both are *good*, both DIE MORE. The bruiser at ~0.95+ is "safe but slower" — the genre's legitimate outlast-the-boss tank. That spread is the defensive axis the instrument can finally see. Ratified.
- **REFINEMENT (named, not a rejection):** the *exact* glass landing point inside 0.6–0.8 is a **TUNE-IN-WAVE decision, not a fixed constant I rule now**, because gamora's band is somewhat sharp at the throwaway fixture (0.82 safe → 0.72 all-dead over ~0.10 armor; flat mob hits, single HP fixture). The recal wave must land the glass cannon in the **HEART of the band (~0.70) at FULL POPULATION**, not at the throwaway's knife-edge. The width of the realized band depends on two things the throwaway didn't carry: (a) the population HP SPREAD (a real kit population spreads across the ~0.60-armor headroom rather than bunching at one HP), and (b) whether mob hits carry PER-HIT VARIANCE (currently flat — a flat hit makes a sharp cliff; variance softens it into a graded slope). **RULING: the target is the graded band ~0.6–0.8 for the glass POLE; the recal wave is authorized to add per-hit mob-damage variance and/or rely on population HP-spread to soften the cliff into a genuine grade — that is design-intended, not a fixture artifact to engineer around.** A sharp cliff (binary safe/dead over 0.10 armor) would be a HOMOGENIZATION failure in disguise (it collapses "how much defense" into a single threshold); a soft grade is the 2D axis. The softening IS part of the target.

### 2.2 RULING — gate-SEMANTICS: the boss gate becomes a REAL two-clause gate (survive AND kill, both graded), and a kit's disposition is now a TWO-AXIS shape

**RULING: the boss gate is now `survive AND kill`, both clauses LIVE and both GRADED.** It was running in a degenerate 1D mode (survive vacuous → effectively kill-within-enrage only). Making death real activates the survive limb the adopted doctrine ALWAYS claimed — nothing in the encounter-measurement doctrine reverses; the dormant half wakes. The gate becomes a genuine **2D race**: out-DPS the 240s enrage AND out-survive the boss.

**A kit's disposition is now a point in a 2D plane, not a 1D band:**

- **Axis 1 — OFFENSE (the banked provisional bands):** kill-before-enrage. Already measured; already banked provisional.
- **Axis 2 — DEFENSE (the recal restores):** survive-to-kill. The recal makes this a real, varying signal.
- **A kit's disposition is the JOINT outcome.** Four corners of the plane:
  - **Burst-glass** (high offense, low defense): survives-by-killing-FAST — passes via Axis-1 dominance buying Axis-2. The D3 speed-GR fantasy. PASSES, high-variance.
  - **Tank-bruiser** (low offense, high defense): survives-by-ENDURING, kills slow-but-in-time. The outlast fantasy. PASSES, safe.
  - **Balanced** (mid/mid): passes both clauses with margin. The default-safe profile.
  - **Paper-AND-slow** (low offense, low defense): FAILS — can't kill in time AND can't survive. Correctly told "too fragile and too weak for this boss." This corner is the one the 1D instrument was blind to and the one that SHOULD fail.

**Composition with the banked offensive bands (Matt's close-disposition (b) — two-axis tail-refit):** the four offensive FIXES (mechanism) stand — they are independent of death. But the BANDS are fit over survive+kill outcomes, and making death real CHANGES every kit's survive+kill rate (a kit that killed-in-time may now die first and fail). So **the offensive bands are correctly banked PROVISIONAL and re-rate over the joint two-axis outcome.** This is NOT a regression — it folds into the workstream's own "single tail-refit" discipline: the tail now includes the defensive axis, and bands fit ONCE over BOTH axes. The provisional bands re-rate; they don't re-open. **RULING on the band-fit ordering: bands finalize ONCE, jointly, over both axes — not offense-then-defense in two separate refits.** That preserves the tail-refit discipline's intent and prevents a double re-bank.

### 2.3 RULING — the two carried Gate-2 concerns (clear-shell death channel + trash-vs-boss ordering)

These are encounter-model questions, my lane. Both are about whether clear shells (trash/packs) carry death at all, and how.

**(a) Clear-shell death channel — coverage-pressure is a WEAK lever vs fast-AOE; does it need its own mechanism?**

gamora verified: a circle-AOE glass kit covers + clears the swarm in ~17s before coverage-bleed accrues → survives 1.000 across the full coverage sweep (cov_per_mob 8→150). Coverage punishes POINT/MELEE/SLOW kits, not fast-AOE.

**RULING: clear-shell death is IN SCOPE but RARE-BY-DESIGN, and it is NOT delivered by coverage-pressure alone.** The reasoning is the homogenization guard, applied to trash:

- That a fast-AOE kit clears the swarm before it can be hurt is **the §11.3 guard working as designed on the clear shells** — clearing-fast IS a defensive strategy (offense substituting for defense), exactly as on the boss. I do NOT want to "fix" coverage so that even a fast-AOE kit dies to trash; that would re-introduce a mandatory defensive floor on clear shells (the PoE capped-resist failure, on trash). So coverage-pressure punishing only slow/point/melee kits is **correct, not broken.**
- BUT there must EXIST a clear-shell death channel that bites a kit that is genuinely under-defended AND can't clear fast — the **D3 "I got swarmed and melted" moment.** Coverage alone is the wrong delivery mechanism for the fast-AOE case (it can't reach them, by design); the right one is a channel that doesn't depend on the kit's clear-shape. **RULING for the recal wave: clear-shell death needs its OWN mechanism review — candidates: per-hit mob-damage variance (burst spikes that a high-HP kit absorbs and a paper kit doesn't), or a small fraction of not-fully-coverable threats (a ranged/elite mob the AOE doesn't blanket). NOT a coverage-pressure crank.** The wave evaluates which delivers a rare-but-real swarm-punish without a mandatory floor. If the wave finds no clean mechanism that respects the guard, the fallback is **boss-only death (clear shells carry no death), explicitly logged** — see (b).

**(b) Trash-vs-boss death-risk ORDERING — must clear shells carry death at all, and if so what's the ordering?**

The danger: standard-armor (0.85, 15% taken) and mob-scale must be re-derived JOINTLY for clear shells, or — because clear shells carry 3× the boss's per-hit damage — a boss-only patch makes **trash deadlier than the boss in raw damage but, if the boss death channel is restored and clear shells aren't, the boss becomes the deadlier-in-OUTCOME encounter while trash stays safe.** Either direction, an un-jointly-derived patch creates an INVERSION.

**RULING on the ordering: the boss must be the PEAK death-risk encounter; clear shells carry LOWER, rare death-risk; the gradient runs trash < packs < elite < boss in OUTCOME death-risk.** Reasoning by genre anchor: in D2/D3/PoE the boss is the *test* — the moment the build is proven or found wanting. Trash is the *texture* — it should occasionally punish genuine recklessness (the swarm-melt) but it is not the gate. If trash is deadlier-in-outcome than the boss, the encounter rhythm inverts (you fear the hallway, not the throne room) — that is the D4-launch "every white mob is a threat" meta we are avoiding. So:

- **The recal wave MUST re-derive `PLAYER_ARMOR_FACTOR_VS_STANDARD` + `MOB_DAMAGE_SCALE` JOINTLY for clear shells alongside the boss knobs** — this is a hard constraint, not optional. A boss-only knob-set is forbidden because it would either leave clear shells safe (boss-only death, acceptable as the logged fallback) OR, if mob-scale is global, accidentally make trash lethal (inversion, forbidden).
- **The ordering target:** boss = the graded death gate (the ~0.6–0.8 glass / ~0.95+ bruiser spread). Clear shells = rare-but-real death for genuinely under-defended-AND-slow kits only (the swarm-punish), with fast-clearers passing by clear-speed (guard-as-designed). Trash death-rate STRICTLY below boss death-rate for every kit profile.
- **If the wave cannot deliver clear-shell death without violating the guard or the ordering, the ruled fallback is BOSS-ONLY DEATH** (clear shells carry no death channel), logged explicitly as a scope decision — NOT shipped as silent texture. Boss-only death is a coherent, genre-defensible model (only the gate carries the test); it is the safe fallback if the rare-but-real clear-shell channel proves un-deliverable inside the guard.

### 2.4 RULING — proxy Wave-3 inheritance: CONFIRMED, it inherits the real death channel

The proxy packet §4 asked, for the summoner boss: "grade the proxy boss on the clear-time band OR add a player-death channel." **That question is now ANSWERED for solo by Matt's (B) ruling: death is real.**

**RULING: proxy Wave-3 INHERITS the real death channel — it does NOT re-decide A-vs-B.** The summoner question is no longer a fresh encounter-model fork; it narrows to a calibration INSIDE an answered model: "how does the summoner army's defensive profile (proxy/minion survivability + caster-self exposure) read against the SAME real death channel solo now carries?" The proxy boss is graded on the SAME two-axis gate (survive AND kill, both graded). One fewer open design fork downstream. The proxy packet §4's "OR" collapses to "AND, inherited." knight-rider/Matt do not re-rule this at Wave-3; they calibrate the summoner's specific defensive profile against the inherited channel.

### 2.5 RULING — the homogenization guard is a RULED CONSTRAINT on the recal wave (not aspirational)

My §11.3 PRIME CONSTRAINT, now verified satisfiable (§1.2 above), is **a ruled constraint the recal wave's calibration MUST preserve, not merely aim at:**

- **No mandatory defensive floor.** The wave may NOT calibrate to a knob-set where the only way to pass is to clear one identical armor/HP bar. gamora's guard sweep is the acceptance test: at the chosen production knob-set, a fast kit must survive-by-killing where a slow kit of identical HP/armor dies (offense partially substitutes), AND the bruiser must survive-by-enduring at the same knob-set. Two distinct viable paths, verified, or the knob-set is rejected.
- **Multiple defensive strategies viable:** mitigation (armor) OR avoidance (clear-speed) OR sustain (self-heal inflow) OR kill-speed (offense) — at least the endure-path and the kill-fast-path must both pass at the production knob-set.
- **This is the anti-PoE-capped-resist / anti-D4-launch-one-shot guard.** A death channel that forces every build to the same armor number is a defect dressed as the fix. The wave carries this as an ACCEPTANCE CRITERION, not a hope.

## 4. Player consequence (the anchor)

Before: a player invests in a defensively-fragile kit, the sim blesses it (offensively in-band, kills in time), and the build is a lie the moment a real death channel exists — the inverse of the D4-launch "wet noodle": not "my damage feels weak" but "the game told me this works and then killed me for a choice it never warned me about." After the recal: the glass cannon is correctly told "viable but you'll die more — play sharp"; the bruiser is told "safe but slow — outlast it"; the paper-AND-slow kit is correctly failed; and CRUCIALLY, no build is forced to one armor number (the guard), so the player who wants to trade defense for kill-speed CAN, and the instrument SEES it. That is the genre's second axis restored without the genre's most common "death matters" failure (homogenization). The boss becomes the test it was always dressed as; trash stays texture; the encounter rhythm runs the right direction (fear the throne room, not the hallway).

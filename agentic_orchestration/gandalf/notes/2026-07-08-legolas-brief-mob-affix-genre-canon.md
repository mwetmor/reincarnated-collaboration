## 2. Research question

**What is the complete design canon of ARPG monster-modifier (affix) systems — per game, what the affixes ARE, how they're telegraphed, named, budgeted, combined, constrained, and coupled to loot — and what did each studio learn (including by failure)?**

## 3. Per-game canon required (primary thread)

For EACH game below: (a) full or near-full affix/mod list with mechanical effect; (b) rarity/tier structure (how many mods per monster rarity class); (c) telegraph conventions (visual/audio language per mod); (d) naming conventions (how the name communicates the mechanic); (e) loot coupling (does affix count/type drive drops); (f) design lessons from patches, dev posts, postmortems.

1. **Diablo II / D2R** — champion types (Extra Strong, Extra Fast, Cursed, Magic Resistant, Fire/Lightning/Cold Enchanted, Mana Burn, Multiple Shots, Aura Enchanted, Stone Skin, Spectral Hit, Teleportation, Thief…) + unique-monster mod stacking + the boss-pack minion inheritance rule. The genre floor — get the exact list right.
2. **Diablo III** — the elite affix pool across its life (Waller, Jailer, Arcane Enchanted, Molten, Plagued, Desecrator, Frozen, Vortex, Fire Chains, Orbiter, Thunderstorm, Illusionist, Shielding, Health Link, Wormhole, Juggernaut…): which are **spatial choreography** (dance-with-the-ground) vs stat inflation; the **Reflects Damage removal** and why; CC-resistance scaling; affix count by rarity (champion pack vs rare + minions) and by difficulty/GR tier.
3. **Diablo IV** — the deliberately smaller, harder-telegraphed pool; Elites/affix rework across seasons; what D4 dropped from D3 and the stated reasoning.
4. **Path of Exile — with the Archnemesis case study IN DEPTH.** Pre-AN rare mods → AN league (3.17) recipe system → AN-goes-core (3.18) → community revolt (mod opacity, loot-conversion mods, "ele-reflect on sight" class problems) → dismantling (3.19–3.20 back to simpler mods). This is the genre's best-documented **over-complexification failure**; extract the specific failure axes (readability, mod-stacking lethality spikes, reward opacity).
5. **Last Epoch** — rare mods + shrine/echo modifiers; what a modern mid-size studio kept minimal and why.
6. **Grim Dawn** — hero monsters carrying **item-grammar prefix/suffix affixes** + the **Nemesis** faction-vengeance system (named hunters spawned by reputation) — the strongest precedent for our RIVAL-class thread.
7. **Torchlight II** + **Diablo Immortal** — briefly: T2 champion mods; DI's mobile compromises (what survives at 3-minute session grain).

## 4. Cross-cutting syntheses required

- **A. Affix taxonomy** — cluster the union of all mods into functional families (damage-add / defense / terrain-denial / displacement / summon-spawn / resource-attack / player-debuff / on-death). This becomes the design session's axis skeleton.
- **B. Telegraph grammar** — how each game makes a mod READABLE pre-engagement (name plate, VFX color, ground decal, audio sting). D7 AI-tell line applies downstream: our names/flavor will be substrate-derived, so the grammar matters more than the strings.
- **C. Roll budgets** — mods-per-rarity tables across games; lethality-stacking guards (banned pairs, mutually-exclusive families, budget caps).
- **D. Affix↔loot coupling** — where drop quantity/quality reads off the mod count (D2 boss packs, PoE rare rarity bonuses, AN loot-conversion failure).
- **E. Difficulty scaling** — how affix pools widen/deepen by tier (D3 Torment/GR gating, PoE map tiers) — maps onto our threat-tier axis.

## 5. Secondary thread (lighter): named-monster / rival conventions

Isekai + ARPG named-encounter canon feeding the ruled **RIVAL class** (E9-C: kit-built encounters sourced from certified-non-rostered kits — "other reaped souls on the same descent"): PoE rogue exiles (the direct mechanical precedent — player-skill-built hostiles), Grim Dawn Nemesis spawn/announce rules, D3 keywardens; isekai named-calamity conventions (floor-boss naming in SAO, gate/rank grammar in Solo Leveling, named-evolution in Slime). One section, not exhaustive — enough to give the design session a naming/announcement grammar.

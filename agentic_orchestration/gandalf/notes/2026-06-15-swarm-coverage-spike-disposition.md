## 1. Disposition of the architectural hypothesis — CONFIRMED + RELOCATED (the correct reading)

The dispatch's reading is correct, and I sharpen it on the data:

**What is now CONFIRMED (on evidence, not silence):**
- **The single-global-modifier limitation is REAL.** A glass single-target rogue kit, even with the role floor intact AND a working swarm-coverage cost biting the encounter geometry, cannot be shaped by one global modifier to clear both swarm and the upper tiers simultaneously. This was the hypothesis (§ 6-quinquies (f), § 6-octies (c)) and it is now demonstrated, not merely inferred from a hot-swarm crater.
- **The lever is a real lever, not a proxy.** The §2 anti-AoE-proxy falsifier was the decisive design check, and it held under jack-ryan's independent code verification. The ON/OFF deltas prove the mechanic acts on the actual variable: with it ON, swarm fell off the 1.0 ceiling (−0.167) and the modifier lifted off the deep floor (0.0719→0.1333, +0.061). A dead lever would have produced zero delta. This one produced motion. **We probed the right variable.** That alone makes the spike a success: it eliminated "the swarm tier was just position-naive" as a sufficient explanation.

**What is RELOCATED (the genuinely new fact):**
- **The crater is on the upper-tier side, not the swarm side.** This is gamora's pre-registered HONEST_FAIL shape #2 in substance: *"swarm comes into band but upper tiers STILL don't clear → the crater was NOT solely swarm-suppression."* One precision note for honesty: the harness emitted the `shape1` enum because swarm at 0.833 sits just ABOVE the 0.80 ceiling (it did not land strictly INSIDE [0.65,0.80]), so prong (a) failed on the high side rather than passing. **But the LESSON of shape #2 fired regardless of which enum the band-edge fell on**: the controlled experiment (swarm pushed from 1.0 to 0.833, modifier lifted from 0.0719 to 0.1333) demonstrates that *even with the swarm confound substantially removed, elite/mini_boss/boss do not move off 0.0.* The deeper upper-tier throughput gap that shape #2 was designed to expose is exposed. The exact-band-clearance of swarm is a tuning question; the upper-tier zero is a structural one. **Do not let the `shape1` enum label obscure that shape #2's diagnostic content is what we got.**

**What remains OPEN:**
- WHERE in the upper-tier-throughput chain the zero comes from (kit-side, balance-architecture-side, or both — §2 below).
- The caster-side generalization (§ 6-septies). The spike was physical-rogue-cell only. The convergence-tell still holds: the same lever was the re-open criterion for both sides. But the spike does not close the caster question — it sharpens what to look for there (an upper-tier-throughput gap, not a swarm gap).

**Net: the architectural hypothesis is CONFIRMED on the physical side and the problem is RELOCATED to upper-tier throughput. That is the correct reading.**

## 2. Where the upper-tier throughput gap lives — BOTH, and the two are separable (locate, don't prescribe)

A glass single-target kit clears swarm trivially (raw ST throughput against low-HP mobs — my §1 mechanism, unchanged) but craters elite/mini_boss/boss. With swarm now cost-controlled and the modifier still unable to lift the upper tiers off 0.0, the gap is in how the kit performs against high-HP / single-target-resistant upper-tier enemies. **It is both a kit-design issue AND a balance-architecture issue, and the spike just proved they are separable** — which is itself the useful design fact:

**The kit-design half (a REAL ARPG archetype tension, not a bug).** A glass single-target striker is, by genre construction, swarm-strong and boss-fragile-without-investment. This is not a defect to be balanced away — it is the *shape of the archetype*. D3 Demon Hunter pre-itemization was a swarm-shredder that fell apart against rift bosses without specific defensive/burst-sustain investment; PoE glass-cannon ST builds famously delete packs and then die to a single boss slam without flask/defensive-layer tech. The rogue cell genuinely *should* find bosses harder than swarm. The question is whether it lacks the TOOLS to fight a boss at all (a composition gap the role floor was supposed to address — and the floor DID reserve a burst skill) or whether the tools it has are being nullified downstream. The data says the latter: the burst skill is present (`burst_damage:1`) but at modifier 0.1333 it does nothing. **So the kit is not tool-less; its boss-killer tool is being suppressed by the modifier.** That points at the second half.

**The balance-architecture half (the single global modifier cannot EXPRESS this kit).** Here is the structural finding stated cleanly: the single global modifier is asked to do two contradictory things at once for a swarm-strong/boss-weak kit. To bring swarm into band it must push the modifier DOWN (suppress the over-clearing ST throughput); to bring the upper tiers up it must push the modifier UP (so the reserved burst can actually drop a boss). With ONE knob, these fight to the equilibrium the spike found: swarm 0.833 / modifier 0.1333 / upper tiers 0.0 — a point where neither demand is met. **The single global modifier has no degree of freedom to express "strong against swarm, needs help against bosses." It can only scale the whole kit uniformly.** The coverage lever helped — it let the modifier rise from 0.0719 to 0.1333 by taking some swarm-suppression load OFF the modifier — but it could not give the modifier the SECOND degree of freedom the kit's shape requires. **The gap lives at the intersection: a kit whose genre-correct shape (swarm-strong, boss-weak) cannot be expressed by a one-dimensional balance instrument.** Locate it there; do not prescribe the fix.

## 3. The lever-B question — my read CHANGES, with a precise qualification

My §3 disposition flagged per-tier modifiers (lever B) as the band-aid anti-pattern: it passes the sim while leaving the PLAYER experience (ST trivializes swarm) exactly as broken — the D3-pre-RoS "symptom patch you don't understand" lesson. **That read was correct WHEN we did not yet know whether the swarm over-clear was the whole problem.** It is no longer the whole picture, and the spike is what changed it. Here is the refined read:

**The spike RULED OUT the cheap version of lever A.** Coverage pressure was the encounter-side lever that could have fixed the experience (a fast striker now *manages* a swarm rather than auto-deleting it — the genre-correct tension I argued for). It bit, but it could NOT close the gap, because the gap is no longer (only) on the swarm side. **So the encounter-side lever that I argued was the right experience-preserving fix has been tested and found insufficient.** That is a real update, and it costs me the clean "lever A solves it" position.

**This does NOT promote lever B (per-tier modifiers) to "the answer" — it reframes lever B as one of three architecturally-honest responses, and sharpens what makes it band-aid vs. legitimate:**
- Per-tier modifiers applied to a glass ST kit so it passes the gate WHILE the player still auto-deletes swarm = the band-aid I warned against. Still an anti-pattern.
- BUT per-tier modifiers (or, more precisely, a balance instrument with a SECOND degree of freedom) may be the *honest expression of a real archetype shape* — "this kit is genuinely swarm-strong and boss-weak, and the balance system should be ABLE to say that rather than forcing uniform scaling." That is not a band-aid; that is the balance architecture acknowledging a truth the single global modifier structurally cannot represent. **The line between band-aid and legitimate is: does the per-tier shape MATCH the kit's genuine archetype tension (legitimate) or PAPER OVER a degeneracy the player still feels (band-aid)?** For a glass ST rogue, "stronger vs swarm, weaker vs boss" IS the genuine archetype. So a per-tier instrument here is closer to legitimate than I credited it at §3.

**Is there a kit-side / encounter-side lever still un-probed?** Yes — two, and they should be named before any architecture change is committed:
- **(C) Upper-tier kit-throughput investigation** — un-probed. We confirmed the burst tool exists but is suppressed by the modifier; we have NOT confirmed that at a HEALTHY modifier the kit's reserved burst actually drops a boss within the kills-only timeout. If even at modifier 1.0 the rogue's single burst skill cannot kill a boss in time, that is a kit-throughput gap that no modifier architecture fixes — it is a composition/scaling question (does a glass ST kit need more than one burst slot, or a sustained-ST channel, to be boss-viable?). **This is the cheapest un-probed lever and should run BEFORE any balance-architecture conversation** — it tells you whether the problem is "the modifier can't express the kit" (architecture) or "the kit can't kill the boss even unconstrained" (composition). The spike's equilibrium does not distinguish these; a modifier-pinned-high boss-only probe would.
- **(D) Encounter-side upper-tier geometry** — un-probed. The coverage lever attacked the SWARM tier's geometry. The upper tiers (elite/mini_boss/boss) have their own encounter geometry that was untouched. Whether a glass ST kit's boss fight has an experience-correct lever (positional, telegraph-dodge, sustained-DPS-window) is a separate encounter-design question the spike did not touch.

**My refined lever read: the honest answer is most likely "the single-global-modifier architecture cannot express a swarm-strong/boss-weak kit, AND we have not yet confirmed the kit can kill a boss unconstrained" — so probe (C) cheaply FIRST to disambiguate, THEN have the balance-architecture conversation (per-tier / second-degree-of-freedom) if (C) shows the kit CAN kill a boss at a healthy modifier.** The bigger decision (changing the balance-loop architecture) should not fire until (C) rules out the cheaper composition explanation.

## 5. The decision I would surface to Matt — three framed options, the call is his

The spike answered its question precisely: coverage is a real but insufficient lever; the gap is upper-tier throughput under a single global modifier; the architectural limitation is confirmed and located on the boss side. The next decision is Matt's. I frame three options:

**Option 1 — Cheap kit-throughput disambiguation FIRST (my recommended next step).** Run lever (C): pin the modifier high (or unconstrained) and probe whether the floored rogue kit's reserved burst can actually kill elite/mini_boss/boss within the kills-only timeout. ~minutes, no architecture change, no destructive move. This DISAMBIGUATES the §2 finding: if the kit CAN kill a boss at a healthy modifier, the problem is purely architectural (modifier expressiveness — go to Option 2); if it CANNOT, the problem is composition (the glass ST kit needs more boss-killing tooling — a rocket composer question) and no balance-architecture change would have fixed it. **This is the highest-information, lowest-cost, lowest-risk next move and it gates which of the other options is even relevant.** Recommend running it before any larger commitment.

**Option 2 — Balance-architecture conversation (the big one; gated on Option 1).** If (C) shows the kit can kill a boss when not modifier-suppressed, then the single-global-modifier architecture is confirmed as the constraint, and the question becomes: does the balance loop get a second degree of freedom (per-tier or per-tier-band modifiers) to express swarm-strong/boss-weak kits? This is a real architectural decision for Matt — it touches the balance-loop, not content; it is a substrate-led design conversation, not a quick fix; and the band-aid-vs-legitimate line (§3) must be held as the design discipline if it proceeds (per-tier shape must MATCH genuine archetype tension, not paper over degeneracy). Do NOT enter this conversation before Option 1 rules out the composition explanation.

**Option 3 — Accept rogue-as-a-known-limitation-with-b6-retained and move on.** Fully legitimate. b6 carries the tier-completeness net at zero cost; the architectural finding is documented and durable (this note + § 6-novies); the workstream can re-engage the modifier-expressiveness question when there is appetite for a balance-loop change. The recognition→validate→commit discipline is satisfied: we recognized the gap, validated it on evidence (this spike), and the commit (architecture change) is the deferred piece with a NAMED empirical re-open criterion (Option 1's result). This is the move if the balance-architecture conversation is not where the project's energy should go right now — and choosing it does not lose anything, because the finding is captured and b6 holds the line.

**My recommendation: run Option 1 (cheap, gates everything), then let its result tell Matt whether Option 2 (architecture) or a rocket composition probe is the real next gate — and Option 3 is a fully honest place to rest if the appetite for a balance-loop change is not present.** The call on all of it is Matt's; I locate and frame, I do not decide.

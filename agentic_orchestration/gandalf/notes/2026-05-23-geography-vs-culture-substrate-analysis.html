Substrate analysis — geography vs culture (2026-05-23)

Author: gandalf · Data source: weapon_knowledge_entries (dedup-canonical only; N=69,137 of 89,841 total) · Companion: canonical/story/marginal-lineage-tagging-pattern-2026-05-23.md

Headline finding: the cultural_lineage_canonical field empirically captures at least two distinct modes: cultural-tradition-of-origin (Mode A) and geographic-region-of-deployment (Mode B). For marginal lineages (arctic_circumpolar, oceanic, mesoamerican, south_american_indigenous, north_american_indigenous), Mode B + ambiguous-period-modern dominate; Mode A (the intended cultural-tradition meaning) is the minority. This empirically refutes naive use of the lineage tag as "cultural tradition" substrate for downstream design surfaces. Per engineering-disciplines.md Discipline #25 (semantic-layer rep-audit), downstream consumers must rep-audit before inheriting lineage tag as cultural-tradition substrate.

1. Classification methodology — geographic vs cultural proxy

The substrate doesn't have an explicit "geographic vs cultural" flag. This analysis uses register_canonical + historical_period_canonical as proxies to assign each row to one of five modes:

ModeDefinitionInterpretation
Mode A — cultural (pre-industrial historical) register = 'historical' AND period ∈ {pre_classical, classical, medieval, early_modern} Most likely the intended cultural-tradition-of-origin meaning of the lineage tag
Mode AB — ambiguous (modern-period historical) register = 'historical' AND period ∈ {industrial, modern, contemporary} Could be Mode A (modern-made traditional cultural item) or Mode B (modern military item tagged historical-register); requires per-row audit
Mode B — geographic (military-modern) register = 'military_modern' Confirmed modern-military items; lineage tag captures geographic region of origin/deployment, NOT cultural tradition
Mode F — fictional register ∈ {fantasy, sci_fi, mythological} Invented items; lineage tag describes invented aesthetic, not real-world cultural origin
Mode U — period unknown period = 'unknown' (intersects with above) Metadata residue; period-unspecified items often need curation review (e.g., Cluster 90 East Asian Uncurated-Period Metadata Pool)

The proxies are approximate. Mode B is the cleanest signal — military_modern register is explicitly modern-military items. Mode A is reasonably clean for pre-industrial periods. Mode AB (modern-period historical-register) is where the 4-mode collapse hides most artifacts.

8. What emerges — load-bearing recognitions

Recognition 1: The lineage tag is structurally a 4-mode field, not a 1-mode cultural-tradition field. This was hypothesized in canonical/story/marginal-lineage-tagging-pattern-2026-05-23.md and is empirically confirmed at scale. Mode A is the intended cultural-tradition meaning; Modes B/C/D are artifacts that share the tag.
Recognition 2: Marginal lineages are catastrophically Mode-B-dominated. For 4 of 5 marginal lineages, Mode A is the smallest category. The remaining one (north_american_indigenous) is the clean-control case per meta-record because its tag was not cross-contaminated by modern arms-industry catalogs (no major USA-arms-industry tagging convention applies "north_american_indigenous" to modern firearms). The other 4 are contaminated proportionally to their region's modern arms-industry presence.
Recognition 3: Even the major lineages have substantial Mode AB. European has 41% Mode AB (modern-period historical-register; mixed real-history and modern-military). East_asian has 64% Mode U (period-unknown — most rows are uncurated metadata, primarily catalog-ID-format names per Cluster 90 finding). The "cultural tradition" reading of these tags is partial even for the well-covered lineages.
Recognition 4: Phase E-1.5 sensitivity sweep's Mode-B-dominance finding holds across the full substrate, not just clustered subsamples. The 48,430-row v_category_sample showed this pattern at clustering stage; this analysis confirms it at the full 69,137-row dedup-canonical level. The empirical signal is structural.

11. What this analysis does NOT do

  • Does NOT use a precision rep-audit on every row (~69K rows would require LLM-classifier or hand-audit). The proxy method here likely UNDER-estimates Mode B contamination.
  • Does NOT distinguish Mode C (naming-allusion) from Mode B (geographic) — both classify as Mode B in this analysis. Mode C examples (FX-05 Xiuhcoatl rifle named after Aztec serpent-deity) require deeper audit.
  • Does NOT propose specific re-tagging actions per cluster — elrond's 9.11-D + 9.11-E own that workstream.
  • Does NOT predict whether 9.10-E substrate expansion would surface Mode A clusters for marginal lineages — that's empirical, fires when 9.10-E executes.
  • Does NOT make architectural commitments — recognition record only; commitments deferred per recognition-validate-commit cycle.

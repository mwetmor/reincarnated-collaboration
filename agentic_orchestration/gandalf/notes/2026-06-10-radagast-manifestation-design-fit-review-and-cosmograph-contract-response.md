### 3.2 The load-bearing AMENDMENT — one shared layout, two projections

Radagast asked the exact right question and correctly flagged it as mine: *"Whether the 2D /forge layout and the 3D sphere layout derive from one shared embedding."*

**They MUST.** Here is the design reasoning and the player consequence:

The cosmograph is the player's **map of meaning** — the same star-signs appear on the /forge 2D web (loadout app) AND on the 3D night sky (UE manifestation + persistent realm). If those two surfaces run **independent** dimensionality reductions (UMAP/t-SNE are stochastic and non-isometric — two runs produce different neighbor structures), then a star-sign that sits next to another in the forge becomes a stranger in the sky. **The player's spatial memory breaks across surfaces.** They learn "fire kits live up-and-left" in the forge, look up at the sky, and it's wrong. Cross-surface coherence is a **player-experience anchor**, not an engineering nicety — it is the difference between the cosmograph being a *place the player knows* and a *different chart every time they change windows*.

**The architecture that guarantees coherence:**
- **One canonical layout, deterministically projected to each surface.** The engine computes ONE spatial layout from the existing kit/star-sign embedding (the same embedding already driving clustering), and each surface is a *fixed, deterministic projection* of that one layout — not its own reduction.
- **I lean: the sphere is the canonical source-of-truth**, and the 2D /forge web derives from it via a fixed unwrap (azimuthal/stereographic), NOT the reverse. Reason: the night sky is the richer in-fiction metaphor (it IS the cosmograph, canonically), and a sphere unwraps to a plane cleanly while a plane does not lift to a sphere uniquely. Compute the sphere; flatten for the forge.
- **The projection must preserve cluster locality** — signs in the same cluster region must be spatially contiguous on the sphere (so a rune-glyph anchor sits naturally at its cluster's sky-centroid, and "twirling the sky" sweeps coherent regions, not confetti). This argues for a **neighborhood-preserving projection** (sphere-constrained MDS or UMAP-to-S²), NOT PCA-to-sphere or random placement.

### 3.5 Per-kit constellation figure geometry (member stars + edges) — a D7 call

This is the one part of the contract with a genuine human-authorship dimension.

- **Member stars:** substrate-derived — the star-signs/primitives composing the kit are already known from the kit's composition. No design call needed; emit them.
- **Edge list (the figure "lines"):** real constellations are **human-authored cultural figures.** Orion's lines are a story humans drew; the stars aren't physically close or naturally connected. This is *exactly* the kind of meaning-bearing surface where pure-substrate output (e.g., raw nearest-neighbor edges) reads **arbitrary** — a scatter of dots with lines that mean nothing. That's a D7-adjacent failure: it would *look* procedurally generated, because it would be.
- **But 400 kits → hand-authoring 400 figures is infeasible.** So the D7-compliant compromise:
  - **Procedural baseline for all kits** — a figure-generating heuristic over the member-star positions (minimum-spanning-tree, or a "trunk + limbs" silhouette heuristic) that produces a *plausible* figure, not a random graph.
  - **Human-curation override slot** for hero/signature kits — the 3 anchor kits (Duskweaver, Cannonade Cleric, Stonefist) and any marquee kit get a *hand-drawn* edge list that makes the constellation *recognizably theirs*.
  - The schema carries `edges[]` + `edge_source: "procedural" | "curated"` so curation is a per-kit upgrade, not a global requirement.
- **Procedural-floor + curated-marquee** is the same pattern as the rest of the project (the sky fills procedurally; the heroes are hand-touched). It scales to 400 and still lets the moments that matter carry authored weight.

## 4. Matt's Avatar / out-of-body framing — recognition (RE-OPENS § 1.2 delta #1)

Matt, this session: *"more of an out-of-body experience that you can jump into and jump out of... Like Avatar the movie. When you want to jump back to the earth and play in the persistent realm, that is always open to you (except there won't be much to do there until you begin to amass a court of forms)."*

This is a meaningful refinement and it changes the *topology* of the manifestation moment — Radagast had the location right (Earth) but the transition is richer than "departure threshold."

### 4.1 What it changes

The Earth↔new-world relationship is not a one-way reincarnation-crossing. It is a **recurring projection** — the Earth Self persists (the body in the link, the human who is never in danger), and the player **projects into** a new-world form (the avatar), able to **jump back** to the persistent realm at will. This composes cleanly with canon already on the books:
- Earth Self = persistent player identity → **the human in the link-chair.**
- Reincarnated forms = **avatars you project into** (the word "avatar" is now literal).
- "Court of forms" = the gacha-accumulated form library (`project_earth_meta_layer.md`) → **your stable of bodies to project into.**
- Spirit guide = future self → the constant across projections.

So Radagast's "knoll = departure threshold, NOT arrival" refines to: **the knoll is the projection point, and it is recurring, not one-time.** Still Earth, still pastoral. But two consequences for the build:
1. **The first manifestation (creation cascade) and subsequent jumps (into already-created forms) are different weights.** First time per form = the elaborate cascade Radagast specced. Returning to an existing form = a *lighter* jump-in. The UE transition architecture should anticipate the recurring, lighter case — not just the one-time cinematic. (This does NOT change Phase 1; it's a Phase-3+/Phase-4 note.)
2. **Earth must be a returnable hub,** not just a launch ramp. Which surfaces the cold-start risk in § 4.3.

### 4.2 The strong genre anchor — Warframe (name it hard)

The structure Matt is describing is, almost exactly, **Warframe's Operator/Orbiter model:** the player is the Operator (a persistent, vulnerable self) aboard the Orbiter (persistent hub), who **projects into Warframes** (the collected, swappable bodies) to go on runs, and returns to the Orbiter between them. Map it 1:1:
- Operator → **Earth Self**
- Orbiter (persistent hub) → **Earth persistent realm**
- Warframe arsenal → **court of forms**
- Going on a mission → **a season in the new world**
- Returning to the Orbiter → **jumping back to Earth**

Warframe is a *proven, beloved, decade-durable* structure — that the framing lands on it is reassuring, not derivative. James Cameron's Avatar is the cinematic reference Matt named (link-pod projection, human safe, avatar expendable); Warframe is the *interactive-loop* reference for how this actually plays. Also adjacent: Destiny's Tower (social/management hub between activity-runs), Hades' House of Hades (persistent hub you return to between runs, which fills with meaning as you progress).

### 4.3 The design payoff — and the one caution

**Payoff 1 — seasonal death stops being identity-loss.** In Avatar/Warframe, losing the projected body does NOT kill you. So the ARPG's seasonal-form-death has *low stakes-of-loss but high stakes-of-collection*: the player isn't afraid to lose a form, they're motivated to **amass** them. That is a *healthier* loop than permadeath-dread for a seasonal ARPG — it reframes loss as **molting**, not death. This is a genuinely good consequence of the framing; it dissolves the "my character died forever" anxiety that makes some players bounce off seasonal/hardcore structures, while keeping the collection drive that makes gacha-accumulation compelling.

**Payoff 2 — a possible unification I want to offer (recognition, NOT a commitment):** the cosmograph could **BE** the court of forms. When you jump back to Earth and look up, your sky is *your collection* — each form you've bound is a constellation you've lit. Early game = a near-empty sky (few forms). Late game = a rich personal heaven. This would:
- unify the creation-menu cosmograph with the persistent-realm collection surface (one substrate, two contexts — and the § 3 shared-layout contract makes this *free*, because both are projections of the same canonical sky);
- make "amass a court of forms" *literally* "fill your sky";
- give the gacha accumulation a mythic frame (you are not collecting cards; you are lighting your heavens);
- and directly soften the cold-start problem below.

**The one caution — the empty-hub cold-start.** Matt's own parenthetical — *"there won't be much to do there until you amass a court of forms"* — names the risk precisely. The **first** return to Earth, right after the mythic manifestation, must not be a literal empty field. An anticlimactic void after the game's namesake moment would undercut the whole beat. Warframe's early Orbiter is sparse but never *empty* — it always has immediate purpose (mod management, navigation, the cephalon's voice). My recommendation: the first return to Earth needs **at least one meaningful beat** — the spirit guide reflecting on the first form; the first constellation visibly lighting in an otherwise-dark sky (the cosmograph-as-court unification pays this off directly: one form = one lit constellation = a reason to look up = the seed of the collection). Don't ship the persistent realm as a dead room at first contact.

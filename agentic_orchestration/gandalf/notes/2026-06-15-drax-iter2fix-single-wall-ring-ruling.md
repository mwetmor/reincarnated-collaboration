## 0. The defect (confirmed in frames)

iter2 built **TWO concentric wall rings per room**: an INNER ring boxing the playable pit (at the playable-footprint edge) AND the OUTER multi-level ring at the extended visual edge — with the annulus dressing **sandwiched between two walls.** This re-creates the exact "tabletop board boxed pit" the diorama extension was meant to ELIMINATE. The playable pit is still walled-in; the eye hits an inner wall instead of flowing from the pit across the annulus to the far walls.

## 1. The ruling — ONE wall ring, playable pit UNWALLED

There must be **exactly ONE wall ring per room: the OUTER one** (multi-level far walls, low/open camera side). **REMOVE the INNER wall ring at the playable-footprint edge entirely.** The playable floor flows CONTINUOUSLY into the annulus floor; the annulus dressing (tombs/statues/rubble) provides the visual framing of the play space — **NO full-height wall between the playable pit and the annulus.**

**Cross-section (one room; camera at the LOW/open near side looking toward the TALL far wall):**

```
[low/open near side]  →  playable pit (clear floor)  →  annulus (dressing)  →  [TALL far wall]
        ^                         ^                            ^                      ^
   camera sees in           NO WALL HERE                 nonpassable           the ONLY
   over low/no wall      (continuous floor)               dressing             wall ring
```

Floor is continuous from pit → annulus → single outer wall ring. There is NO wall at the playable-pit edge.

## 2. Optional soft demarcation (NOT a wall)

If a play-space demarcation aids readability, use **LOW SEE-OVER elements only** — knee-high ruined-wall fragments, a curb, a floor-texture ring — **NEVER a full-height wall.** Default: no inner demarcation; the dressing ring + the combatants mark the play space. This matches the reference image (#59): the pit is bounded by LOW broken walls / tombs you see OVER, with tall architecture only at the far back.

This is the operational form of the **playable-footprint vs visual-footprint decoupling**: the playable footprint is NOT a walled room — it is an invisible sub-region of a larger dressed floor. The ONLY physical room boundary is the visual-footprint (outer) wall. Walling the playable footprint re-couples the two and defeats the entire move.

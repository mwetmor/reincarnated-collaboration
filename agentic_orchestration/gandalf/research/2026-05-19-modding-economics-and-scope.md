# Research — Mod-Pack Revenue Models, Content-Type Value, and Community Scope Expectations — 2026-05-19

**Mode:** A (analytical)
**Commissioner:** Gandalf (Pattern-B commercial direction — class/character overhaul scope viability)
**Sources consulted:** Patreon public pages (Calamity, Vault Hunters, Median XL), Steam Workshop mod pages, Nexus Mods help documentation, Crate Entertainment official mod guide, ModDB, community forum threads (Crate Entertainment Forums, Steam Community), PCGamer, PC Gamer, GGMods launch coverage, Graphtreon.com top earners data.

---

## TL;DR (5-sentence executive summary)

Class-only and mastery-expansion mods are the **backbone of ARPG modding communities**, not a fringe edge case — Grim Dawn's two most recommended mods (Dawn of Masteries, Grimarillion) add only classes and items within the existing world, and they are universally treated as complete, high-value artifacts by the community. No ARPG-specific modding host currently enables direct paid mods; revenue flows almost entirely through Patreon (for servers/community mods) or Nexus Mods Donation Points (a platform pool, not consumer purchase). Calamity (Terraria) demonstrates the ceiling: 9.18M Steam subscribers, ~$2,552/month Patreon on 2,669 members — a massive class + biome + boss + lore total-conversion. Class-only mods at the ARPG scale don't reach those heights numerically, but they do not fail commercially either — they simply operate on a free + optional-support model where the constraint is not scope but host-level monetization policy. The load-bearing conclusion for Reincarnated's Pattern-B decision: **class + skill tree + seasonal cohesion + new loot is a recognized complete artifact in the GD/TQ/TL2 community**, not a partial one; the question is not "will players accept it" but "can you charge for it," and the current ARPG host ecosystem says no on direct sales, yes on Patreon-as-operating-fund.

## Q1 — Mod-Pack Revenue Models Per Host

### Direct paid mods

**Grim Dawn, Torchlight 2, Titan Quest AE:** None of these hosts allow direct-sale paid mods. Crate Entertainment's official modding documentation emphasizes free community-generated content. The entire mod ecosystem across these three titles operates on free download only. No paid-mod marketplace exists or is tolerated. Reign of Terror explicitly states "no donations are accepted for RoT development."

**Skyrim/Fallout (Bethesda Creations, formerly Creation Club):** The only major ARPG-adjacent host with a working paid-mod pipeline. Creation Club (2017) contracted modders directly as work-for-hire. "Creations" (December 2023) shifted to royalty-based revenue share, giving modders a cut of sales. Community reception has been persistently negative — one of the most downloaded mods on Nexus after the Creations launch is a mod that removes the Creations menu from the in-game interface. No public revenue-per-mod figures released by Bethesda.

**Terraria (Steam Workshop / tModLoader):** No paid mod system. All mods are free on Workshop and the Terraria forum browser. Monetization occurs entirely via Patreon (see below).

**Minecraft (CurseForge / Modrinth):** No direct paid modpacks in the traditional sense. CurseForge pays creators through an ad-revenue model based on downloads (low individual payouts). Monetization for large modpacks occurs via Patreon and YouTube/streaming.

**BG3 (Nexus Mods / Larian official mod manager):** No official paid mod path as of early 2026. Community backlash when modders attempted to gate content behind Patreon early access. GGMods (launched January 7, 2026, soft-launched targeting Skyrim, BG3, Fallout 4) is attempting salaried contracts and transparent per-engagement payouts for modders — games supported at launch do not include Grim Dawn or Torchlight 2.

### Patreon support — specific numbers

| Mod / Creator | Host Game | Patron Count | Monthly Revenue | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calamity Mod Team | Terraria | 2,669 members | ~$2,552/month | Starting tier $2/month |
| Vault Hunters (Iskall85 team) | Minecraft | 1,868 members | ~$2,455/month | Starting tier $5/month |
| Median XL | Diablo 2 | 693 members | Not disclosed (starting $1/month) | Funds server infrastructure |
| Dawn of Masteries | Grim Dawn | None found | $0 — no Patreon | Free, no monetization |
| Reign of Terror | Grim Dawn | None — explicitly refused | $0 — explicitly refused donations | Policy statement on forum |
| Grimarillion | Grim Dawn | None found | $0 | Free |
| Nydiamar | Grim Dawn | None found | $0 | Free |
| SynergiesMOD | Torchlight 2 | None found | $0 | Free |

**Pattern:** Patreon support in ARPG modding is primarily a server-cost / community-sustainability mechanism (Median XL runs multiplayer servers). Purely single-player content mods like all major GD mods have no Patreon at all. The two games with successful Patreon mod support (Terraria via Calamity, Minecraft via Vault Hunters) are sandbox games with different community norms around creator support.

**Patreon ceiling benchmark:** Calamity at $2,552/month with 9.18M Steam subscribers is the highest-performing mod Patreon in this category. This is the total-conversion ceiling. It is not large by content-creator standards — Game Maker's Toolkit (video essays) earns $7,502/month; Minecolonies (Minecraft mod) earns $10,124/month. The top gaming Patreon (Czepeku, map packs) earns $80,925/month. Mod Patreons for ARPG-adjacent titles cluster in the $1K–$5K/month range at best, and only when the mod is a massive total-conversion with server infrastructure needs.

### Nexus Mods Donation Points — revenue to authors

Nexus Mods runs a Donation Points (DP) pool: modders earn points proportional to unique downloads from a monthly pool. The pool as of 2024 was approximately $325,000 USD / £325,000 GBP per month, distributed across all opted-in mods. Rate: 1,000 DP = $1. Distribution algorithm is private (changed June 2024 to be more equitable). Individual earnings are not publicly disclosed. There is no "top earner" transparency. A major Grim Dawn mod with tens of thousands of downloads likely earns a few hundred dollars per month at most from this pool — the pool is divided across every opted-in mod on the entire platform (hundreds of thousands of mods). This is supplemental income, not a business model.

### GGMods (emerging, 2026)

GGMods launched January 7, 2026 with salaried contracts for top-tier modders and transparent fixed-rate per-engagement payouts. Games at launch: Skyrim, BG3, Fallout 4. No ARPG-specific hosts (Grim Dawn, Torchlight 2, TQAE) included. This is the most significant structural change in mod monetization in years, but not yet applicable to the Pattern-B target hosts.

## Q2 — Mod Content-Type Value Comparison

### Class-only mods (new classes/masteries, existing world)

**Dawn of Masteries (Grim Dawn):** Compiles 53 unique classes from multiple mod sources (Cataclysm, Diablo 2, Diablo 3, Grim Quest, NCFF, Sparker, Zenith masteries) into one package. Adds cosmetics (wings, hairstyles, auras) and a unique-trading token system. No new maps, no new quests, no new world content. Distribution: Nexus Mods + ModDB, free. Specific download/endorsement counts blocked by Nexus 403 responses during this crawl, but the mod is consistently in the top 3–5 recommended Grim Dawn mods in every community thread found.

Community reception: Treated as a **complete, high-value artifact**. Players who use DoM describe it as permanently replacing vanilla play. One thread commenter: "just can't play Vanilla anymore." No sentiment found describing it as "incomplete" or "missing" anything due to lack of maps.

**Grimarillion (Grim Dawn):** Similar scope — adds masteries from Titan Quest, Diablo 3, Zenith, and more. Also adds items and relics. No new maps/quests. Free. Actively maintained through GD 1.2. Treated as the most stable class-expansion option. 661,700+ Steam Workshop subscribers for SynergiesMOD (TL2's equivalent) shows the demand ceiling for this category in a smaller game.

**SynergiesMOD (Torchlight 2):** 661,700 current Steam Workshop subscribers, 5-star rating with 12,795 ratings, 972,070 unique visitors. Adds new classes (Necromancer, Paladin, Warlock, Ranger), endgame dungeons, and 300+ new legendary items. PCGamer's "#1 free download of the summer"; ModDB "Best Overhaul of the Year 2013." This is class + endgame content but **no new story/map/quest campaign**. Massively successful on those terms.

**Soulvizier AERA (Titan Quest AE):** 12,947 Steam subscribers, 977 ratings (5-star). Expands existing masteries to 60 levels, adds 220+ unique items, 710+ unique Souls, 44 new artifacts. No new classes, no new world/quests — loot and mastery depth expansion only. Community calls vanilla "ten thousand times better with Soulvizier." Treated as complete.

**Verdict on class-only scope:** In the GD/TQ/TL2 ecosystem, class-only mods are the **primary canonical mod type**. Community members do not express "I wish this had maps" about them — they express "now I can play this game forever." The class-content type is not perceived as incomplete in this ecosystem.

### Total-conversion mods (new world + new classes + new everything)

**Reign of Terror (Grim Dawn):** Full Diablo 2 reimplementation — all D2 Acts 1–6, all 7 D2 classes, D1/D2 items, uniques, rune words, Tristram recreation. Free. Explicitly refuses donations. Thread on Crate forum reaches hundreds of pages. Community reception: uniformly described as "exceptional," one of the best Grim Dawn experiences available. Still in active development (v0.7.6.3); community notes it is technically pre-1.0 beta but does not treat it as incomplete — pre-1.0 status is a development milestone, not a content-incomplete signal.

**Nydiamar (Grim Dawn):** Standalone campaign — 25+ new environments, 12+ roguelike dungeons, 6 devotion dungeons, choice-based quests, 40+ original soundtrack tracks, 12–18 hour playthrough, 3.2 GB download. Free. Last updated February 12, 2025. Community: "vastly more captivating than Cairn." This is the full-world-content type. Massively praised — also free, no Patreon.

**Calamity (Terraria):** 9,181,036 Steam Workshop subscribers (current as of May 2026), 55,223 ratings (5-star). Adds Rogue class, multiple biomes (Abyss, Astral Infection), dozens of new bosses, difficulty modifiers, original lore. Patreon: 2,669 members, ~$2,552/month. The canonical "what a massive total-conversion looks like." Even this achieves only $2,552/month on Patreon despite 9M subscribers — a 0.03% conversion rate to paying supporters.

**Could a Calamity-scope-but-class-only mod have achieved the same Patreon support?** Empirically: no. The Calamity Patreon is tied to active server costs, music licensing, and ongoing total-conversion development. Class-only mods for single-player ARPGs (GD, TL2, TQAE) have no Patreon at all. The correlation is: scope drives Patreon need (server costs, scale of ongoing dev) more than scope driving willingness-to-pay. Single-player class mods have no server costs, and ARPG modding culture does not support discretionary Patreon tipping for class content.

### Map/quest packs only (no class changes)

**Median XL (Diablo 2):** Full overhaul — new skill trees for all 8 existing classes (not new classes, but full rework), thousands of new items, 50+ new quests, dozens of new locations. Free. 693 Patreon members (starting $1/month) — primarily for server maintenance. Median XL is the canonical example of "skill tree overhaul + loot + maps + quests" without new classes. It is the most popular Diablo 2 mod ever and still heavily played. Revenue: minimal Patreon primarily server-cost.

**Project Diablo 2:** Seasonal model — 4-month ladders with balance updates, new items, PoE-inspired endgame maps, group dungeons. No new classes. Free. Donation-supported but not Patreon. Community: thriving, multiple ladder resets per year. Seasonal content is a **proven engagement model** even for free mods — PD2's seasonal resets drive player return without charging for them.

**Map/quest pack without class work:** No strong standalone examples found in the ARPG space. The weak-performing end of the spectrum is map/quest content without class/loot hooks — players engage with new world content for one playthrough and do not replay it the way they replay class builds.

### Class + skill tree + VFX packs (Reincarnated's closest analog)

No direct "pure class + skill tree + VFX" paid mod exists in the ARPG space. The closest analogs are all free: Dawn of Masteries (GD), Grimarillion (GD), SynergiesMOD (TL2). The scope matches what Reincarnated would ship. These mods are all free, all well-received, and none have attempted monetization.

The absence of paid examples in this category is not evidence of market rejection — it reflects the culture and host policy of the target platforms. Grim Dawn's modding ecosystem does not have a paid-mod mechanism. Torchlight 2's ecosystem (Steam Workshop + runic fansite) does not either.

**Key nuance:** VFX and SFX in these mods are typically minimal or borrowed from the host game's own assets. A mod with **entirely original VFX/SFX** would be unusual and technically ambitious — this may differentiate Reincarnated's output if the engine generates or specifies novel particle/audio assets, but the community has not priced this differentially.

### Cosmetic packs

Bethesda Creation Club / Creations: paid cosmetics exist ($1.99–$4.99 range for armor sets, weapon skins). Community reception: negative. The most popular reaction mod removes the Creations menu. This model works only when the publisher controls the storefront and forces discovery. Independent cosmetic packs have no commercial precedent in ARPG modding.

### Loot/affix overhauls

Median XL (new items + quests + skill reworks), Soulvizier (loot tables, new uniques), SynergiesMOD (300+ legendaries): all free, all community-embraced. Loot overhauls are treated as quality-of-life upgrades and do not trigger Patreon support in the ARPG space.

### Seasonal/themed packs

Project Diablo 2 is the strongest precedent: 4-month ladder seasons with themed content drops, balance patches, new items. Each season has a thematic hook (Season 13 is "Betrayal"). Community engagement around season launches is high; players return for each new ladder. Revenue model: free + donation. This is the closest existing analog to Reincarnated's "seasonal cohesion" framing. PD2's season model proves the concept is community-embraced — players track season launches, build guides for new season items, and organize around the seasonal reset.

No ARPG mod has charged per-season for new seasonal content. The seasonal model is currently a free engagement driver, not a revenue mechanism.

## Q3 — Per-Host Community Expectations

### Grim Dawn (primary host)

**What makes a mod "worth paying attention to" in GD:**

Community threads (Crate forums + Steam Discussions, 2024) consistently surface the same three mod tiers:

1. **Class compilation mods** (DoM, Grimarillion): Adding 20–50+ new masteries is treated as the major content delivery mechanism. No new maps expected or demanded. The "complete" criterion for these mods is: stable, maintained, no missing item tags, compatible with current game version.

2. **Total-conversion campaigns** (Reign of Terror, Nydiamar): New world + new quests + class content. These are held to a "standalone experience" standard — playable start-to-finish with new narrative. Community clearly distinguishes these from class mods but does not treat class mods as inferior; they are different categories.

3. **Quality-of-life tools** (Rainbow Filter, GD Stash, Grim Internals): Universally recommended alongside, not instead of, content mods.

**Dawn of Masteries precedent:** DoM ports ~53 classes from Titan Quest to Grim Dawn with no new world/quests. Community reception: embraced as a definitive Grim Dawn experience. Players who run DoM do not express "I wish this had maps." The mastery-and-items scope is fully satisfying. One thread explicitly states players "can't go back to vanilla" after DoM. This is the most direct empirical evidence that class-only scope is not perceived as incomplete.

**Reign of Terror precedent:** Full D2 reimplementation in GD engine. Community frames this as "an entirely different game" — framing is about breadth, not about it being "more complete" than DoM. The community acknowledges DoM and RoT as serving different player needs, not DoM as a lesser artifact.

**"Complete" signal:** In GD community discourse, "complete" for a class mod means: all skills functional, all items have loot entries, no orphaned tags, maintained for current game version. New maps are not part of the definition. The community explicitly does not expect class mods to ship with world content.

**Monetization culture:** All major GD mods are free. Reign of Terror explicitly refuses donations. This is not a norm that supports a paid tier for class content in the current ecosystem. Attempting to charge for a GD mastery pack would be a significant culture break and likely generate community backlash based on observed norms.

### Torchlight 2

**Community norm:** SynergiesMOD with 661,700 Steam subscribers is the canonical example. The mod adds new classes, endgame dungeons, and items. No new campaign maps or quests. It is the most subscribed TL2 mod. Community treats it as the definitive way to extend TL2. PCGamer and ModDB both recognized it as best overhaul without requiring it to have added story content.

**Minimum viable scope for attention:** For TL2, "worth installing" means new class/build variety or difficulty tuning. Map/quest content is a bonus, not a requirement. The game's original campaign is replayed as the vehicle — players don't require a new campaign when new classes make old content fresh.

**Monetization culture:** No paid mods exist in TL2 ecosystem. Runic Games explicitly endorsed free modding. No Patreon found for any major TL2 mod.

### Titan Quest AE

**Community norm:** Soulvizier AERA with 12,947 subscribers and deep loot + mastery expansion but no new classes or world — 5-star reception. Multi-Class Mastery Mod (Nexus) extends the class-combination system. Community engagement is active but smaller than GD (the game is older, less active modding scene overall).

**Minimum viable scope:** Meaningful loot or class depth addition. New world content is very rare in TQAE modding; the community has not developed expectations around it. A class-only mod is fully in the mainstream.

**Monetization culture:** Same as GD — free only. TQAE's Steam Workshop and Nexus pages have no paid mod infrastructure.

### Terraria (Calamity comparison)

**Different norm:** Terraria has a donation/Patreon culture that ARPG games don't have. This stems from: (a) much larger player base, (b) sandbox community with YouTube creator crossover, (c) total-conversion scale requiring ongoing team and server investment, (d) Calamity adds 1 new class but its real mass draw is bosses, biomes, and lore — not a pure class mod.

**Could a class-only Calamity have achieved the same?** No. The 9M subscriber base and $2,552/month Patreon reflect the full scope. A class-only Terraria mod at high quality might achieve 500K–1M subscribers but would not have the ongoing server/team cost that drives Patreon adoption. The Patreon correlation is to ongoing operational need, not to content quality.

## Cross-Cutting Insights

**Insight 1: Class-scope is the dominant content category in ARPG modding.** The top-ranked mods by community recommendation in GD, TL2, and TQAE are all class/mastery additions, not total conversions. This is the default expectation, not a niche. Reincarnated's output matches the mainstream.

**Insight 2: Revenue ceiling is very low across all ARPG modding.** Even Calamity — the most downloaded mod in this research — earns $2,552/month. All ARPG-specific mods (GD, TL2, TQAE) earn $0 from monetization. The revenue model for ARPG mods is currently: free, with platform Donation Points as supplemental income (estimated hundreds of dollars/month for major mods, not thousands).

**Insight 3: Seasonal framing is validated as an engagement model but not yet a revenue mechanism.** Project Diablo 2's seasonal resets are the closest precedent. Seasonal content drives player returns and community excitement. No one charges for seasonal mod content yet. The concept is community-accepted; the commercial ask has not been attempted.

**Insight 4: Total conversions achieve cultural prestige but not proportionally higher monetization.** Reign of Terror is arguably GD's most prestigious mod. It earns $0 and explicitly refuses donations. Nydiamar earns $0. The prestige/effort/revenue curve is flat — scope does not unlock monetization in this ecosystem.

**Insight 5: Attempting paid class mods would be a culture break, not just a pricing decision.** GD community norms are explicitly free-mod. Reign of Terror's donation refusal is a principled statement. Any attempt to charge — even $5 — for a GD mastery pack would require framing the offering outside the GD ecosystem norms entirely (standalone product, different distribution channel).

**Insight 6: GGMods (2026 launch) is the first platform with a viable paid-mod structure, but doesn't yet cover ARPG hosts.** If GGMods adds GD/TL2/TQAE, the infrastructure for paid class content would exist. Currently, it does not.

## Pattern-B Recommendation Implications

These are empirical observations, not strategic recommendations (that is Gandalf's domain). The facts relevant to Matt's question:

**"Is class system only — without maps/quests/NPCs — commercially weightless?"**

Empirically: class-only content is not weightless — it is the most valued content category in the ARPG modding community. DoM, Grimarillion, SynergiesMOD collectively represent millions of player-hours of engagement on class-only scope. Community reception is enthusiastic, not dismissive.

**"But is it worth paying for?"**

Empirically: not in the current ARPG modding ecosystem. No existing ARPG class mod charges for its content. The constraint is not perceived value but host-platform norm and culture. The community actively resists paid mods on Bethesda's platforms even from a publisher — they would resist it more on community-normed platforms like GD/TL2.

**Path to revenue given these constraints:**

- Patreon: viable only if there is ongoing server/community cost to justify it, as in Median XL. A single-player class mod with no multiplayer infrastructure has no Patreon hook.
- Nexus Donation Points: supplemental, low (hundreds of dollars/month at best for a top GD mod).
- Standalone product: distributing outside the GD mod ecosystem as a standalone game or game expansion — the approach that sidesteps mod-culture norms entirely. This is not a modding-revenue question; it's a game-publishing question.
- GGMods: possible future path if platform expands to cover ARPG hosts.

**On seasonal cohesion as differentiator:**

The seasonal framing is novel in ARPG modding. PD2 demonstrates seasonal resets drive community re-engagement. No mod has yet turned seasonal content into a subscription or per-season fee. If Reincarnated ships LLM-generated seasonal class variants as a recurring service (not a one-time mod), the commercial model shifts from "mod pricing" to "content service pricing" — a different frame that may have more room.

## Open Questions

1. What is Crate Entertainment's explicit policy on paid mods for GD? The official site emphasizes free community content but does not explicitly prohibit commercialization. A direct inquiry or forum post by the dev team would clarify whether a paid standalone distribution channel is viable or prohibited.

2. Does the "standalone product" path (distributing as a standalone game rather than a mod) change the commercial calculus entirely? Enderal (Skyrim TC) released as a standalone app on Steam for free but with Steam store presence — this is different from a paid mod. A Reincarnated-generated class pack as a standalone cheap DLC or separate product on itch.io or Steam bypasses mod-culture norms.

3. Is there precedent for LLM-generated or procedural content as a recurring mod service with a subscription? Nothing found in this research pass — this would be novel.

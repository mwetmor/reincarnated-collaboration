§ 4 — What the data is saying (interpretation)

4.1 — The pipeline-accessibility axis is the dominant variance source

Across the 16 candidates, pipeline-accessibility scores range 1.0 → 5.0 (full 4-point spread). Schema-compatibility scores range 1.0 → 4.0. KPI-compatibility ranges 1 → 4. Community-vitality ranges 1 → 5. Pipeline accessibility is doing more work than its 30% weight suggests because it's the axis with the widest absolute spread. Hosts with strong communities and weak pipelines (Elden Ring 5/3, BG3 5/3.5) stay stuck in Niche tier; hosts with weak communities and strong pipelines (TL2 2/4) rise to Secondary. For Reincarnated specifically — a procedural content-injection use case — pipeline accessibility is the real gate.

4.2 — The schema-compatibility moat for Crate-lineage hosts

Grim Dawn (4.0) and TQAE (3.5) are the only two hosts where schema-compatibility scores at or above the 3.5 threshold. This is because the mastery-system DBR architecture maps cleanly to Reincarnated's class/skill/monster/gear JSON schemas. This is the single most important fact in the entire matrix. Schema work is the irreducible engineering cost of mod-export; it cannot be skipped or shortcut. Grim Dawn and TQAE are the two hosts where the schema work is shortest.

4.3 — The community-vitality floor — what "small" actually means

Community-vitality scores of 2/5 (TQAE, TL2) translate to: hundreds-to-low-thousands concurrent players. That's not a viable commercial reach for monetized mod sales — but it is a viable proof-of-concept population. The honest framing for Phase 1+2 is: "we ship into the most-modded ARPG-mastery-system hosts to demonstrate the engine works at all, not to extract direct mod revenue." Direct mod revenue per the Director's debrief is ~$5K–$50K per pack ceiling for niche-genre. The strategic prize is the engine-sale conversation, and the GD+TQAE pairing is the demonstration that earns that conversation.

4.4 — The Track-F gap as commercial-path differentiator (v3 corrected)

The fight-integrity gap diagnosis (Gandalf, 2026-05-18) identified ~9–15 dev-weeks of engineering required to ship a genre-credible standalone (Path A): per-tier balance targets, spatial sub-gauntlet, AI/range schema migration, demo collision/leash, demo AI parity. Path B routes the gap into a host-calibration problem — host games handle runtime substrate, but our balance loop still has to converge against *something*, and host-game combat is what it must converge against. 4.5 — R6 Host-Calibration Protocol (correction after Matt push-back)

Matt's challenge (verbatim): "Although these two host games will manage the spatial substrate, AI behavior, range mechanics, and aggro/leash defaults, how will we provide balanced classes through the engine without converging on those via use of said mechanics at generative/sim time?"

Acknowledged: the original "R3 OPTIONAL" claim conflated runtime substrate (host game absorbs) with generation-time balance convergence (engine still has to converge against *something*). The engine's balance loop currently tunes classes against the 1D PackProxy gauntlet — a combat model that does not exist in Grim Dawn. Classes shipped to GD without calibration would manifest the same fight-integrity gap the demo shows today, just on a different surface. The host game doesn't make our balance true; it exposes the same wrongness in a new place.

The fight-integrity gap diagnosis is now stronger, not weaker. It was named as "engine balances the wrong game vs. demo." The correction restates it as "engine balances the wrong game vs. ANY runtime that isn't the 1D gauntlet itself" — including all three Path-B host candidates. This will be reflected in a canonical-doc amendment after Pattern-B closes.

§ 5 — Open gaps (what the data is NOT yet saying)

Gap 1 — Per-host developer-permission scope (5 riders en route)

The scoring matrix captures can you author (pipeline accessibility) and does your schema map (schema compatibility) — but it does NOT capture are you permitted to author (the EULA / developer-policy axis). Specifically unknown per host:

  • What's modifiable vs locked. Can a mod replace the skill-tree system or only fill into existing mastery slots? Can it replace combat math, or only tune data values? Custom shaders / particle systems, or asset-swap only?
  • Monetization rights. Crate's, THQ Nordic's, Re-Logic's stance on paid mods. Workshop monetization policy per-host.
  • Total-conversion legal precedent. Grim Dawn's Reign of Terror directly reimplements Diablo 2 in GD — is this Crate-blessed, tolerated, or under-the-radar? Critical for understanding how aggressively we can push the host.
  • Distribution restrictions. External dependencies (linked DLLs, network calls for cosmology fetch, telemetry) per-host. Reincarnated wants to ship content + LLM-generated naming, possibly with cosmetic image-gen — what's allowed.
  • Engine fragility 2024-2026. Patch cadence per host; mod-break risk; DLC fragmentation handling.

Four riders are filing per-host modding-scope briefs (Grim Dawn, TQAE, Torchlight 2, Terraria/tModLoader) for v2 augment.

Gap 2 — Modding-purpose taxonomy per host community

Different host communities make different KINDS of mods. Reincarnated's procedural content-injection use case fits some communities naturally and is unusual in others. The scoring matrix doesn't capture this. The 5th rider (Mount Doom scout) is producing per-host taxonomy: QoL / Cosmetic / Content-addition / Total-conversion / Balance-overhaul / Lore / Mechanic-injection / Multiplayer / Procedural-generative (the closest analog to Reincarnated's use case).

Hypothesis to be tested by data: the Crate-lineage communities (GD + TQAE) lean content-addition + balance-overhaul + total-conversion; Terraria leans content-addition + mechanic-injection at very large scale; BG3 leans cosmetic + class-injection. Reincarnated's procedural-generative pattern is most natural where communities have already normalized "content-from-elsewhere flowing in" patterns. If communities are hostile to procedural injection, MFS scores must adjust downward post-rider.

Gap 3 — Post-training-cutoff games (Mount Doom scout)

Several 2024–2026 ARPG/RPG releases were not yet in the survey at the time of the 2026-05-18 pass. Specifically: Last Epoch (Eleventh Hour, 1.0 Feb 2024), Path of Exile 2 (GGG, EA Dec 2024), Diablo IV: Vessel of Hatred + Spiritborn (Blizzard, late 2024), No Rest for the Wicked (Moon Studios, EA April 2024), Avowed (Obsidian, Feb 2025), Tainted Grail: The Fall of Avalon, Stalker 2, Eternal Strands. Most are unlikely to add to the PRIMARY/Secondary tier (most AAA releases forbid modding; most modern indie ARPGs lack official tools), but the 5th rider is checking for surprises. Path of Exile 2 in particular would be a major addition if GGG has softened on modding.

§ 7 — What I deem useful additional context

7.1 — The fight-integrity gap is the structural reason Path B is the Director's correct call

The Director's reasoning for Path B was commercial — mod sales build the case for engine acquisition. The fight-integrity gap analysis adds an architectural reason that compounds the commercial one: Path B is the only path where the host game absorbs our hardest unsolved problem (spatial substrate) for free. Path A pays the ~9–15 dev-week tax. Path C pays bimodally. Path B alone gets the spatial substrate as a free dependency. When the engineering case and the commercial case point the same direction, that's rare enough to take seriously.

7.2 — The Director's "cement-deep-season" framing is closer to Path-C-MVP than originally priced

The engine already generates variants within a thematic frame (every season regen is exactly this — we just don't bank them deliberately). Banking is mostly persistence + tagging (weeks not months). The decision-tree authoring tool is genuine new surface (4–8 weeks). The buyer's API is custom per-deal anyway. Minimum-viable Path C — enough to demonstrate the cement-deep-season model — may be ~6–10 weeks, not the 3–6 months originally priced. Enterprise-grade Path C is still the bigger number, but only relevant after a buyer says yes.

7.3 — The Q5 dimension the data cannot speak to

Path B and Path C both take the deliverable away from "the game we're playing together" toward "the content that runs in someone else's game" or "the tool that powers other studios' seasons." Matt's son has been load-bearing in design-instinct via playtesting. The data cannot tell us whether the playtest partnership is a load-bearing project constraint or a graceful-degrade benefit. Only Matt can. The recommended Path B + warm-parallel Path C posture assumes graceful-degrade; if the partnership is load-bearing, the answer becomes Path A-prime (Reincarnated-the-game as developer-portfolio-grade playable demo with explicitly documented Phase-0 scope, eating less Track F because per-tier WR failures and missing range mechanics are named rather than hidden). This is the hybrid the original draft did not explicitly name and the data alone cannot resolve.

7.4 — Visual benchmark trajectory (galadriel, in flight)

Today's other in-flight work: galadriel's visual benchmark report scored the demo's current state at 2.3/5 against the Diablo: Path of Exile / Diablo IV (DoE) reference. Another structural Path-B advantage.

9.1 — What the rider returns change about the recommendation

The TQAE IP-assignment clause is the load-bearing finding. The Crate-lineage "killer pairing" relied on the assumption that TQAE adoption costs ~+25% incremental engineering on top of Grim Dawn for ~2× audience-plus-credibility. The engineering case still holds — the schema work translates. But the commercial case now has a poison-pill axis: any Reincarnated content published as a TQAE mod could be claimed by THQ Nordic under their IP-assignment clause. This does not affect Grim Dawn Phase 1, but it changes what Phase 2 actually is:

  • Phase 2-as-credibility-only (acceptable): if Phase 2 TQAE is just a technical-credibility play — "we ship into the second Crate-lineage mod-host to prove cross-host portability for Path-C buyer narratives" — and we never plan to monetize TQAE content separately, the IP-assignment clause is dormant. The risk surfaces if Reincarnated later tries to commercialize or license the TQAE-ported content.
  • Phase 2-as-revenue (not acceptable without legal): any path that sells TQAE mods directly, or that publishes Reincarnated-branded TQAE content as a portfolio artifact, runs into the IP clause. Requires written agreement with THQ Nordic GmbH that does not currently exist for any modder we found.
  • Phase 2-as-skipped (also viable): the data might be telling us to ship Grim Dawn Phase 1 + skip directly to Torchlight 2 Phase 3 Option A as the second host, because TL2's permission shape is also imperfect (Runic ToU non-commercial, IP-orphan-at-Embracer) but lacks the active IP-assignment poison pill. This is a meaningful Pattern-B refinement.

The Grim Dawn monetization UNKNOWN is the second material gap. Crate publishes no monetization policy; zero precedent for paid GD mods exists. Before Phase 1 ships commercially, Matt needs to directly contact Crate (Medierra / Zantai are public dev presences on the Crate forum) and ask. This is a pre-Phase-1 outreach, not a Pattern-B blocker — but it is a real action item the data surfaced.

The Torchlight 2 IP-orphan state strengthens its Phase-3 position precisely because no one is enforcing anything. Runic ToU §6.4 prohibits commercial exploitation, but Arc Games has not issued mod policy at any IP transition since 2017; SynergiesMOD operates without a publishable license under absence-of-enforcement. For a non-commercial Phase-3 credibility play, this is workable. For a paid mod ladder, it is not — but neither is anywhere else in the corpus.

The Terraria runtime-JSON workaround is more viable than the original scoring assumed. kRPG's pre-allocated-slot pattern + ModConfig.ServerSide auto-sync is a documented production pattern. The CI pipeline (transpile Reincarnated JSON to C# weekly, push to Workshop) is technically constructible with no documented frequency cap. But the 1.4.5 compat lag (9-month historical) means any Reincarnated season mod is tied to 1.4.4-stable indefinitely — which is fine for procedural content output but means Reincarnated cannot ride Terraria's most-current-version momentum.

11.1 — Matt's question, empirically resolved

Q (verbatim): "Will a complete overhaul of classes/characters for Grim Dawn with novel move sets, character and seasonal cohesion, with new loot (exactly matching the needs of the character/class progression), new skill trees, new VFX/SFX be enough? If there is not also new maps, new questlines, new NPCs, is it still worth paying for? Is the class system worth anything without the story/map changes?"

A: YES — the community accepts class-only as complete. The constraint is host-platform culture, not perceived completeness. Direct empirical proof from four top-tier mods that ship class-only-with-loot-and-skills without new world content:

  • Path B = years of credibility-building that earns the engine-sale conversation.

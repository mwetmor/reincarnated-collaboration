R2 Spatial Sub-Gauntlet — Visual Schematics

Faithful-to-scale diagrams of all three scenarios. Source: reincarnated-engine/src/reincarnated/simulation/spatial_gauntlet/arena.py. Authored 2026-05-19 by gandalf at Matt's request.

What you're looking at: the engine's internal 2D combat-test scenarios that R2 ran 51 classes through (3 scenarios × 30 fights each = ~4,500 fights logged). These are NOT player-facing surfaces — they're internal simulation arenas the balance loop uses to measure per-skill geometry-type variance, leash mechanics, range mechanics, and boss-with-adds composition. The dimensions and spawn coordinates below are exact, pulled from the production code.
Legend (applies to all three diagrams):
  • ● blue = player (0.5m entity radius)
  • ● red = swarm mob (0.5m radius)
  • ● darker red = elite add (0.5m radius)
  • ● large dark red = boss (1.5m radius — hard collision body)
  • Dashed circle = 35m leash override (swarm only in Scenarios 1 + 2; boss uses monster JSON default)
  • Yellow shaded zone = chokepoint x-clamp region (Scenario 2 only)
  • Grid lines every 5m for scale; bottom-left origin (0, 0)

What this scenario measures

Reference baseline for geometry-type WR divergence. The 50×50 open layout removes geometry constraints so AOE shape differences emerge cleanly: cone/circle vs point vs line skills produce different per-mob coverage. The 8-mob spread at y=8-20 produces a target cluster that's reachable for cone (~5m radius) but partial-coverage for point skills.

Engagement-geometry fix applied (2026-05-19)

The 18m monster JSON default leash caused mobs to oscillate at their leash boundary before reaching the player (mobs at y=8 leashed at y=26 < player y=40). The 35m override lets mobs pursue across the arena: M6 at y=8 + leash=35 = leash boundary y=43 > player y=40 ✓. Genre semantic preserved: "approach across open space" (D2 Bloody Foothills / PoE Twilight Strand).

R2 H1 finding for this scenario

Mean WR by geometry type: circle = 1.000 (n=3); cone = 1.000 (n=4); point = 0.721 (n=43). Point-geometry classes lose 28pp WR vs cone/circle at open arena. This is the geometry-type variance signal the 1D PackProxy gauntlet couldn't surface.

What this scenario measures

Cone / line AOE exploitation + positional advantage. The 5m bottleneck (the yellow zone) clamps mob x-position to [2.5, 7.5] when y ∈ [23, 27], queueing mobs into a narrow lane as they pursue the player. This concentrates targets for cone/line skills (perpendicular AOE swept across the corridor) but does not help point skills (single-target damage doesn't benefit from spatial concentration).

Chokepoint mechanic

The x-clamp is the engine's simplification of pathfinding — entities pass through the y∈[23,27] zone with their x forced into [2.5, 7.5]. Outside that y-range, entities move freely within the 10m corridor width. This models the bottleneck without requiring full pathfinding (per arena.py docstring + math note § 3.3).

R2 H3 finding for this scenario

Chokepoint gap: +0.130 mean (threshold 0.05; 2.6× over). Cone/circle classes achieve mean WR 1.000 in chokepoint (same as open). Point classes drop from 0.721 (open) to 0.581 (choke) — a 14pp WR decrease. The geometric signal "point can't exploit chokepoints" is empirically confirmed.

What this scenario measures

Spatial composition that the 1D gauntlet literally cannot represent. The 1D PackProxy collapses N entities into a single proxy at a single distance scalar — but here, the boss is straight ahead (17m) while the adds flank at ~95° offsets (~12m diagonal). This forces decisions visible in the data: aggro adds first (risk losing time on boss-DPS) or fight boss while adds chip (risk being interrupted by add damage)?

The 95° flanking angles (jack-ryan WARN-2 fix)

Initial scenario design had adds at exactly 90° lateral offsets; jack-ryan's WARN-2 noted that strict 90° produces measurement artifacts at the boundary of "side" vs "behind" — strictly-flanking-but-not-behind requires >90° offset. The 95° design ensures the adds are unambiguously side-flanking the player from boss-facing orientation.

R2 boss-with-adds result for the shipped catalogue

All 51 classes scored 0.000 mean WR on this scenario. Same pattern as 1D boss WR. The shipped catalogue cannot kill the boss whether the engine measures with PackProxy or with full 2D spatial composition — the kit-quality pathology is invariant to measurement methodology.

Why these three scenarios?

The three scenarios are designed to surface three distinct signals the 1D PackProxy gauntlet cannot:

The engagement-geometry fix (2026-05-19)

The 18m → 35m leash override

Initial R2 implementation used monster JSON default leash = 18m. Empirical test (class_0020): only 11 mob-hits in 120s when expected ~686. Player HP 98.8% at timeout.

Root cause: mobs at y=8 with leash 18m have leash boundary at y=26 — but the player is at y=40. Mobs reached their leash boundary and oscillated south of y=26 instead of pursuing the player at y=40.

Fix: per-scenario leash override of 35m for swarm mobs in open_arena + chokepoint_corridor. Boss-with-adds keeps the JSON default (boss at y=8 + player at y=25 = 17m distance < 18m default, so no oscillation issue).

Verification: mob at y=8 + leash=35 = leash boundary at y=43 > player at y=40 ✓.

The "kills-only timeout" semantic change

Open arena + chokepoint both use kills-only timeout: if any mob is alive at 120s, the player LOSES regardless of HP. The previous semantic (HP>50% survival win) was retired because it produced "I camped the leash boundary and survived" wins that didn't measure kill efficiency — the actual question R2 wants to answer.

Boss-with-adds keeps the boss_killed win condition (boss HP → 0 wins regardless of add status). This is a forward-flagged boss-tier-specific calibration question for VS2b (§ 3.4 forward-flag in arena.py).

What R2 does NOT model

Some things the spatial sub-gauntlet explicitly does NOT include:

  • Full pathfinding. The choke zone is an x-clamp, not A* pathfinding. Entities don't navigate around obstacles dynamically.
  • Line-of-sight. Skills don't check LOS; if target is in range, skill fires.
  • Terrain variation. No elevation; no movement cost; no environmental damage.
  • Multi-pack composition. Each scenario has one mob group (or boss+adds). No "wave 2 spawns when wave 1 dies."
  • Visible rendering. R2 produces telemetry, not Pixi.js frames. The demo's 2D rendering is drax's seam (R4 + R5); R2 is the engine-side measurement layer.

Mapping to the player-facing demo

R2 is the engine's measurement layer; drax's demo (R4 + R5) is the player's rendering layer. They share the same R3 schema (per-skill range; aggro/leash/disengage AI behavior fields) so the engine's measurements match the demo's runtime behavior — that's R7's parity-test infrastructure. But the demo has its own visual register (Pixi.js sprites, R4 separation force, Drift-15 tilesets) that R2's schematic does not capture.

If you wanted to see R2's scenarios in the demo, drax would need to implement a "spatial gauntlet replay" mode that loads R2 scenario configs and renders them in Pixi.js. That doesn't exist as a feature — R2's purpose is empirical balance measurement, not playtest visualization. Could be authored as a future feature if useful.

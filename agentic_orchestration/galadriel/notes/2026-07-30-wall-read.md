## §0 — The answer in one paragraph

The bricks were never removed and were never unlit. **A second, untextured wall was built in
front of them.** `scripts/wr2_playback.gd::_dress_wall_faces()` — which entered the tree on
2026-07-29 in commit `4f69e93` (WR2-ENCGEO, the very first trace-playback commit) — builds four
`BoxMesh` slabs that exactly fill the 0.750 m band between the sim's arena line (±18.00 m) and the
kit wall's inner face (±18.75 m), at full wall height, spanning each side plus both corner bands.
They carry a plain `StandardMaterial3D` with **`albedo_color = Color(0.115, 0.118, 0.132)` and no
texture at all**. That is 10.8× less linear reflectance than the Synty brick standing behind it.
Because the slab's top sits at exactly `WALL_H = 3.005743` — the kit wall's own top — it occludes
the masonry **completely** from any camera inside the room. What Matt has been looking at since
2026-07-29 is the R-WR1-21 obstruction band, not the wall.

**Hypothesis verdict: H2 (material/mesh) — but not the H2 variant that was proposed.** It is not a
culled backface, not a missing texture on the kit wall, and not a different wall construction in
the 37.5 m room. It is an *added occluding slab with a near-black flat albedo*. **H1 (light
starvation) is refuted. H3 (exposure/tonemap crush) is refuted as a cause, and named as an
aggravator.**

## §1 — The mechanism, read off the source

`scripts/wr2_playback.gd` `_build_stage()` calls `_dress_wall_faces()` **unconditionally** — on
both the `--with-level` path and the bare-arena fallback. Its own header states the intent
plainly, and the intent is sound; the problem is entirely in the material:

> *"the band is FILLED, not marked. Four slabs, 0.750 m deep and one wall-course high, occupy it
> exactly: outer face flush to the kit wall, INNER FACE EXACTLY ON THE ARENA LINE. A player clamped
> to x = 0.5 m is then standing 0.5 m from a solid face at eye level, which is what the trace says
> is happening."*

The geometry it builds:

| element | size | seat | material |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FaceNorth` / `FaceSouth` | `(37.5, 3.005743, 0.750)` | z = ±18.375 (spans the 18.00→18.75 band) | `StandardMaterial3D`, **albedo (0.115, 0.118, 0.132), roughness 0.92, NO texture** |
| `FaceEast` / `FaceWest` | `(0.750, 3.005743, 37.5)` | x = ±18.375 | same |
| `Pilaster{E,W}{N,S}` ×4 | `(1.125, 3.186, 1.125)` | the four corners, proud 0.18 m | `StandardMaterial3D`, **albedo (0.145, 0.148, 0.163), NO texture** |
| kerb ×4 | `(36.0, 0.02, 0.10)` | on the arena line at floor | unshaded emissive (0.62, 0.45, 0.22) |

Three facts make the occlusion total rather than partial:

1. **Height parity.** Slab height = `WALL_H` = `3.005743` = the kit wall's exact height
   (`kit_replica_level.gd::WALL_H`, a measured number off the dark-fantasy wall FBX). Nothing of
   the wall face clears the slab.
2. **Corner closure.** `span = ARENA_M + band*2` = 37.5 m — the header says it explicitly: *"so
   the corners close solid (no 0.75 m notch where two faces meet)"*. There is no seam to see
   through.
3. **Depth.** 0.750 m of solid box between the eye and the masonry, on all four sides.

The masonry itself is untouched. `kit_replica_level.gd::_make_wall()` still calls
`_apply_single_tex(inst, _p(_kit["tex_wall"]))`, and the N/E/W faces still carry
`walltop_occlude.gdshader`, whose own header records that it is deliberately **LIT** —
`render_mode blend_mix, depth_prepass_alpha, cull_back;` with no `unshaded`, `occlude = 0.0` by
default (fully opaque), `stone_tint = (1,1,1)`. Nothing in the wall path changed. It is simply
behind a box now.

### §1.1 The arithmetic of the darkness

| surface | albedo sRGB | albedo LINEAR |
|---|---|---|
| `Brick_Small_01.png` (the kit's `tex_wall`, measured mean over the whole texture) | (0.4076, 0.4137, 0.4218) | (0.14337, 0.14788, 0.15431) |
| WR2 arena-shell slab | (0.115, 0.118, 0.132) | (0.01251, 0.01305, 0.01573) |
| WR2 corner pilaster | (0.145, 0.148, 0.163) | (0.01848, 0.01915, 0.02272) |

**Linear reflectance ratio slab / brick = 0.0927.** The surface Matt is looking at returns **9.3%**
of the light the brick would have returned — under the same lights, at the same place, at the same
angle. A 10.8× drop is not "a bit dark"; it is a different class of surface.

*(Sanity note on the brick number: (0.4076, 0.4137, 0.4218) is not a value I chose — it is the
measured brick mean already recorded in `kit_replica_level.gd` at line ~1132, and my independent
re-measure of the PNG returns it to four decimals. The instrument agrees with the record.)*

## §2 — The discriminating pixel evidence, WITHIN ONE FRAME

Cross-frame luma is not comparable here — the frames were shot at different cameras, distances and
subject distances. So the load-bearing reading is taken **inside a single frame**:
`tmp/camlock/plates/_raw_plate1080.png`, the CAM-LOCK player_lock plate — the exact camera at which
Matt noticed this.

Two readings, one exposure, one lighting rig, one frame:

| region in that frame | mean luma | p95 | max | **\|grad\|** | true (0,0,0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the "black wall" (arena-shell slab) | 12.88 | 14.75 | 14.96 | **0.04** | **0.0 %** |
| floor, kit tile | 34.43 | 40.72 | 41.64 | 1.07 | 0.0 % |
| **kit masonry standing ABOVE the slab line** (auto-located brightest non-bloom 24×14 patch) | 43.16 | 61.33 | 61.33 | **7.12** | 0.0 % |

`|grad|` = mean absolute 1-px luma gradient — a texture-energy reading, deliberately chosen because
it is far less exposure-dependent than mean luma. **A brick wall that is merely underlit still
carries its coursing.** This one carries none: `0.04` against the masonry's `7.12` in the same
frame — **183× flatter**, and 3.35× darker.

That single row pair settles all three hypotheses at once, with no cross-frame assumption anywhere:

- **The geometry is NOT absent, and NOT culled.** The slab renders: `true-black = 0.0 %`, mean
  12.88, and a smooth warm gradient where the sconces fall on it.
- **The masonry is NOT unlit.** The kit wall's own top course — the one piece of masonry the slab
  does not cover, because the wall-top cap seats at `WALL_H + CAP_LIFT + CAP_THIN*0.5`, above the
  slab's top — reads at mean 43.16 with full brick coursing, **in the same frame**. Whatever is
  wrong, it is not that light stopped reaching the walls.
- **It is NOT crushed below the visible band.** No pixel in the slab region is (0,0,0); the band
  sits at 12.9/255 with live shading. It READS as black on a display, but it is rendering.

### §2.1 H1 (light starvation) is refuted twice more, independently

1. **By geometry.** `_sconce_positions()` seats the sconces at `_sconce_off = (37.5/2) − 0.85 =
   17.90 m` — i.e. **0.10 m in front of the slab face** (which begins at 18.00 m), at energy
   `4.6 × k^1.55 = 15.3` and range `8.5 × k = 18.2 m`, twelve of them, spaced 11.93 m. The
   suspect surface is the **closest surface in the room to the room's own light sources**. Light
   starvation is not available as an explanation for it.
2. **By the false-sun-deletion frame itself.** `tmp/pclight/frames/final_B.png` — the very frame
   that verified the CEILING-1 repair, with the false directional sun already deleted — shows the
   interior wall faces as **lit brick masonry** on N/E/W. The sun deletion did not darken the
   walls; the frames that certified the deletion show bricks.

### §2.2 H3 is named as an aggravator, not a cause

At 12.9/255 with the filmic tonemap and glow enabled, the band sits at the very bottom of the
visible range on any display not calibrated in a dark room. That is why it took the zoomed
player_lock camera for Matt to see it at all: at the judge camera the band is thin and
foreshortened; at player_lock it fills a large part of frame. **The exposure did not cause the
black — it caused the black to be invisible AS a defect for a month.** Worth recording, because it
is the same class as the "metric green / eye red" defect drax named in WR1-ROOMS §1.1.

## §4 — A second black-wall mechanism, unrelated to Matt's question, found on the way

Reported because the survey found it, and because it will matter to BEAUTY-CORNER — **not** as an
answer to today's question, which §1–§3 close.

`kit_replica_level.gd::_build_walls_and_caps()` gives the **South** wall (`ei == 1`), inner **and**
outer leaf, `walltop_void_radial.gdshader` via `_south_mat_common()`, with `south_blackout = true`.
That shader is `render_mode unshaded` and multiplies albedo to pure black across a south azimuth
sector. This is deliberate and Matt-directed in its own lineage (*"dissolve the south boundary into
void so the viewer can't tell if it's a wall or open space"*) — it is why the near/south edge is
black in `tmp/pclight/frames/final_B.png`, and that black is correct there.

**The finding:** the shader measures azimuth from `ring_center`, and `_south_mat_common()` sets
`ring_center = Vector3(0, 0, 0)` while the shader resolves position through `MODEL_MATRIX` — i.e.
**world space**. In the single-room 17.5 m stage the room centre *is* the world origin and the maths
is exact. In `wr1_level.gd` the rooms are translated along +X to `x = 0 / 47.5 / 100.0 / 157.5`, and
the azimuth is then measured from the world origin instead of from the room:

| room | centre x | south-wall azimuth from world origin | `v_south` (half-width 0.80, feather 0.60) | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 trash | 0.0 | 0.00 – 0.785 rad | 1.0 | south wall **fully black** (as designed) |
| 3 boss (the room WR3 renders) | 157.5 | ≈ 1.45 rad | ≈ 0.0 | **no blackout** — but the wall is still `unshaded`, so it renders flat texture × tint (0.82, 0.74, 0.62) with no light response at all |

So in the boss room the south wall is neither correctly blacked nor correctly lit — it is an
unshaded flat plate. Nobody has seen it because the WR2 slab covers it. It is an inherited
single-room assumption that did not survive the multi-room translation, and it will surface the
moment the slab is addressed. Flagged, not fixed; not mine to fix.

## §7 — RECOMMENDATION *(the only non-findings section; clearly separated per survey-mode)*

**Fix class: MATERIAL, not lighting.** One line — give the arena-shell slabs the kit's own wall
material instead of a flat dark albedo.

The R-WR1-21 requirement is *"the visible obstruction face sits at the arena-datum line"*. It is a
requirement about **where the face is**, not about **what the face is made of**. It is satisfied
identically by a slab wearing `Brick_Small_01` through the kit's own occlude shader. The slab is a
0.75 m thickening of the wall — so it should look like the wall thickening, which is what a crypt
would actually do.

Concretely, for whoever takes it (drax, in BEAUTY-CORNER):

1. **Texture the four faces.** `_dress_wall_faces()` already runs inside a scene that has the kit's
   handles; the cleanest route is `kit_replica_level`'s `_build_occlude_mat(occlude_shader,
   tex_wall)` — same shader, same tint, same roughness as the wall standing behind it, so the band
   composites as continuous masonry rather than as a second material. A `StandardMaterial3D` with
   the brick texture + `uv1_scale` matched to the wall's brick period would also do; the shader
   route inherits the camera-occlusion behaviour for free.
2. **Pilasters likewise**, and they will then do the job their own comment claims (*"a corner has
   to READ as a corner from the play camera"*) — at 0.145 flat albedo they currently read as a
   slightly-less-black rectangle.
3. **Acceptance metric, already instrumented:** wall-band `|grad|` at the CAM-LOCK camera should
   land in the masonry range (≈ 6–7), not the current 0.04. That is a one-number gate and it is
   reproducible from `wall-read-probe.py`.
4. **Do NOT reach for lighting.** Adding light to a 0.0125-linear-albedo surface to make brick
   appear cannot work — there is no brick in it to reveal. If BEAUTY-CORNER's player-light and
   torch shadows land on the current slab, they will land on a featureless plane and the sheen/
   shadow work will read as mush. **This fix should precede the BEAUTY-CORNER lighting elements,
   not follow them** — the wall is the largest single surface those three elements will play
   against.
5. **Sequenced behind it, not with it:** the §4 south-wall `ring_center` world/local defect. It
   becomes visible the moment the slab is textured, and it should be fixed by passing the room's
   own centre rather than by widening the sector.

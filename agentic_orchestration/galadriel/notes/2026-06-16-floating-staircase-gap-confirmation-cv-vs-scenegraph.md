Three structural reasons it is blind to this defect:

1. **It measures edge DENSITY/PERIODICITY, not 3-D support.** A staircase that floats produces the *same* edge pixels whether its base touches a deck or hangs in air — its silhouette is identical. The float is a relationship between the stair's base and the surface below it; "is there geometry under this base" is not a quantity in the edge histogram.
2. **It has no ground-contact term at all.** The four numbers are {upper density, lower density, ratio, column-CV, ACF-peaks, ACF-max} (output object, code lines 124-129) — every one a scale-normalized fraction/ratio/count, none a support measure. Even a *human* can't read ground-contact here (this is gandalf's §1 second blindness, independently confirmed at the pixel level: the float lives on the screen-axis the probe has the least signal on).

The four iter4 falsifier classes (F1 orphan uprights / F2 wall-top arches / F3 free verticals / F4 arch-foot-on-empty-floor) are a **closed enumeration of *vertical* orphans** (rescore §3). A floating *horizontal/diagonal* element (a stair flight, a gallery deck, a cantilevered run) is a *different topological failure* — base-detached rather than top-detached — and was simply not in the probe's or the close-read's defined set. **This is a healthy out-of-distribution gap-find, not a scoring error:** the rescore's own §0 caveat already flagged the probe as degraded by the top-down framing and explicitly leaned on the manual close-read as the gate — and the manual read's checklist was the four vertical-orphan classes, which this defect is orthogonal to.

## 2. PART 2 — is "floating element" CV-detectable in principle, and CV vs scene-graph?

### 2.1 In principle: yes — but only with the angle, and only as a *weaker* instrument than scene-graph

A "floating element / unsupported horizontal" IS detectable by a CV falsifier-probe **in principle**, but it is conditional on the frame and it is the harder, less reliable home for this class:

- **Frame angle required:** the current top-down frame is unusable (foreshortening kills the support-gap, §1.3). The defect becomes CV-legible only on the **low-oblique audit frame** (gandalf's Part-2 camera, 20–40° below horizontal) — where the stair's base and the surface beneath it occupy *different vertical screen-space*, so a gap between them is a measurable band of background pixels.
- **Plausible instrument on that frame:** segment the structural-mesh silhouette (a distinct luma/material cluster against floor + background), find its lowest contour, and test whether floor/deck pixels sit directly beneath it within tolerance — or, cheaply, an **under-element background-visibility probe** (count background/void pixels in the vertical strip directly below the element's base; a grounded stair has ~0, a floater has a bright band). **The single strongest CV signal is motion:** gandalf's premium 360° orbit *video* makes a floater "swim" against parallax — an optical-flow / frame-difference probe on the orbit clip is the most robust CV form, because a floating element's background-disocclusion rate under camera motion differs sharply from a grounded one's.
- **Why it's still the weaker home (the honest CV limitations):** (a) it needs a *bespoke* camera re-validated against the known-positive every time the scene changes (gandalf's §4 acceptance loop — correct, but recurring cost); (b) silhouette segmentation against a cluttered, self-occluding crypt is genuinely hard — a stair half-behind a pier reads as "supported" to a 2-D contour test when it isn't, and vice-versa (false-negatives + false-positives both live here); (c) it is a *projection* of a 3-D question onto 2-D, so it is fundamentally lossy — it can only ever *approximate* "is there geometry under the base."

gandalf's §5 leans scene-graph for geometric support-failures because it is deterministic and camera-free. **I pressure-tested that against the real `arena_descent.tscn` — and the verdict is: gandalf's lean is RIGHT and the substrate is favorable, but on a *different and simpler* basis than the prior interrupted pass claimed.** The corrected facts (all read directly from the scene text, line-cited):

What the baked scene actually carries (read-only inspection, corrected):
- Every structural element is a `MeshInstance3D` node with an explicit world-resolvable `Transform3D` and a `groups=["nonpassable_dressing"]` tag. The basis is a 180° yaw; the **origin is `(36.5, 0.0, 6.1)`**. The `check_descent_parity.py` precedent **already walks exactly these node blocks and reads exactly these `Transform3D` lines** — the text-walk is proven on this file. **Node origins (pivots) ARE available to a text-walk; geometric AABBs are NOT** (see correction below).
- **The float is a node-transform Y mismatch, legible from origins alone (THIS is the load-bearing fact for drax).** The parent chain is untransformed: `Zone5_mini_boss` (line 26868, `Node3D`, no transform → world Y=0) → `GalleryStorey` (line 31945, `Node3D`, no transform → world Y=0). So node-origin world-Y = node-local-Y. And:
  - the **gallery-deck floor** `SM_Bld_Base_Floor_01` (sibling, line 31948): `transform = Transform3D(1,0,0,0,1,0,0,0,1, 33, 6, 6.6)` → **world Y = 6.0** (the deck surface the gallery furniture sits on; railings, statues, the *second* stair `@MeshInstance3D@4493` at Y=1.83 are all placed at gallery-relative heights).

**CORRECTION to the prior pass (§ 0-bis item 1), restated in place so drax is not misled:** the prior pass claimed `aabb` is stored per-MeshInstance (210 occurrences → per-element extent). **That is wrong.** The 210 `aabb` lines are `visibility_aabb` particle-culling boxes on `GPUParticles3D` nodes (e.g. line 7167 `visibility_aabb = AABB(-0.5,0,-0.5,1,1,1)` under `FX_Fire_Medium_01`) — **not** structural-mesh geometric extents. **CORRECTION to the prior pass's design-constraint (§ 0-bis item 2):** the prior pass warned a pivot-only check would FALSE-PASS this defect and demanded baked-world-AABB resolution. **The data inverts that:** the stair's origin Y=0 vs its sibling deck Y=6 means a **pivot/origin-Y check absolutely catches this exact zone5 stair** — it is the *simplest* check that works. ## 3. PART 3 — resolution bump vs the 960w normalization (CONFIRMED no perturbation)

`arch-grammar-band-probe.mjs` does NOT hard-pin to 960w — it reads native dims (line 84 `const W = info.width, H = info.height;`) and computes on them; the rescore's "960w inside-fit" is the standard pre-resize in the *hue/edge* instruments. Either path, the bump is safe, for instrument-design reasons confirmed in code:

- **All probe outputs are scale-normalized ratios/fractions, not pixel counts.** `upperBand_edgeDensity = upperSum/upperCnt` (line 105) is a *fraction* of band pixels over threshold; `storeyRatio` is a ratio of two fractions (line 107); `columnProfile_CV` is a coefficient of variation (mean-normalized, `stats()` line 56); the arcade-rhythm column profile is **downsampled to a fixed `BINS=120` regardless of source width** (lines 111-116, `Math.floor((x/W)*BINS)`), and ACF peaks are computed on those 120 bins. **None of the six outputs is in absolute-pixel units**, so feeding a 1440p (or 4K) source — or normalizing it to 960w first — lands the same scalars within sub-1% antialiasing jitter.
- **Confirming the normalization holds:** because the instruments downsample/normalize before measuring (960w inside-fit for hue/edge; the fixed 120-bin column profile for this probe), a larger *source* render only improves the downsample input quality, not the measurement basis. 1440p→960w and 4K→960w both yield a cleaner 960w grayscale than 1152→960w did; distributions are stable. The Sobel threshold (`thr=40`, line 91) is a luma-gradient magnitude, invariant to resolution after normalize.
- **Practical note for when the audit frames land:** the 4K audit frames are a *new input class* (low-oblique), not a re-render of the combat frame. The existing probe is calibrated on top-down framing and (per §1.3 + rescore §4) under-counts diagonal/foreshortened rhythm; I would NOT re-use the arch-grammar probe's *arcade-rhythm* axis on the oblique frames without a calibration-sweep (Discipline #17). The *resolution* is a non-issue; the *new angle* is what would need fresh methodology before any new CV axis runs on it — but per Part 2, the support-failure class shouldn't get a CV axis at all.

**Confirmation: the 1440p-combat / 4K-audit bump perturbs NO existing galadriel instrument. All probe outputs are scale-normalized ratios + fixed-bin profiles; the 960w inside-fit normalization holds. Safe to bump.**

## Summary

The ARPG genre beyond Diablo/PoE contains a diverse set of design experiments: Last Epoch (1.0, Feb 2024) is the genre's most significant recent new entrant, earning its place as a peer to Diablo and PoE through its skill-per-skill specialization trees, the trade/self-found faction choice, and deterministic crafting. Grim Dawn is the genre's best-regarded dual-class system and remains a reference point for class identity through mastery combination. Lost Ark is an outlier — an MMO-ARPG hybrid with Korean design sensibilities that struggled in Western markets due to cultural mismatch on monetization and progression wall design. The Torchlight series illustrates a downward arc: two beloved accessible games (T1/T2) followed by a failed live-service pivot (T3), then a mobile iteration (Infinite). Wolcen is the genre's canonical cautionary tale for shipping early — broken skills, balance failures, ended development. Chronicon is a competent indie that succeeds where Wolcen failed. Undecember is a Korean classless ARPG positioned between Diablo and PoE, notable for build flexibility.

## Findings

### 1. Last Epoch — Skill Specialization Tree, Item Factions, Release 1.0

**Released:** February 21, 2024 (1.0); $34.99 on Steam; Early Access from 2018

**Design premise:** A time-travel-based ARPG (players move through different eras of the game world's history). Combat is Diablo-style isometric hack-and-slash.

#### Skill Specialization System — The Core Innovation

Last Epoch separates skill leveling from character leveling:
- **5 base classes** (Acolyte, Mage, Primalist, Rogue, Sentinel), each with **3 Mastery classes** (15 total masteries)
- **120+ skills** across all classes
- Each active skill has its own **dedicated skill tree** — a miniature passive tree specific to that skill's behavior
- Skills level up via use when placed in Skill Specialization Slots; early game: 1 slot; level 50: 5 slots
- Each specialized skill tree has up to 20 points to allocate

**Why this is notable:**
This design separates "what skill you use" from "how that skill behaves." A Fireball in the Mage becomes a distinct ability depending on its specialization tree — it could be a skill that fires multiple projectiles, or one that detonates explosively, or one that leaves a burning pool. The player makes two distinct decisions: *which skills* to equip, then *how to build* each skill. This creates combinatorial build space without requiring a massive character-level passive tree.

Community reception: "The skill-rich action RPG" (PC Gamer) — the complexity is praised as accessible because each decision point is scoped to a single skill rather than the entire character.

#### Item Factions — Trade vs. Self-Found Resolution

Last Epoch's most praised design innovation: **two joinable factions** that formally acknowledge the ARPG community's trade-vs-self-found tension:

- **Merchant's Guild:** Players trade freely with each other via the Bazaar (full auction house functionality). Items found are trade-eligible.
- **Circle of Fortune:** No meaningful trade access; instead, item drop rates are enhanced, and the faction provides "prophecies" (conditions that, when met, guarantee specific rare drops). Solo self-found experience made viable by compensating drop quality.

Key design property: both factions are **integrated into the game's lore and NPC relationships**, not just mechanical flags. Players experience different questlines and faction-specific rewards.

**Why this is genre-notable:**
Every ARPG faces the trade dilemma. PoE's solution is friction-based (trade exists but is inconvenient). D2's solution was no official trade system (player-driven). D3's solution was the AH (failed) then smart loot (removed trade need). Last Epoch's faction system is the first design that explicitly offers *both options* within the same game, respecting both playstyle preferences without forcing compromise on either.

#### What Last Epoch Prioritizes vs. Diablo/PoE

| Dimension | Last Epoch | Diablo 4 | Path of Exile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build complexity | High (per-skill trees) | Moderate | Very high |
| Loot grind | Moderate (crafting can target stats) | High | Very high |
| Accessibility | Moderate | High | Low |
| Story/world | Moderate (time travel framing) | High (cinematics) | Low |
| Faction/social systems | Innovative (trade factions) | Minimal | Trade-site (external) |
| Price | $34.99 one-time | $69.99 + seasons | Free-to-play + cosmetics |

**Sources:** PCGamesN (https://www.pcgamesn.com/last-epoch/review), Maxroll LE (https://maxroll.gg/last-epoch/news/item-factions-preview), Worthplaying (https://worthplaying.com/article/2024/1/11/news/140405-last-epoch-details-its-unique-trade-item-factions-system-trailer/), Aggronaut (https://aggronaut.com/2024/02/19/last-epoch-1-0-primer/)

### 2. Grim Dawn — Dual-Class System, Faction System

**Released:** 2016; developer: Crate Entertainment; post-apocalyptic fantasy setting

**Design premise:** Victorian-era monster apocalypse — a world invaded by two supernatural forces that use humans as resources. The tone is deliberately dark and grim; less high fantasy than most ARPGs.

#### Dual-Class (Mastery) System

Grim Dawn allows players to combine any two of its **9 masteries** (Soldier, Demolitionist, Occultist, Nightblade, Arcanist, Shaman, Inquisitor, Necromancer, Oathkeeper) to create a character. Each mastery has its own skill tree.

**Critical design properties:**
- Two masteries chosen sequentially (first at level 2, second at level 10)
- The combination creates a new named class (e.g., Soldier + Arcanist = "Spellbreaker"; Occultist + Shaman = "Conjurer") — 36 named class combinations
- Each mastery tree has skills that synergize with another mastery's skills — not just additive but specifically designed for cross-tree interaction
- The Devotion constellation system adds a third layer: celestial power constellations unlocked through shrines, each granting passive bonuses or proc abilities that can be assigned to skills

**Community view:** Widely cited as the best build diversity in any ARPG. The combinatorial space of mastery + devotion constellations is nearly unlimited. Grim Dawn is often used as the reference when discussing what "real" build diversity looks like.

**Design trade-off:** Complexity is high enough that new players feel lost. The game does not hold your hand in explaining how mastery synergies work — discovery is part of the experience, but can be alienating.

#### Faction System

Eight factions with reputation tracks (from Nemesis to Revered). Players improve faction standing by completing quests, killing specific enemies, and using faction-specific items.

**Design tension — incompatible factions:**
Two factions are mutually exclusive: **Kymon's Chosen** and **Death's Vigil** are rivals. Aiding one makes the other hostile. This forces a meaningful narrative and mechanical choice:
- Kymon's Chosen: zealous order opposing the undead
- Death's Vigil: necromantic order that uses the undead

Each grants different endgame items, questlines, and one-of-a-kind recipes. The choice is not cosmetic — specific builds benefit from one faction's items over the other's.

**Design lesson:** Incompatible faction choices create genuine player differentiation and replayability without requiring the player to experience entirely different content. The same dungeon run ends with different rewards depending on faction.

**Sources:** Crate Entertainment official site (https://www.grimdawn.com/guide/about/major-features/), Wyrmpres blog (https://wyrmpres.wordpress.com/2022/01/13/the-dual-class-system-in-grim-dawn-deserves-a-double-take/), Steam community discussions

### 3. Lost Ark — MMO-ARPG Hybrid; Western vs. Korean Design

**Released:** Korea 2019; Western (Amazon Games) February 2022

**Design premise:** A Korean MMO-ARPG hybrid where the ARPG combat layer (isometric, hack-and-slash, class-specific skill combos) sits inside an MMO-scale world with endgame raiding, guilds, PvP, and gear tiers typical of Korean MMOs.

#### What Lost Ark Prioritizes

- **ARPG-quality combat within an MMO social structure** — the combat feels like Diablo; the endgame feels like World of Warcraft
- **Class fantasy through class-locked gender and playstyle** — classes are visually and mechanically distinct; gender-locking was controversial in the West
- **Raid-centric endgame** — max-level content is group raids (4–8 players), not solo dungeons. This is the sharpest divergence from Diablo/PoE expectations

#### Western vs. Korean Version — Key Differences

| Dimension | Korean Version | Western Version |
|---|---|---|
| Classes at launch | 20+ | 15 |
| Content updates | Immediate | 3–6 month lag |
| Character design | More revealing cosmetics | Censored/adjusted costumes |
| PvP system | Full ranking system with multiple modes | Simplified, no ranking system at launch |
| Pet system | Rent utility features (p2w) | Pets auto-loot (utility granted for free) |
| Pay-to-win signals | More prominent | Reduced for Western launch |

The Western publisher (Amazon Games) made deliberate localization choices: reducing pay-to-win signals, censoring costume designs per Western platform norms, simplifying PvP. These were not fully successful — community criticism of progression walls (Honing system — item upgrade with failure probability) remained intense.

**Honing System (progression design):**
To enter raid content, players must upgrade gear to specific tiers via the Honing system: spend materials → attempt upgrade → succeed or fail (failure consumes materials, provides a small probability boost for next attempt). This creates a mandatory repetition loop that the Western community found frustrating as a gate before new content. It is a standard Korean MMORPG mechanic that assumes players engage with daily/weekly content routinely; it conflicts with Western ARPG players who expect to play at their own pace.

**Community reading of Lost Ark:**
Lost Ark proved that Western audiences will engage with Korean ARPG aesthetics (the combat was widely praised). But the MMO-progression design layer — raid gates, gear tier walls, calendar-driven content — conflicts with the Western ARPG preference for self-paced loot-hunt progression. The game is best understood as a **Korean MMO with ARPG-quality combat**, not an ARPG with MMO features.

**Sources:** SVG (https://www.svg.com/780301/lost-ark-the-biggest-differences-between-the-us-and-korean-versions/), MMORPG.com review, Dualshockers, Wikipedia (Lost Ark)

### 4. Torchlight Series — Design Generations

**Developer lineage:** Runic Games (T1, T2) → Echtra Games (T3, failed) → XD (Torchlight: Infinite, mobile)

#### Torchlight 1 (2009) and Torchlight 2 (2012) — The Beloved Pair

- Direct spiritual successors to Diablo/Diablo 2 (several Runic developers were ex-Blizzard North, the D2 team)
- Torchlight 1: single-player only; colorful aesthetic; simplified systems; notable for: fishing minigame, pet system (pet carries items to town to sell, frees player from town trips), randomized dungeon structure
- Torchlight 2: added 4-player co-op, expanded world to surface zones (not just dungeons), four classes with skill trees, New Game+, workshop/mod support
- Metacritic: T1 = 83/100 (PC), T2 = 88/100 (PC)
- Developer note: Runic praised for responsiveness — patching an eye-strain issue hours after a forum post

**Why they worked:**
Accessible versions of D2's design with no friction — no online requirement, no AH, no progression walls. The pet system addressed the inventory-burden anti-pattern. The colorful aesthetic made the genre approachable for players who found D1/D2 too dark.

#### Torchlight III (2020) — The Failed Pivot

Originally planned as "Torchlight Frontiers" — a free-to-play online game targeting global monetization. Negative reception to the F2P MMO-lite direction caused a pivot back to single-player, full-price. The result:
- Mixed-to-negative community reception (review bomb on Steam)
- Criticized as "mobile game experience" — UI buttons and level design felt built for quick mobile sessions
- Absence of features beloved from T1/T2 (fishing, traditional pet mechanics, Spell scrolls)
- Balance issues: broken skills, difficulty balance problems
- The F2P-to-premium pivot during development left visible seams in the design

**Design lesson (articulated by Max Schaefer, Runic co-founder):** The team had to educate new employees on "what Torchlight is about" — the identity was not fully transmitted across studios. Development continuity matters for design coherence.

#### Torchlight: Infinite (Mobile, 2022–ongoing)

- Developed by XD Inc. (Chinese publisher)
- Mobile-first live-service ARPG with seasonal updates (SS11 in January 2026)
- Positions itself in the "cozy ARPG" space for mobile
- Has a dedicated player base; not considered a peer to PoE/D4/LE in design depth

**Sources:** Gamedeveloper.com (Torchlight history: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-look-back-at-the-storied-history-of-action-rpg-i-torchlight-i-), Wikipedia (Torchlight, Runic Games, Torchlight III), Toolify.ai, Inven Global (Torchlight Infinite)

### 5. Wolcen: Lords of Mayhem — Cautionary Tale

**Released:** February 2020 (out of Early Access); developer: Wolcen Studio

**Design premise:** Ambitious ARPG with fluid class system (no hard class lock — a passive wheel lets players rotate between three class archetypes mid-character), high-production-value action, and a dark fantasy tone.

**What went wrong:**
- Multiple skills that "don't work as intended, don't work at all, are ludicrously overpowered or broken in some other way" (GameSpot review) — shipped with broken implementations
- Difficulty balance issues — content either trivial or suddenly wall-like
- "Despite being out of Early Access, Wolcen still feels very much in-development"
- OpenCritic: ranked 15th percentile among scored games
- Studio eventually announced end of development and shutdown of multiplayer servers

**Design lesson:**
Wolcen demonstrated that ambitious design (the fluid class wheel was a genuinely interesting idea) cannot compensate for broken implementation. The ARPG community is particularly unforgiving of skill malfunction — the core fantasy of the genre is skill expression through combat; if skills don't work as described, the whole premise collapses.

Wolcen is now referenced in ARPG discussions as the cautionary case for "launch before systems are solid."

**Sources:** PCGamer (https://www.pcgamer.com/wolcen-lords-of-mayhem-review/), GameSpot (https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/wolcen-lords-of-mayhem-review/1900-6417418/), OpenCritic, PCGamer (end of development)

### 6. Chronicon — Competent Indie

**Developer:** Subworld (indie, one-person for much of development)
**Release:** Full release 2020 (Early Access from 2015)

**Design premise:** Pixel-art ARPG in the D2 mold. Four classes with procedurally generated dungeons.

**What it does well:**
- High density of skill combinations and gear possibilities — genuine build space for an indie
- In-game loot filter (basic but functional — rare among indie ARPGs)
- Steam community describes as surprisingly deep for its scope
- "A very capable and expansive ARPG that hearkens back to Diablo II" (SaveOrQuit review)
- YouTube essays with titles like "Why Is It a Perfect ARPG?" suggest a devoted niche community

**Known issues:**
- Procedurally generated levels sometimes too large — "two to three hours between waypoints" — impractical for limited play sessions
- Some design ideas not implemented as well as intended

**Design position in the genre:**
Chronicon occupies the "competent indie" space — not innovative enough to shift genre discourse but solid enough to satisfy D2-nostalgic players who want the formula without the live-service overhead. It is cited as evidence that indie studios can succeed in the ARPG space by executing the fundamentals rather than competing on scale.

**Sources:** SaveOrQuit (https://saveorquit.com/2020/09/20/review-chronicon/), Steam community, YouTube

### 7. Undecember — Korean Classless ARPG

**Developer:** Needs Games
**Publisher:** LINE Games
**Released:** South Korea January 2022; global launch later 2022

**Design premise:** Diablo-style isometric ARPG with a **classless character progression system** — no character class selected at game start; instead, the player's build emerges entirely from their skill gem choices (similar to PoE's gem system but without the class start position constraint).

**What distinguishes Undecember:**
- Classless: "you can cast magical spells while wielding two one-handed swords" — no class gates on equipment or skill availability
- Combat tempo closer to D3 (faster, more fluid) than D2
- Gear enhancement and rune property modification system described as deeper than comparable games
- Visually high-fidelity — "mind-blowing visuals" noted on announcement

**Community position:**
Described as "blending Diablo's polished gameplay, Path of Exile's flexible systems, and Wolcen's visuals." Positioned as a PoE competitor for players who find PoE's complexity barrier too high but want more build freedom than D4 offers.

**Limitations noted:**
- Korean ARPG monetization concerns; less scrutinized than Immortal but same platform/publisher context
- Western community reception mixed — the game's profile in Western ARPG community is lower than Last Epoch, PoE, or D4

**Sources:** Fextralife (https://fextralife.com/undecember-gameplay-overview-new-hack-slash-arpg/), Pocket Gamer, Niche Gamer, PCGamesN (https://www.pcgamesn.com/diablo-4/undecember)

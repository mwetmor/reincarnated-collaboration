### Universal finding: all 10 chierit characters share identical canvas dimensions

All 10 archives ship a pre-assembled full-character spritesheet at **288×128 px per frame**. This is confirmed by drax's pipeline inspection ("ALL 10 archives ship a pre-assembled full-character spritesheet at 288x128 pixels") and independently by `characterSprites.ts:31` empirical confirmation noted in the dispatch.

The 288×128 is the **frame canvas** — not the character art bounding box. The character art occupies approximately 80–96 px of the 128 px canvas height (drax notes: "at 288px wide the character is likely ~80-96px tall within the canvas, with surrounding transparent space"). The remaining vertical space is transparent padding.

**Anchor convention:** not explicitly documented in metadata.json. From pipeline design notes, chierit sprites face right by default; the pixi.js integration flips `scale.x` for facing direction. No explicit anchor offset is stored — the renderer uses default Pixi.js top-left anchor (0, 0) on the frame rectangle. Visual tuning of vertical offset is flagged as a post-VS2a TODO.

**Source pack:** `/Users/admin/Games/reincarnated-demo/public/assets/CreativeKind/Fire_Lord_Creativkind/` (drax v0.20 ingest; deferred-pack tier)

The Fire_Lord_Creativkind pack contains 5 color variants (`Spritesheet/1/` through `Spritesheet/5/`) plus VFX sub-packs (`Energy_Ball/`, `Energy_Ball_Explosion/`, `Fire_Attack/`). The character sprite component is the `Spritesheet/<N>/` tree.

#### Figure-content bounding box (PIL measurement)

**CRITICAL FINDING: Fire_Lord has a very small idle figure relative to its 128px frame, with dramatic expansion during attack animations.**

| Animation | Max content H (px) | Max content W (px) | Max bbox | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| idle | 45 | 51 | (35, 59, 84, 104) @ frame 11 | Compact hovering pose; top padding 59 px |
| run | 41 | 42 | (37, 57, 79, 98) @ frame 11 | Similar compact pose |
| casting | 53 | 45 | (38, 44, 83, 97) @ frame 28 | Slightly taller with conjuring pose |
| atk1 | 38 | 63 | (37, 59, 100, 97) @ frame 7 | Wide arm-sweep; shorter than idle |
| atk2 | 89 | 80 | (27, 6, 107, 95) @ frame 11 | Large frame — flames extend upward |
| atk3 | 76 | 69 | (29, 33, 97, 109) @ frames 28-29 | Multi-phase attack; sustained peak |
| atk4 | 122 | 120 | (1, 2, 121, 124) @ frame 13 | Near-full-frame; flame wings fully extended |
| hurt | 38 | 40 | (40, 60, 80, 98) @ frame 0 | Compact recoil |
| death | 42 | 37 | (42, 60, 79, 102) @ frame 5 | Compact collapse |

**Anchor observation:** The figure consistently floats 24–35 px above the frame bottom in idle/run/hurt/death animations (bottom padding range: 24–35 px). In peak attack frames (atk4) the figure nearly fills the canvas (bottom padding 4 px). This is a floating/hovering character by design — not a ground-contact character. There is no consistent ground-contact line. **Anchor convention: the renderer should treat the FRAME BOTTOM as the anchor point; the floating appearance is the character's design intent.** However, a `y_anchor_offset` will likely be needed to prevent visual float in combat (same class of issue as Ground Monk's 6px float, but more severe: ~30 px at idle).

**Representative content height for scale calculation:**

- **Idle content H = 45 px** (max across idle animation; stable, conservative reference for persistent on-screen presence)
- **Peak attack content H = 122 px** (atk4 frame 13; flame-wings-fully-extended; extreme but brief)
- **Combat-stable upper bound = 76 px** (atk3 frames 28–29; multi-frame sustained peak; best estimate of mid-combat presence)

#### Palette composition (color variant identification)

Color variants are identified by per-pixel RGBA analysis on idle frame 11 (521 opaque pixels):

| Variant | Dominant color | Median (R,G,B) | Primary palette description |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | BLUE/PURPLE | (188, 192, 217) | Blue-tinted body with white highlights; blue flame aura |
| V2 | BLUE/PURPLE | (188, 192, 177) | Slightly warmer blue-grey; similar to V1 |
| V3 | BLUE/PURPLE (dark) | (34, 47, 52) | Dark blue-black; deep shadow variant |
| V4 | GREEN | (50, 43, 52) | Green-tinted body |
| V5 | RED/ORANGE/FIRE | (190, 132, 11) | Fire palette — red/orange body, warm flame aura |

**CRITICAL PALETTE FINDING: Color Variant 1 is NOT the fire palette. Variant 5 is the fire/orange palette.**

In V5 atk3 frame 28 (the 76px peak combat frame): 1403 opaque pixels, of which 1358 (96.8%) are red-channel dominant. Brightest colored pixels: (255, 237, 202) — warm white-orange flame highlights. The fire signal in V5 is overwhelmingly red/orange-dominant (R > G > B throughout).

**Thunder-palette-shift readiness assessment:**

The pack already ships with a blue-dominant variant (V1 and V3). V1 is blue/purple with white highlights — this is already close to a thunder/lightning visual register. V3 is the dark variant. The fire-orange signal (V5) is confined to Color Variant 5 only; Variants 1–4 carry no fire-orange signal.

**Implication:** For the god-of-lightning thunder-palette swap (Case D), drax should use **Color Variant 1 (V1)** as the base rather than applying a Pixi.js palette tint to V5. V1 is already blue/purple-dominant — the thunder register is achieved without any tinting operation. A secondary option is V3 (dark blue-black) for a more imposing variant. The `palette_shift_required_pre_render` flag in the per-slug lookup v2 table may not be needed — the vendor has already supplied a blue-palette variant.

**For fire-elemental swap (Case A):** Use Color Variant 5 (V5) — the fire/orange palette.

#### Animation completeness assessment

Fire_Lord has a full animation set: idle, run, casting (32 frames), 4 distinct attack animations (atk1–atk4), hurt, and death. This is the **richest animation set in the CreativeKind monster roster** (compare: god-of-lightning with 1 frame). The casting animation at 32 frames is particularly notable — Lich's equivalent was 29 frames and was flagged as a "B11 caster pairing" candidate. Fire_Lord's casting animation is equally suited to mage/caster boss presentations.

### Sea of Stars (Sabotage Studio, 2023) — MEASURED

**Internal rendering resolution: 640×360 (confirmed from two independent sources)**
Steam Community FAQ states: "the game's internal aspect ratio is 16:9 (640×360)." The Pixel Perfect setting adds letterboxing on non-16:9 monitors to preserve crisp pixels. The 3× upscale factor (640×3=1920, 360×3=1080) is exact at 1920×1080.

**Measurement methodology:** Five official Steam store screenshots downloaded from Steam CDN at confirmed 1920×1080 (JPEG, 540K–848K filesize). Screenshots confirmed as 1920×1080 via PIL image size check. Character heights measured by visual pixel-position identification + programmatic color scanning to find character boundaries. JPEG compression introduces ±5–10px tolerance on all display measurements.

**Screenshot sources (accessed 2026-05-16):**
- `ss_f756ff477590284c7192ffcef99237de056e4aeb.1920x1080.jpg` — battle scene, snowy cave; Valere visible at left
- `ss_889d4ddea7d0884d9d370dd280878570824e68fe.1920x1080.jpg` — boss fight, cave; Zale (left) and Valere (right) visible
- `ss_e7ac3a6e5ebe5f4561fc46d116c33678cf4ba811.1920x1080.jpg` — overworld, forest path; three characters on tree trunk

**Character height measurements (display pixels at 1920×1080, ±10px):**

| Screenshot | Character | Context | Display px | Internal frame px (÷3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sos_0 (battle) | Valere | Snowy cave, left battle position | ~75 px | ~25 px |
| sos_2 (boss fight) | Zale | Cave boss fight, left position | ~90 px | ~30 px |
| sos_2 (boss fight) | Valere | Cave boss fight, right position | ~85 px | ~28 px |
| sos_4 (overworld) | Valere | Forest path, foreground (perspective larger) | ~100 px | ~33 px |
| sos_4 (overworld) | Zale | Forest path, midground (perspective smaller) | ~70 px | ~23 px |

**Standard battle camera reference:** 75–90 display px (midpoint ~82 px) at 1920×1080. This is the most stable measurement — battle scenes have a fixed camera distance, making it the appropriate reference for scale factor comparison.

**Critical semantic note — internal vs displayed for SoS:**

The "640×360 → 3× → 1920×1080" upscale applies to the ENTIRE rendered frame. Sea of Stars renders characters as 2D billboard sprites in a 3D perspective scene. This means:

1. The 3× upscale is NOT a direct sprite-size multiplier for characters in 3D space. A character that is 30px tall in the 640×360 rendered frame appears as 90 display pixels at 1920×1080 — but this 30px is not the "sprite texture height" the artist drew.
2. The "display/3" figure (~25–30 px at battle camera) represents the character's footprint in the rendered 640×360 frame — not the original sprite art height.
3. The original sprite texture height is inaccessible from public sources (Spriters Resource 403; no modding documentation found).
4. As a cross-reference: Chrono Trigger (the explicit design inspiration for SoS) renders Crono at 36px in SNES's 256×224 resolution. At Sea of Stars' 640×360, equivalent screen proportion (16%) would yield ~57px internal. This is larger than the ~25–30px measured, consistent with SoS being a 3D world where characters fill less vertical screen space than in a flat 2D scene.

**Confidence:** Medium-high for display pixel measurements (grounded in confirmed 1920×1080 screenshots). Medium for internal frame conversion (math is exact, but JPEG tolerance is ±10px).

### Octopath Traveler (Square Enix / Acquire, 2018) — MEASURED

**Rendering resolution: native 1080p on PC (confirmed)**
Source: Nintendo Everything / Digital Foundry confirm 720p docked on Switch, 1024×576 portable. PC version renders at native display resolution (no fixed internal canvas).

**Sprite texture size: 512×512 px (confirmed from extracted game files)**
Source: OctoOverworldSprites GitHub repo (github.com/mastermind1919/OctoOverworldSprites) — `Technical Stuff/CustomSpriteDB.md` states: "In most cases, the size of the file is 512×512, so we divide by that." Character art occupies a portion of this 512×512 canvas. Overworld character icon extracted from the same repo: `Starseer Ophilia/Starseer Icon.png` = 192×192 px (confirmed via PIL measurement) — this is a UI icon, not the full character texture.

**Critical difference from SoS:** Octopath Traveler has NO fixed internal render resolution on PC. The sprite textures (512×512) are scaled by the UE4 renderer at whatever the output resolution is. At 1920×1080 PC, the displayed pixel height IS the "displayed px count" with no further internal-vs-external distinction. There is no "÷3" operation for OT.

### Semantic resolution: internal-vs-displayed for gandalf's 80-100px target

**The question:** Does gandalf's "80–100px HD-2D target" refer to internal sprite pixel count or displayed pixel count?

**Answer: Displayed pixel count at 1080p is the correct anchor, specifically at the overworld/exploration camera distance.**

Reasoning:

1. **OT overworld: 80–90 px displayed at 1080p.** This directly hits the "80–100px" range. OT is the canonical HD-2D reference.
2. **SoS battle: 75–90 px displayed at 1080p.** Close to the range; slightly below OT. SoS is the secondary reference.
3. **Internal frame pixels (SoS ÷3) = 25–30 px.** This is clearly NOT what gandalf means — it is far too small for "HD-2D quality" and does not match any recognizable design intent.
4. **Sprite texture pixels (OT: 512×512 canvas).** This is too large. The 512×512 texture houses the full-character atlas; individual frame height is a fraction of that.
5. **Confidence in the range:** The "80–100px" claim is empirically well-supported at overworld camera distance for OT, and approximately consistent (75–90px) for SoS. The range is a reasonable design target.

**Key caveat — battle vs overworld:** OT battle camera places characters at 120–130 px (above the 80–100px range). If gandalf's target is meant for the BATTLE context specifically, the range should be revised upward to 100–130 px. Recommend gandalf clarify which context (overworld vs battle) the target applies to.

**The 3× factor for SoS:** The 3× upscale from 640×360 is NOT the correct conversion for gandalf's purposes. The character's display height at 1080p (75–90 px in battle) is the meaningful figure. The "internal frame px" (÷3) corresponds to character footprint in the 640×360 canvas — not sprite art complexity. Sprite art complexity for SoS is determined by the original texture art size, which is not accessible from public screenshots.

### 4c — Identified mathematical impossibility cases

The following monsters cannot reach their tier target height without upscaling beyond the quality threshold (1.5×) if source pixel count is taken at face value:

1. **fire-elemental (elite, 68px frame height):** To reach 90px (tier floor) requires 1.32× upscale; to reach tier midpoint (102px) requires 1.50×. Nearest-neighbor filtering mitigates this, but the pixel art will appear large-pixel/chunky at 1.5× magnification. The 68px frame height is also unusually short (flat sideview aesthetic per drax), meaning the character art itself may be considerably shorter than 68px. If the character art is, say, 50px of the 68px frame, the required scale for 102px becomes 2.04× — a genuine quality-loss case.

2. **angel-guardian (boss, 192px frame height):** At 1.52× to reach 292px, this is at the threshold. However, for a boss at maximum screen presence, larger pixel size can be an aesthetic choice rather than a defect, especially with nearest-neighbor filtering. Not a hard impossibility.

3. **hellfire-rhino (mini_boss, 112px frame height):** At 1.38× for the tier midpoint, this is a moderate upscale. The 234px width is also non-standard. The character may appear blockier at this scale.

4. **sword-warrior (trash, 280px frame height):** At scale 0.23, this is a 4.3× downscale. At such extreme downscaling, the fine pixel detail in a 280×280 source will be heavily compressed. The character will render at approximately 65px tall but with ~15px width at that tier target, which may cause loss of recognizability. If the 280×280 canvas includes significant empty/transparent padding, the actual character art may be much smaller than 280×280 and the downscale less extreme than calculated.

5. **god-of-lightning (boss, 256×256, single frame):** The single-frame static sheet is an incomplete animation pack per drax's flag. The 256px height gives acceptable boss-tier sizing at 1.14× upscale, but the lack of animation is a deeper issue than scale. flagged for drax follow-up.

**Impact on 3× factor assertion from prior Section 3 derivation:**

- This is NOT the same as the sprite texture height (which is larger, unknown from screenshots).
- The prior "79px internal → 237px displayed" was based on the incorrect assumption that the 79px figure was the internal frame contribution; instead it was an estimate of "character as proportion of screen height" which did not account for the 3D perspective correctly.
